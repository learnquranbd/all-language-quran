Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the jilbāb verse, drafted
source-gated. 1,563 EN words; Bengali written from the idea.

Sources (all read, each attribution tied to its Arabic clause — see
LEDGER): Ibn Kathir (ar 14 / en 169), at-Tabari (15), al-Qurtubi (90),
as-Sa'di (91), al-Baghawi (94), al-Muyassar (16), Ma'arif (168).

What the sources gave:
- jilbāb defined as the large outer wrap drawn over the everyday
  clothes (ridāʾ over the khimār, the milḥafa/mulāʾa); al-Qurtubi's
  ṣaḥīḥ: the garment that covers the whole body.
- stated aim kept central: recognition + protection from harassment
  (as-Suddī's night-harassers of Madinah; Mujāhid; al-Hasan's context
  in al-Qurtubi; as-Sa'di "covering cuts off the predators").
- the household-first address and 66:6 (as-Sa'di); placement between
  33:57-58 and 33:60 (Ma'arif's two-harms reading).
- closes on Ghafūr Raḥīm as mercy for what passed before the command.

Disagreement kept as disagreement (no position, neither side called
sinful): the EXTENT of the jilbāb — whole face but one eye (Ibn ʿAbbās,
ʿUbayda) / most of the face, eyes may show (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatāda) / half
the face (al-Hasan); and the wider majority-vs-Ibn-Hazm debate on the
face (Ma'arif).

Hadith (verified via quranx, grading reported not upgraded):
- Sahih Muslim 890 (Umm ʿAṭiyya, "let her sister clothe her from her
  jilbāb") — ṣaḥīḥ by the collection; quoted whole.
- Sunan Abi Dawud 4101 (Umm Salamah, Anṣār women like crows) — recorded
  with no collector's grade; given as the community's response, same
  chain Ibn Kathīr cites from Ibn Abī Ḥātim.

Non-policing balance stated in both languages (§6): the verse's own aim
is that women not be harmed, so it licenses no shaming, coercion or
harassment over dress; the gaze is men's duty first (24:30); the
free/slave frame is 7th-century history and condemns no one today.

Dropped: al-Baghawi's ʿUmar/slavegirl whip report and al-Qurtubi's
thin-clothing anecdotes and genealogy (policing tone / off-topic).
