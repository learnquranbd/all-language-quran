Card and bilingual article for 26:26, Mūsā's third answer to Pharaoh's
court: "Your Lord and the Lord of your first forefathers."

Spine: tawḥīd ar-rubūbiyyah driven home. After Pharaoh's mocking "what
is the Lord of the worlds?" (26:23) and his sneer to the court (26:25),
Mūsā turns the court's own lineage-pride against it — even the fathers
they revere had a Lord over them, so Pharaoh, a man with buried
ancestors, cannot be god.

Sources (all confirmed against fetched text):
- Ibn Kathir: gloss (Creator of you and your forefathers before Pharaoh)
  + context that Pharaoh denied the Creator outright (28:28), and that
  when beaten in debate he fell back on force (bridge to 26:27).
- at-Tabari: the verse as the completion of Mūsā's invitation; the
  possessive "your" Lord.
- al-Qurtubi: Mūsā "increased the exposition" — the argument from
  origination (they had fathers; those perished; a Maker/Changer must
  be); Pharaoh replied in mockery.
- al-Muyassar: the sting — how worship one created like you, whose
  fathers perished like yours?
- as-Sa'di: the truth stands whether you marvel, grow arrogant, or yield.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: prophetic dialectic — Mūsā argues for truth, having
  admitted his own fault (26:20) and answered the favour-boast (26:22).
- al-Baghawi fetched but not named (text is verse only).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 1385 (fitrah, Abu Huraira), quoted whole,
graded ṣaḥīḥ. Marked explicitly as a thematic parallel, NOT attached to
26:26 by any tafsir — the forefathers who turn a soul from its innate Lord.

Wrongdoer-court scene: carries the "licenses nothing against any living
person or community" sentence, and guards heritage/ancestry from
contempt. Does not pre-empt 26:28 (2:258 parallel dropped) or the later
sorcerer-scene targets.

Dropped: As-Suddi's 20:49–50 parallel and the 2:258 Nimrod parallel
(belongs to 26:28); an uncited "every mushrik affirms the Creator" claim
reworded to the article's own voice.
