ʿĪsā in the cradle: made blessed wherever he is, enjoined prayer and zakāh for
life. Every clause of 19:31 is the servant's own testimony — He made me blessed
(jaʿalanī), He enjoined upon me (awṣānī) — Allah the subject, ʿĪsā the object.
Continues 19:30 "I am the servant of Allah"; kept central as the plain refutation
of divinity/sonship, stated gently and without polemic.

Barakah "wherever I am": al-Muyassar (great in good/benefit wherever found) and
as-Saʿdī (blessing reaches whoever sits with him). The commentators' spread on
its meaning, kept as converging readings: naffāʿ / teacher of good (Mujahid,
Sufyan, ʿAmr b. Qays, ath-Thawrī, via at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir), commanding good
and forbidding evil (Wuhayb b. al-Ward, "fuqahāʾ agreed"), calling to Allah's
tawḥīd (ʿAṭāʾ, al-Qurtubi with at-Tustarī). All outward-facing.

Awṣānī: Ma'arif (waṣiyyah = emphatic command; prayer + zakāh obligatory in every
prophet's law, Ādam→Muhammad ﷺ); at-Tabari (keeping prayer's limits). Zakāh
disagreement kept open: zakāt al-māl on niṣāb (Ma'arif; al-Baghawi "if I had
wealth") vs purification of the soul / surplus-charity independent of wealth
(at-Tabari's leaning; al-Baghawi "increasing in good"). Both share: a servant
bound to give.

Mā dumtu ḥayyan: lifelong, worldly-life duty (at-Tabari, Ma'arif); Ibn Kathir
ties it to 15:99, and Mālik b. Anas reads it as proof against deniers of qadar.

No hadith: no sound marfūʿ report is attached to 19:31 in the tafsirs; support is
all athar, said plainly in one sentence. No asbāb; placement in the cradle-speech
noted. Dropped: 17:23/31:14 and the Nawf al-Bikali narrative (19:32 / setup, not
this verse). Gate: ALL CLEAN, 1677 EN words, 8 sections.
