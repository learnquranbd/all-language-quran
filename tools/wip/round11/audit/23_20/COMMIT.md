Card and deep bilingual article for 23:20, the olive that issues from
Mount Sinai and yields oil and a relish for those who eat.

Spine: the olive as one item in the signs-of-provision passage
(23:17-22) — a single tree giving light-oil, food and relish — kept to
gratitude for layered, ordinary provision.

From the sources:
- Tree is the olive (all mufassirun). Singled out for its place in Sham
  and its many uses, and because it needs little tending (as-Sa'di,
  al-Qurtubi).
- Tur = mountain; Ibn Kathir's note that a height bears the name Tur when
  trees grow on it. Tur Sina'/Tur Sinin is the mountain of Musa (AS) in
  Sham, olives in the ranges around it (Ibn Kathir, Ibn Abbas via
  at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi; Ibn Zayd's ridge Egypt->Ayla).
- Link to Tur Sinin of 95:2 carried by al-Baghawi and Ibn Kathir; 95:1
  (fig and olive) noted only as that surah's opening.

Disagreement kept open, both readings, no position:
- "Sina'" = blessed (Mujahid) / beautiful (Qatadah, ad-Dahhak) / any
  fruit-bearing mountain (Muqatil) / a proper name like Uhud (jumhur).
  at-Tabari's tarjih (proper name; blessed as a place, not by the word)
  reported as his view.
- "sibgh" = dye (root sense) vs relish/idam dipped into (Qatadah, Ibn
  Zayd, as-Sa'di), with al-Qurtubi's bridge between the two.
- tanbutu/tunbitu qira'at and the ba'-za'ida debate noted; meaning
  converges.

Hadith: sound idam report featured whole from Sahih Muslim 2052 (Jabir),
marked general and not revealed about this verse. The fitting olive
narration (Tirmidhi 1851, Ibn Majah, from Umar) reported with
at-Tirmidhi's own iddirab grading and not leaned on as sound; grading
not upgraded.

No asbab established; placement used instead. Dropped: al-Qurtubi's
extended idam fiqh, Ibn Kathir's extra olive-oil chains, and the finer
qira'at sub-variants (see LEDGER).
