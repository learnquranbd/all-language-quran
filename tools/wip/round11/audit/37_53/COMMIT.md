Tadabbur 37:53 — That when we have died and become dust and bones, we will
indeed be recompensed?

The quoted mockery itself: the Paradise-companion's qarin, in his own words,
doubting not just resurrection but the reckoning that would follow it.

Sources: Ibn Kathir (ar/en abridged), at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di,
al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar and Ma'arif al-Qur'an, all already fetched in PREP.md.
Confirmed 37:16's Arabic directly from data/quran-json/37.json to ground the
article's central comparison: 37:16 ends la-mab'uthun (resurrected), 37:53
ends la-madinun (recompensed) — same opening, escalated doubt.

Kept as a genuine disagreement: lamadinun as muhasabun (Mujahid, as-Suddi) vs
mujaziyyun bi-a'malina (Ibn 'Abbas, Muhammad bin Ka'b al-Qurazi), both named,
with Ibn Kathir's own "both views are correct" also reported. Al-Baghawi's
istifham inkar (denial-interrogative) used for the grammar section; at-Tabari's
note on reciter consensus used to show the wording itself was never in dispute.
At-Tabari's report of Ibn 'Abbas's proverb, kama tadinu tudan ("as you repay,
so you are repaid"), given its own section. As-Sa'di's reading of the whole
37:51-53 exchange as the Paradise-companion's own testimony to his fellow
believers, quoted and translated.

The companion's identity: Ma'arif al-Qur'an's explicit caution that the
Qur'an gives no name and nothing can be said with certainty is kept intact;
the Tafsir Mazhari naming (Yahudah/Matrus) and the Ibn Jarir/Furat bin
Tha'labah two-partners narration (8000 dinars, house, marriage, two gardens)
are both presented as commentators' reports, not settled fact.

No hadith attaches to this verse in any source fetched; said so and moved on.
No asbab an-nuzul in any fetched source. Dropped: the Suyuti/tabi'in anecdote
in Ma'arif al-Qur'an (a differently-detailed version of the two-partners
story) rather than hybridizing it with the Ibn Kathir version; any claim tying
the د-ي-ن root to "yawm ad-din" (not stated in the fetched texts); any
elaboration of 37:54-57, kept as future ground per the brief.

Card: reflection and five questions built on the qarin framing and the choice
of companions, no scholar names or hadith refs. Themes: hereafter,
accountability.
