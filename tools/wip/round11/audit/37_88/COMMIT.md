Tadabbur 37:88 — And he cast a look at the stars

Sources: Ibn Kathir (ar), at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi, Ma'arif al-Qur'an
(en) and Ibn Kathir (en, abridged), all fetched live for this verse. as-Sa'di
fetched but not cited (redundant with Ibn Kathir); al-Baghawi fetched but
returned no usable commentary (24 chars) and was dropped.

Kept as a genuine range, no position chosen: five glosses on "inni saqim"
(plague-stricken per Sufyan/Ibn Abbas via al-'Awfi; a rising star borne as a
hardship for his religion per Qatada/Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab; a true statement
about every creature's future sickness, backed by Ma'arif's 39:30 grammar
parallel; sickness of heart at the idol-worship; and al-Hasan al-Basri's
no-stars version of lying down and looking at the sky). Also kept as a range:
why he looked at the stars at all — Qurtubi's astrology-knowledge background
(his people were herders/farmers who read the sky), Ibn Abbas's view that
star-knowledge was itself prophetic knowledge until nullified after Yusha' bin
Nun, and the Hasan al-Basri/Khalil/Mubarrid idiom reading that "looked at the
stars" is simply Arabic for "pondered."

Hadith: confirmed live via hadith.js against quranx.com. Sahih Muslim 2371
(Abu Hurayrah, full three-sayings wording) and Sahih al-Bukhari 3357 (same
narrator, shorter wording) both quoted on their own terms, no grading beyond
their inclusion in the Sahihs. A second report, via Ibn Abi Hatim back to Abu
Sa'id al-Khudri, is named with its weaker chain through 'Ali ibn Zayd ibn
Jud'an flagged explicitly, since it could not be confirmed independently.

Framing: the article never says in its own voice that Ibrahim (AS) lied. It
reports the hadith's own "lie" wording as the hadith's wording, then gives
Ibn Kathir's and Ma'arif's own reconciliation — ma'aridh al-kalam, ta'rid,
tauriyah — in their own terms, as the dominant scholarly position. States
plainly, in both languages, that the verse describes one people's
idol-worship in that moment and licenses no verdict on any living community.
37:89-94 (the idols broken, the people's return) left as future ground.

Dropped: Ma'arif's digression into the Shari'ah status of astrology generally
(Ibn Mas'ud's hadith on restraint, 'Umar's saying) as tangential to this
verse's event; several of Ma'arif's supporting tauriyah examples (Abu Bakr's
hijrah answer, Ka'b ibn Malik's route-changing, the Shama'il "old woman"
hadith), keeping only the core ruling that tauriyah is permitted on genuine
need.
