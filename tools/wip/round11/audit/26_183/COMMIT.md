Shuʿayb's twin prohibition: do not short-change people of their due, and
do not rampage through the earth as corrupters.

Sources (all confirmed on the verse before citing):
- at-Tabari: bakhs = reducing people's rights in measure and weight;
  taʿthaw = multiplying corruption in the land (the broad reading).
- Ibn Kathir: bakhs = diminishing people's wealth; taʿthaw = highway
  robbery, with the 7:86 parallel (do not sit on every road threatening).
- Kept the taʿthaw reading as a genuine disagreement: general corruption
  (at-Tabari) vs a specific crime, banditry (Ibn Kathir). No position taken.
- al-Muyassar and Ma'arif al-Qur'an carry ashyāʾahum off the scales:
  "or anything else" / "measurable or otherwise", the labourer who gives
  short time, and Ma'arif's ʿUmar(RA)/Muwatta ṭaffafta remark on a missed
  prayer. Named the surah al-Muṭaffifīn without a number (Ma'arif's printed
  number is garbled).
- al-Muyassar's list of fasād (shirk, killing, plunder, terrorising, sins).
- Ibn Kathir (en) for the 181–182 symmetry: give as you take, take as you give.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2581 (the muflis), quoted whole; graded by Muslim's
collection, not upgraded. Marked a general narration on wronging people's
rights, not a cause of revelation — no fetched tafsir attaches one.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi (no commentary in the fetched text).
Did not equate al-Aykah with Madyan (disputed, unsourced here).

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or community"
statement in both languages; the people of al-Aykah are the condemned
people the verse describes, while the command stands as a universal ethic
of honest dealing and not corrupting the earth. Did not preempt 26:184 or
the punishment of 26:185+.
