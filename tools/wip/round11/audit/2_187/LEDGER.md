## Attributions and the clause each rests on

§1
- al-Qurtubi, uhilla means it had been forbidden before: "فيه ست وثلاثون مسألة: الأولى: قوله تعالى: أحل لكم لفظ أحل يقتضي أنه كان محرما قبل ذلك ثم نسخ".
- Ma'arif, earlier rule not in the Qur'an's text: "the order or rule which has been abrogated by this verse ... has not appeared in the text of the Holy Qur'an anywhere. The noble Companions acted in accordance with this rule as set by the Holy Prophet ﷺ".
- Ibn 'Abbas via at-Tabari (2396), rafath and allusion: "الرفث: الجماع, ولكن الله كريم يكني".
- 2:185 "yuridu llahu bikumu l-yusra" and 2:186 are the Qur'an's own text (PREP.md).

§2
- Ibn Kathir, the earlier rule and the hardship: "كان إذا أفطر أحدهم إنما يحل له الأكل والشرب والجماع إلى صلاة العشاء أو ينام قبل ذلك ... فوجدوا من ذلك مشقة كبيرة".
- as-Sa'di: "فحصلت المشقة لبعضهم، فخفف الله تعالى عنهم ذلك، وأباح في ليالي الصيام كلها الأكل والشرب والجماع، سواء نام أو لم ينم".
- Bukhari 1915, quoted whole (see Hadith).

## Asbab an-nuzul

Used: the occasion as al-Bukhari words it (1915 for Qays, 4508 for "some men used
to betray themselves"). 'Umar's case only as the tafsirs report it, by name, with
the note that they attach no grading. In line with the brief, the article never calls
'Umar's act a sin in its own voice. It uses the verse's "takhtanuna anfusakum" and
"taba 'alaykum", and Ibn al-'Arabi's quoted line. Not quoted: the Prophet's ﷺ remark to
'Umar in at-Tabari 2415 ("lam takun haqiqan bi-dhalika") and the details of the
night in the Ibn Abi Layla reports. These were left out for register; the remark is
confirmed in the text, the reports are not graded.

## Dropped or not used

- Ibn al-'Arabi via al-Qurtubi: the occasion was 'Umar's case rather than Qays's hunger,
  or the verse would have said "so now eat". al-Qurtubi also says the first half answers
  'Umar and "kulu wa-shrabu" answers Qays. This conflicts with Bukhari 1915, which
  links the opening words to Qays. Dropped for length; an auditor may want it back as
  a disagreement.
- al-Qurtubi's dawn disagreement: the majority view of the dawn spread across the
  horizon, against a group (reports from 'Umar, Hudhayfah, Ibn 'Abbas, Talq, 'Ata',
  al-A'mash) for light in the streets. Also at-Tabari's second view (the white thread
  is sunlight; the Hudhayfah reports). Dropped for length. Only at-Tabari's verdict
  is kept.
- Ma'arif's "two extremes" reading of yatabayyana (neither over-anxious nor careless):
  dropped for length.
- Mujahid, Qatadah and as-Suddi on libas = sakan (at-Tabari) were cut in a trim.
  Ibn Kathir's longer list is also unused.
- Not used: az-Zajjaj on rafath, al-Qurtubi on the preposition ila, the reading
  "wa-ttabi'u" (Ibn 'Abbas), and al-Qurtubi's view that "ma kataba" is the Qur'an
  (Ibn 'Abbas, az-Zajjaj).
- Ibn Kathir's suhur hadiths, Bilal / Ibn Umm Maktum, the junub hadiths, wisal and the
  "wide pillow / wide nape" wordings of the 'Adi report. Not used (length and register).
- as-Suddi's report in at-Tabari that the fast was prescribed to the Christians with
  the same sleep rule. Not used: unattached to any graded hadith, and 2:183 already
  treats earlier communities.
- Which mosques: al-Qurtubi's three views, as-Sa'di (mosques of the five prayers),
  Ma'arif (any mosque with congregation). Dropped for length.
- Ibn 'Abbas via Ibn Kathir (the i'tikaf rule holds night and day, in or out of
  Ramadan) and ad-Dahhak (men left the mosque to go to their wives): cut in a trim.
- The Safiyyah hadith and al-Shafi'i's comment in Ibn Kathir; 'A'isha combing his
  hair; Ma'arif on kissing, gargling and medicine while fasting. Not used.
- al-Qurtubi on kaffarah, forgetful eating, the menstruating woman, cupping,
  intention, Shawwal fasts. Off-centre, not used.
- Nothing was written from memory. No claim was softened in place of dropping it.
