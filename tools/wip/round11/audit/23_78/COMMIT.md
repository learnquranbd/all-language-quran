Card and deep bilingual article on the three faculties as a gift that
demands both gratitude and right use: hearing, sight, and the heart that
understands.

Spine: the ear, eye and afʾidah are the very instruments by which the
recited verses should have been received; earlier in the surah (23:66-67)
these same people had the verses recited to them and turned back on their
heels. The gift arrives as a claim, and "little do you give thanks" is the
verdict on faculties given but not spent on their purpose.

Sources give:
- afʾidah = intellect/reason, near-unanimous: Ibn Kathir (العقول والفهوم),
  as-Sa'di (العقول, what sets man apart from beasts), at-Tabari (تفقهون),
  al-Muyassar (تفقهوا), al-Baghawi (تعقلوا). Fuʾād-as-emotion dropped.
- Ibn Kathir: ear and eye are means to take lessons from the signs pointing
  to Allah's oneness (tawḥīd).
- at-Tabari: verse addresses the resurrection-deniers — the One who first
  produced (anshaʾa) the faculties can restore them; ties to 23:79-80.
- as-Sa'di: the counterfactual (deaf, blind, no reason — what would you
  lose?); gratitude is the door to standing on His tawḥīd and obedience.

Disagreement kept: al-Qurtubi's two readings of qalīlan mā tashkurūn —
"only a little" vs the reported view "not at all" — left side by side.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (the seven shaded), quoted whole in Bukhari's
wording, grading not upgraded. Marked GENERAL and NOT attached to 23:78 —
no fetched tafsir attaches a hadith to this verse (stated in the article).

Dropped: links to 16:78 / 32:9 / 67:23 (no fetched tafsir cites them); any
mufassir remark on the hearing-before-sight order (none comment on it, so
noted only as the verse's own wording). Asbab not used — the Abu Sufyan
famine occasion belongs to 23:76, not 23:78; verse placement used instead.
