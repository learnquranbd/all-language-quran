Ya-Sin opens with Allah swearing by His own Book, and 36:3-5 are
the oath's answer: you are among the messengers, on a straight path,
a revelation of the Almighty, the Merciful. The card and article keep
the weight on 36:2 and name the answer only to complete the qasam.

al-ḥakīm is given as a range, no position taken: the wise / full of
ḥikma (as-Sa'di), the firmly-made / muḥkam (Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari,
al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi), and the judging-deciding sense of the root
ḥ-k-m (kept as the root's range, not pinned on one mufassir).

Sources: Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari read muḥkam (falsehood reaches it
from neither side; firm by its aḥkām and proofs); al-Qurtubi presses
it to no invalidity/contradiction, cites 11:1, and notes faʿīl can
stand for mufʿil (ḥakīm ~ muḥkam, like alīm ~ mu'lim); as-Sa'di reads
ḥikma as putting each thing in its place and joining a ruling to its
reason; al-Muyassar glosses the path of 36:4 as Islam. al-Baghawi
gives no gloss on the verse and is not cited.

36:1 (Ya Sin) is passed over as muqaṭṭaʿāt whose meaning rests with
Allah, tied to the 26:1 approach; Ma'arif's reports that it names
Allah or the Prophet ﷺ are given as unasserted.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6108 (swear by Allah or keep silent) quoted
whole and marked as a general narration not tied to 36:2, used for the
oath adab. The famous "Ya Sin is the heart of the Qur'an" (Tirmidhi
2887) is reported as weak in at-Tirmidhi's own words (gharīb, a majhūl
narrator, a ḍaʿīf supporting chain) and nothing is rested on it; the
other virtue narrations are dropped. No sabab established; verse
placement written instead.
