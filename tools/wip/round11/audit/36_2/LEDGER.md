# SOURCES — 36:2 (wa'l-Qur'āni'l-ḥakīm)

## Tafsir fetched (all pre-fetched per PREP; none re-fetched)
IDs: 14, 15, 16, 90, 91, 94, 168, 169.

### Named in the article, with the Arabic/English clause each attribution rests on
- **Ibn Kathir (14 ar / 169 en)** — al-ḥakīm = al-muḥkam: "المحكم الذي لا يأتيه
  الباطل من بين يديه ولا من خلفه" / "Al-Muhkam (perfect) which falsehood cannot
  come to from before it or behind it." (Noted in article as echoing 41:42 — my
  cross-reference, not attributed to him.) 169 also gives 36:4 "following a
  straight methodology and religion, and an upright Law" and 36:5 "a revelation
  from the Lord of might Who is Most Merciful to His believing servants."
- **at-Tabari (15)** — muḥkam "بما فيه من أحكامه، وبيِّنات حججه" = by its rulings
  (aḥkām) and the clear proofs of its arguments.
- **al-Muyassar (16)** — "يقسم الله تعالى بالقرآن المحكم بما فيه من الأحكام والحكم
  والحجج" (swears by the muḥkam Qur'an: rulings, wisdom, proofs); and the oath's
  answer "إنك أيها الرسول لمن المرسلين ... على طريق مستقيم معتدل، وهو الإسلام"
  (36:3-4, the path = Islam). Covers 36:2,3,4.
- **al-Qurtubi (90)** — "والحكيم: المحكم حتى لا يتعرض لبطلان وتناقض، كما قال:
  أحكمت آياته" (muḥkam = no invalidity/contradiction, cites 11:1); firmness in
  "نظمه ومعانيه فلا يلحقه خلل"; and the grammar point "وقد يكون الحكيم في حق الله
  بمعنى المحكم، بكسر الكاف، كالأليم بمعنى المؤلم" (faʿīl standing for mufʿil).
- **as-Sa'di (91)** — ḥikma = "وضع كل شيء موضعه" (command/prohibition and
  recompense each in its fitting place); and "يجمع بين ذكر الحكم وحكمته" (joins a
  ruling to the wisdom behind it).
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an (168)** — muqaṭṭaʿāt: "one of the isolated letters the
  knowledge of which rests with Allah alone"; Imam Malik (via Ibn al-ʿArabi) and
  a narration from Ibn Abbas: one of the Divine Names; Ethiopian "O human";
  Ibn Jubayr: a name of the Prophet ﷺ.

### Fetched but NOT named (dropped)
- **al-Baghawi (94)** — file contains only the verse ("والقرآن الحكيم"), no gloss,
  so nothing attributed to him.

## Lexical range of al-ḥakīm (watch-point: give the range, no position)
Presented as three facets of one word, no position taken:
(a) the wise / full of ḥikma — as-Sa'di (91);
(b) the firmly-made / muḥkam — Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi;
(c) the judging/deciding sense of the root ḥ-k-m — framed as the root's own range
(supported by at-Tabari's aḥkām and al-Qurtubi's discussion), NOT pinned on a
single mufassir.

## Hadith
- **Sahih al-Bukhari 6108** — USED, quoted whole, English wording.
  URL: https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-6108
  First ten words: "Verily! Allah forbids you to swear by your fathers."
  Grading: Sahih (Sahih al-Bukhari). **Marked in the article as a general
  narration NOT revealed about / tied to 36:2** — used only for the oath adab
  (a human swears by Allah alone; Allah swears by what He wills).
- **Jami at-Tirmidhi 2887** — "Ya Sin is the heart of the Qur'an" (Anas).
  URL: https://quranx.com/hadith/Tirmidhi/Hadith-2887
  First ten words: "Indeed for everything there is a heart, and the"
  Grading: at-Tirmidhi's OWN words — "hādhā ḥadīthun gharīb", narrator Hārūn Abū
  Muḥammad "shaykhun majhūl" (unknown), and the corroborating Abū Bakr report
  "isnāduhu ḍaʿīf" (weak). Reported honestly as weak; NO virtue/reward claim
  rested on it. Grading NOT upgraded.

## Asbab an-nuzul
None used. No established sabab for 36:2; the verse's placement (oath +
its answer in 36:3-5) is written instead.

## Dropped (could not confirm, or chose not to lean on)
- Virtue-of-Ya-Sin narrations in Ma'arif (reciting over the dead/dying; Torah
  names; intercession counts) and in Ibn Kathir 169 (Abū Hurayrah "recites Ya Sin
  in the night → wakes forgiven", chain graded "jayyid"; Jundub via Ibn Hibban) —
  dropped; none used to assert reward. Only the "heart of the Qur'an" narration is
  named, and only to report its weakness.
- al-Baghawi gloss — none exists for this verse (see above).
