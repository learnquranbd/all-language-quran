Tadabbur 20:87 — the calf-makers' excuse: "we were made to carry" burdens
from the ornaments; the anatomy of a bad excuse.

Card (theme: character): the reply as a three-move excuse — deny the will,
blame the weight, point at another hand — with the plain word it never says:
I did it. No verdict on anyone living.

Article, 8 sections / ~1,797 EN words, source-gated:
- bi-malkinā across qirāʾāt (Qurtubi, Baghawi): by our power / authority /
  choice; Qurtubi's maṣdar reading that turns it into a confession of error.
- awzār = burdens (= sin), zīnat al-qawm = the Egyptians' ornaments; borrowed
  on a feast-pretext and not returned (Qurtubi, Baghawi, as-Sa'di, Ma'arif),
  with the sea-cast-spoil alternative. Either way, wealth never rightly theirs.
- ḥummilnā (made to carry) vs ḥamalnā (we carried): the passive that hides the
  doer, and Qurtubi's recorded counter that they carried it willingly.
- fa-qadhafnāhā: the pit and fire; Hārūn (AS) keeping them from unlawful spoil
  vs the Sāmirī urging the casting — kept as a disagreement.
- fa-kadhālika alqā al-Sāmirī: his own casting (dust from the hoofprint of
  Jibrīl's horse per Muyassar/Baghawi; a handful from the messenger's track per
  Sa'di/Ibn Kathir); identity kept to what is fetched; result reserved for 20:88.
- Ibn Kathir's al-ʿudhr al-bārid (the cold excuse) dissected (§7).
- Explicit note (both languages): the verse licenses nothing against any living
  person or community (§8).

Hadith: none cited — no sahih hadith attaches to this verse; the pit/dust scene
rests on ḥadīth al-futūn (Ibn ʿAbbās), which Ma'arif flags as not wholly
reliable. Said so in one sentence.

Dropped: Qurtubi's 12,000/600,000 figures; Ibn Kathir's off-verse Ibn ʿUmar /
al-Husayn athar. 20:88 (the lowing calf; "this is your god") not preempted.
