# LEDGER 37:112

## Tafsir fetched (from PREP.md, pre-fetched) and used

- Ibn Kathir, ar (t14_112.txt, 1103 chars). Used: his own stated position
  — "لما تقدمت البشارة بالذبيح وهو إسماعيل، عطف بذكر البشارة بأخيه إسحاق"
  (since the tiding of the one to be sacrificed, Isma'il in his view, had
  already preceded, [Allah] followed with the tiding of his brother
  Ishaq) — attributed to Ibn Kathir by name as his own reading, not the
  article's verdict; his cross-reference to Surahs Hud and al-Hijr (named
  only, content not asserted since those surahs were not fetched); his
  grammatical note that نبيا is a حال مقدرة (circumstantial description
  anticipating the future). Also used for three distinct chains he
  transmits back to Ibn 'Abbas via 'Ikrimah: (1) via Ya'qub/Ibn
  'Ulayyah/Dawud — "الذبيح إسحاق... بشر بنبوته" (the one to be sacrificed
  is Ishaq... he was given tidings of his prophethood), plus the Harun/
  19:53 analogy Ibn 'Abbas is reported to use ("كان هارون أكبر من موسى
  ولكن أراد وهب له نبوته"); (2) via Ibn 'Abd al-A'la/al-Mu'tamir b.
  Sulayman — "إنما بشر به نبيا حين فداه الله من الذبح، ولم تكن البشارة
  بالنبوة عند مولده" (he was given tidings of him as a prophet only when
  Allah ransomed him from the slaughter; the prophethood-tiding was not
  at his birth); (3) via Ibn Abi Hatim/Sufyan al-Thawri — "بشر به حين ولد،
  وحين نبئ" (given tidings of him at birth and at being made a prophet).
  Flagged explicitly in the article that chains (2) and (3), both run
  through 'Ikrimah from Ibn 'Abbas, disagree with each other on whether
  the prophethood-tiding is tied to the ransom moment. Also used Qatada's
  report (via Sa'id b. Abi 'Arubah) — "بعد ما كان من أمره لما جاد لله
  بنفسه" (after what had happened regarding his affair, when he gave of
  himself generously to Allah) — kept as a neutral chronology marker per
  the brief's watch-point, not assigned to either camp.
- at-Tabari (t15_112.txt, 1458 chars). Used: his own opening gloss —
  "بشرنا إبراهيم بإسحاق نبيا شكرا له على إحسانه وطاعته" (reward for his
  good deeds and obedience) — taken as neutral, not a verdict on identity.
  Used for the same Ibn 'Abbas/'Ikrimah chain (1) above, which at-Tabari
  also transmits essentially verbatim, confirming it is not unique to
  Ibn Kathir's record. Used for the same Qatada report, nearly identical
  wording. NOT used: the closing chain naming Ibrahim's age as 117 at the
  time of the tiding (anonymous "a shaykh of the mosque-folk," no named
  authority) — dropped as unconfirmable/weak and irrelevant to the
  dispute.
- al-Qurtubi (t90_112.txt, 202 chars). Used: his report of Ibn 'Abbas —
  "بشر بنبوته... وذهب إلى أن البشارة كانت مرتين، فعلى هذا الذبيح هو إسحاق"
  (he was given tidings of his prophethood... and held the tiding came
  twice; so on this view the one to be sacrificed is Ishaq) — attributed
  explicitly to al-Qurtubi as his own drawn inference built on the Ibn
  'Abbas report, flagged as such (not presented as Ibn 'Abbas's own
  verdict). His closing phrase — "جزاء على صبره ورضاه بأمر ربه واستسلامه
  له" (a reward for his patience, his contentment with his Lord's command,
  and his submission to it) — used and flagged as echoing the sacrifice
  story's own vocabulary.
- al-Baghawi (t94_112.txt, 225 chars). Used in full: the two-camp framing
  — "فمن جعل الذبيح إسماعيل قال: بشره بعد هذه القصة بإسحاق نبيا جزاء
  لطاعته، ومن جعل الذبيح إسحاق قال: بشر إبراهيم بنبوة إسحاق" — presented
  as al-Baghawi laying both readings side by side without choosing; his
  attached report via 'Ikrimah from Ibn 'Abbas, "بشر به مرتين حين ولد وحين
  نبئ" (given tidings of him twice, at birth and at prophethood).
- as-Sa'di (t91_112.txt, 183 chars). Used in full: "هذه البشارة الثانية
  بإسحاق... فبشر بوجوده وبقائه، ووجود ذريته، وكونه نبيا من الصالحين، فهي
  بشارات متعددة" (this is the second tiding of Ishaq... multiple tidings
  folded together) — noted that as-Sa'di does not engage the dhabih
  question at all in this entry.
- al-Muyassar (t16_112.txt, 92 chars). Used in full: "جزاء له على صبره
  ورضاه بأمر ربه، وطاعته له" (reward for his patience, his contentment
  with his Lord's command, and his obedience to Him) — noted as the same
  trio of virtues al-Qurtubi used to argue for Ishaq, here left unattached
  to either camp.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an, en (t168_112.txt, 13595 chars, confirmed covers
  37:112). Read in full. Used ONLY the non-polemical, factual portion:
  that this is a longstanding difference among the Sahabah and Tabi'in
  themselves (named on both sides, several individuals credited with both
  views), and that later commentators split too (stated there that
  at-Tabari leaned to the Ishaq view and Ibn Kathir to the Isma'il view).
  Deliberately NOT used: its lengthy engagement with the Genesis text,
  its claim about Ka'b al-Ahbar's Jewish-convert reports being the root
  of the Ishaq view, and its "envy"/textual-corruption framing of Jewish
  tradition — dropped per the watch-point's non-polemical instruction;
  the article does not characterise Jewish or Christian tradition at all,
  only notes a reader "may recognise this question from outside the
  Qur'an's own tradition" without engaging or rebutting it.
- Ibn Kathir, en abridged (t169_112.txt) — WRONG GROUP per PREP.md (covers
  37:99-108, not 37:112). Not opened, not used.

## Sensitive-verse handling (per watch-points)

- The Isma'il/Ishaq dhabih dispute is kept as a genuinely unresolved,
  multi-named range across the whole article (§§2-6): Ibn Kathir's own
  reading (§2) vs. the Ibn 'Abbas attribution carried by both Ibn Kathir
  and at-Tabari (§3) vs. the internal inconsistency even within the
  Ibn 'Abbas/'Ikrimah chains (§4) vs. al-Baghawi's explicit two-camp
  framing (§5) vs. al-Qurtubi's own inference and Qatada's neutral
  chronology marker (§6). No section hands down a verdict.
- §8 frames the dispute explicitly as internal to the earliest Muslim
  narrators (Ibn 'Abbas credited with both answers; Qatada and 'Ikrimah
  inside chains read both ways; at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir landing in
  different places) rather than as Islam-vs-other-traditions. The one
  sentence acknowledging the Bible's Isaac account is descriptive only
  ("readers who know... may recognise this question from outside the
  Qur'an's own tradition") with no engagement, rebuttal, or claim about
  that tradition's text.
- §8 also carries an explicit licensing guard: nothing in this dispute
  licenses treating either of Ibrahim's sons, or any people tracing their
  line through either one, as lesser for being named inside a dispute
  "they never chose to have" — applied symmetrically regardless of which
  camp a reader favours.
- 37:113 (descendants: doer of good / clearly unjust) is not pulled
  forward anywhere; the article's closing paragraph deliberately stops at
  Ishaq's own naming as "a prophet from among the righteous" and does not
  gesture at his descendants' split.
- Prophets' honorific used in English prose is "(AS)" (matching the
  shipped 12:25 template exactly); Bengali prose uses "(আঃ)" throughout,
  per this verse's explicit watch-point instruction (overriding the
  general BANGLA-STYLE.md default of "(আ)" in favour of matching what
  has actually shipped).
