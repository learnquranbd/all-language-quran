Lūṭ asks his people one question (27:54): do you commit the fāḥisha while
you see? The article keeps, as a named difference, the two tafsir readings of
wa-antum tubṣirūn and takes no side: (a) inward — you perceive and know it is
a vile deed (at-Tabari, al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar "while you know its ugliness",
al-Qurtubi "sin the greater for it", as-Saʿdi: minds, fiṭra and the laws judge
it ugly, so they opposed it out of wrongdoing and brazenness towards Allah);
(b) outward — you see one another, openly, refusing to conceal yourselves out
of insolence and rebellion (al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi "it is said"; Ibn Kathir:
you see one another and commit evil in your assembly, nādī).

Angle is distinct from the shipped 26:165: here shamelessness and open-eyed,
deliberate defiance, not "the males of the worlds" / condemning on suspicion.

Hadith: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 6120 (Abū Masʿūd), "if you feel no shame, do as you
wish" — wording Bukhari's, sound by its place in the Ṣaḥīḥ, not upgraded, and
marked a general ḥayāʾ principle, NOT a report about the people of Lūṭ. Stated
plainly that no fetched commentary attaches a hadith to this verse.

Sensitive-verse protocol honoured: sober register; the object condemned is a
conduct as the Qur'an frames it, not a modern category or any living person;
§6 "A Conduct, Not a People" says prominently in both languages that it licenses
nothing against anyone alive, that judgement belongs to Allah and to lawful
authority under due process and never to private hands, and that human dignity
is untouched. No slur, caricature or call to punishment. Lūṭ (AS) only warns;
never said to have erred.

Dropped: Ma'arif al-Qur'an (grouped fetch returned 27:49 Thamūd text, not the
Lūṭ verse); any ḥadd/punishment narration; the "(i.e., homosexuality)" gloss in
the Ibn Kathir English; and the 29:29 number for the nādī phrase (not cited in
the fetched text).
