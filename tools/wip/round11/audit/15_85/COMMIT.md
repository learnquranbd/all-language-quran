Replaces the 724-word, 6-section article with a 1,765-word, 8-section one,
from at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar and en), al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di,
the Muyassar and Ma'arif on 15:85. Slots checked, none shifted. Shipped card
kept.
Bil-haqq: justice (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir with 53:31, 38:27, 23:115-116),
passing away or requital (al-Qurtubi), signification (as-Sa'di, Muyassar),
truth and wisdom (Ma'arif); at-Tabari ties it to the nations of al-Hijr.
The Hour: at-Tabari's "be content with it", Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, the
Muyassar; as-Sa'di's greater-creation argument; 15:86 one line.
As-safh al-jamil: turning away and fine pardon (at-Tabari, al-Baghawi,
al-Qurtubi with 73:10 and al-Hasan's i'rad), the Muyassar's three verbs,
Ibn Kathir with 43:89, Ma'arif's conduct; as-Sa'di's two readings, the
second (no rancour, not in the wrong place) as his preferred.
Abrogation kept as a disagreement: Qatada, ad-Dahhak, Mujahid, Sufyan (in
at-Tabari), al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir's Makkan reason, against
al-Qurtubi's "not abrogated, pardon in his own right". No position taken.
Licensing line in both languages (Thamud and the fighting verses).
Hadith: none; the sayings at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi ascribe to the Prophet
here carry no reference or grading and are not quoted. No asbab.
Fixed in the resumed partial: the 38:27 gloss (Bengali had inverted it).
Dropped from the shipped article as unsourced: the safh etymology, the
grammar note, the jamil family beyond 73:10, 15:94-95, 42:40, and the
stated reason for the non-abrogation view.
