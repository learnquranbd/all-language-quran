Card and 9-section article (1,771 EN words, 20 paragraphs, 73-100 each).
12:43 is 24 Arabic words, per PREP.md with pause marks excluded.

Sources: all eight tafsir IDs; 169 is grouped 12:43-49 and 168 12:42-43,
both stated, and only their 12:43 content used. The king is called by the
verse's title al-malik; "king of Egypt" (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi)
and the name ar-Rayyan ibn al-Walid (Ibn Ishaq via at-Tabari; al-Qurtubi)
given as their reports, with no dynastic claims. At-Tabari on "inni ara"
as a dream report by Arab usage; al-Qurtubi on 'ibara from crossing a
river, and the lam of li-r-ru'ya with az-Zajjaj. The dream as Allah's
means of Yusuf's honourable release (Ibn Kathir ar and en; as-Sa'di, with
the fittingness of the ruler seeing it); al-Qurtubi's Jibril report
flagged as unsourced in the passage.

Disagreements kept: who made up al-mala' (seven accounts side by side);
whether the dry ears overcame the green (as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi)
or the verse's plain line (Muyassar, at-Tabari); the length of the wait
(al-Kalbi via al-Baghawi; Ma'arif's 3-9, some say seven more).

No fetched tafsir attaches a hadith or asbab to 12:43, and the article
says so. The cows-and-ears symbolism is left to 12:47-49 and 12:44's reply
gets one pointer.

Dropped: 'Ali's dream-lore in al-Qurtubi, al-Qurtubi's 12:44 material,
Ibn Kathir en's 12:44-49 interpretation, two grammar/lexical asides cut
for length.
