Card plus deep bilingual article on 34:21: Iblīs had no sulṭān over
the people of Saba — only a call and a whisper; the going-astray was
their own choice, and his "authority" was permitted solely as a test
that sorts the believer in the Hereafter from the one in doubt, the
whole of it under "your Lord guards all things."

Sources (all fetched for this verse, nothing from memory):
- al-Qurtubi: sulṭān = power / proof; "only the call and the beautifying
  of sin"; followers moved by appetite, imitation and whim, not proof.
  The istithnāʾ is munqaṭiʿ (illā = lākin). "Knowing" = ʿilm al-shahāda,
  with the Farrāʾ reading and "that our friends and the angels may know".
- at-Tabari: al-Hasan al-Basri — "no staff, no sword, no whip, only
  false hopes and delusion he called them to"; Qatāda — "it was only a
  trial so God might know the believer from the ingrate".
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en): Ibn Abbas — sulṭān = ḥujja; the ḥafīẓ double
  edge: by His watching some stray, by His care the believers are saved.
- as-Sa'di: the "market of testing", firm faith vs faith that wavers at
  the least doubt; the Guardian keeps servants, deeds and recompense.
- al-Baghawi: the knowing is ʿilm al-wuqūʿ wa-l-ẓuhūr, already known in
  the unseen — God's knowledge does not grow.

No disagreement of substance among the mufassirūn here; the two senses
of sulṭān (power / proof) are kept side by side, not adjudicated.

Hadith: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhari 660 (Abu Hurairah, the seven whom Allah
shades — the man who refuses the woman's call, "I fear Allah"). Ṣaḥīḥ
by Bukhari's standard; marked as general and not revealed about the
verse, since no tafsir attaches a prophetic hadith to 34:21.

Cross-light: 14:22 (Iblīs's own confession). Context: 34:20.

Dropped: Ma'arif's 34:19 material (soothsayer, the dispersal, the
sabr/shukr hadith) as belonging to 34:19; Ibn Kathir's 17:62 / 7:16-17
citations to keep refs light. Added the standing note that the verse
describes a people long gone and licenses nothing against any living
person or community.
