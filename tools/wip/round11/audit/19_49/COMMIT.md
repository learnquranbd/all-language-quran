Card and deep bilingual article on 19:49: after Ibrahim withdrew from his
father, his people and their idols, Allah gave him Ishaq and Yaʿqub, each
made a prophet.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari and al-Muyassar: the withdrawal had two objects, the people and
  the idols worshipped besides Allah.
- as-Sa'di: leaving homeland, family and people is among the hardest things
  on the soul; the rule the story turns on is that whoever leaves a thing for
  Allah, Allah replaces it with something better. Ma'arif al-Qur'an: the son
  came to compensate the loss; 19:48 was a prayer against loneliness, granted
  here.
- at-Tabari, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi (from Ibn Abbas): the gift consoled his
  loneliness with a child.
- al-Baghawi: the gift came after the migration. Ibn Kathir: Ishaq is the son,
  Yaʿqub the grandson (21:72, 11:71; no dispute per 2:133); only these two are
  named to show prophets in his line within his lifetime.
- at-Tabari: nabiyyan is singular because kull is singular. as-Sa'di: the
  honour of a chosen prophetic line.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3353 (Abu Huraira), the Yusuf–Yaʿqub–Ishaq–Ibrahim
lineage; quoted from Bukhari alone; sahih, cited muttafaq ʿalayh by Ibn Kathir.

Dropped: the Makkah-vs-Shām explanation for Ismail's absence (in no fetched
tafsir) — the article names only the migration and lifetime-lineage reasons and
says so. 19:50 content held back for the next ayah. No licensing sentence (not a
condemned-group verse).
