Caps the passage on those who hasten to good works (23:57-61): God's
justice stands on two legs — He obligates no soul beyond its wusʿ, and a
record that "speaks with truth" testifies, so no one is wronged.

Capacity (wusʿ): Ibn Kathir and at-Tabari gloss it as what a soul can bear
and what suits it in worship; al-Muyassar, what it has room to act upon.
as-Sa'di ties the verse to the preceding good-deeds passage — lest a reader
fear the demand is beyond reach — and notes the task is set below full
strength as mercy and wisdom. al-Baghawi makes it concrete: whoever cannot
stand prays seated, whoever cannot fast breaks it. al-Qurtubi refers the
clause back to al-Baqarah 2:286 (which he calls the abrogator of taklīf mā
lā yuṭāq) — the only reason 2:286 is cited.

The record that "speaks": disagreement kept as disagreement. Book of deeds
raised by the angels (Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi's most
apparent, al-Baghawi secondary) vs al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ / "the first Book"
(al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di, al-Qurtubi 2nd) vs the Qur'an (al-Qurtubi 3rd);
al-Qurtubi's own ranking reported, no position taken. al-Qurtubi: the image
is "a threat and a cutting-off of every hope of injustice." Not-wronged:
no sin added, no good diminished (at-Tabari, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi); Ibn
Kathir adds the exactness runs to mercy — good counted whole, much evil
overlooked for believers.

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2639 (the bitāqah), graded hasan gharīb by Tirmidhi (not
upgraded), used as thematic illustration and marked as not verse-attached,
since no fetched mufassir attaches a hadith to 23:62.

Dropped: Ibn Masʿūd's sealing-of-deeds hadith (belongs to 23:63);
al-Qurtubi's garbled "nuṭq" clause. Asbab: none established; placement given.
