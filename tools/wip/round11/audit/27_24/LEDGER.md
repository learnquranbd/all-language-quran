# SOURCES — 27:24

## Tafsir fetched (per PREP.md, not re-fetched)

- **at-Tabari (15)** — NAMED. Arabic clauses rested on:
  - "وجدت هذه المرأة ملكة سبأ, وقومها من سبأ, يسجدون للشمس فيعبدونها من دون الله" → queen of Sheba and her people prostrate to / worship the sun in place of Allah (§2.1).
  - "وحسَّن لهم إبليس عبادتهم الشمس... وحبَّب ذلك إليهم" → Iblis beautified (ḥassana) their sun-worship and made it beloved (ḥabbaba) to them (§3.1).
  - "فمنعهم بتزيينه ذلك لهم أن يتبعوا الطريق المستقيم, وهو دين الله الذي بعث به أنبياءه" → by that adorning he prevented them from the straight road, the religion of Allah sent with His prophets (§4.1).
  - "فهم... لا يهتدون لسبيل الحقّ ولا يسلكونه, ولكنهم في ضلالهم الذي هم فيه يتردّدون" → they do not find/walk the way of truth but keep wavering (yataraddadūn) in their misguidance (§5.3).
- **al-Qurtubi (90)** — NAMED. Clauses:
  - "قيل: كانت هذه الأمة ممن يعبد الشمس; لأنهم كانوا زنادقة فيما يروى. وقيل: كانوا مجوسا يعبدون الأنوار" → it was said they worshipped the sun because (as narrated) they were heretics; and it was said they were Magians worshipping the lights (§2.2) — kept as an unresolved difference.
  - "فصدهم عن السبيل أي عن طريق التوحيد. وبين بهذا أن ما ليس بسبيل التوحيد فليس بسبيل ينتفع به على التحقيق" → sabīl = way of tawḥīd; what is not the path of tawḥīd is not truly a beneficial path (§4.2).
  - The verse-23 waqf discussion (Nafiʿ / al-Mahdawi / Ibn al-Anbari on "ʿarsh"/"ʿaẓīm") was read but DROPPED — it concerns 27:23 grammar, not this verse.
- **as-Saʿdi (91)** — NAMED. Clauses:
  - "هم مشركون يعبدون الشمس" → they are mushrikūn worshipping the sun (§5.1).
  - "فرأوا ما عليه هو الحق" → they saw what they were upon as the truth (§5.1).
  - "لأن الذي يرى أن الذي عليه حق لا مطمع في هدايته حتى تتغير عقيدته" → whoever sees what he is on as truth has no prospect of guidance until his belief changes (§5.2).
- **al-Muyassar (16)** — NAMED. Clause: "وحسَّن لهم الشيطان أعمالهم السيئة التي كانوا يعملونها" → Satan beautified for them the evil deeds they were doing (§3.2).
- **Ibn Kathir — en abridged (169)** — NAMED. Clauses/content:
  - "the hoopoe stayed not long... he was absent for only a short time"; "Saba' refers to Himyar... a dynasty in Yemen" (§1.1).
  - al-Hasan al-Basri: "This is Bilqis bint Sharahil, the queen of Saba'"; throne "adorned with gold and different kinds of jewels and pearls" (§1.2). (Bilqis's name not used in body; "queen of Sheba" used.)
  - historians' palace with windows E/W, sun entering at rising and setting, people prostrating morning and evening (§2.1). Exact window count (360 each side) DROPPED to avoid a bare number; rendered as "windows along the east wall and the west."
  - "from the way of truth" for fa-saddahum ʿan as-sabīl (§4.2).
  - cross-reference to 41:37 quoted in the fetched text (§6.1).
  - the hoopoe calling to good → forbidden to kill it; the four-creatures narration (§6.2).
- **Ibn Kathir — ar (14)** — read; confirms "ʿan ṭarīq al-ḥaqq". Not separately named (en edition 169 carries the same point).
- **al-Baghawi (94)** — fetched but contains ONLY the verse text, no commentary. DROPPED; not named anywhere.
- **Maʿarif al-Qurʾan (168)** — NAMED. "her people were star-worshippers... Some believe that they were Zoroastrians, who worship fire and all forms of light" (§2.2).

## Hadith

- **Sunan Abu Dawud, no. 5267** (Dar-us-Salam; Ahmad Hasan tr. 5247).
  - URL fetched: https://quranx.com/hadith/AbuDawud/DarusSalam/Hadith-5267
  - Narrator: Abdullah ibn Abbas.
  - First ten words (EN used): "The Prophet prohibited to kill four creatures: ants, bees, hoopoes..."
  - Grading: the fetched quranx page surfaced reference + text only, NO grading field; so none is asserted in the article. (Ibn Kathir calls the chain "Sahih," but that is his grading and is not reproduced as the collector's own — not claimed in the body.)
  - Fits §6.2: directly about the hoopoe (hudhud), not a stock neighbour hadith. Avoided Bukhari 660 and Bukhari 3443 (used at 26:123) per watch-point.
  - Note: Ibn Kathir attributes this report to Abu Hurayrah and lists Ahmad/Abu Dawud/Ibn Majah; the fetched Abu Dawud page shows Ibn Abbas. Body cites the Abu Dawud wording and its narrator (Ibn Abbas) as actually fetched — not Ibn Kathir's second-hand attribution.
