Card plus deep bilingual article on the command to repel evil with what
is best, and the promise "We know best what they ascribe."

Sources: at-Tabari (safh/ighda' + patience; Mujahid's "turn away" and
"greet with salam"; al-Hasan's swallowing anger; the placement note that
this came before the command to fight; God knows the slander and will
requite it), Ibn Kathir ar+en (kindness as the beneficial antidote that
turns enmity to friendship), al-Muyassar (do not meet wrong with wrong),
al-Qurtubi (pardon and noble character), as-Sa'di (retaliation permitted
but ihsan better; the benefits it earns; God's own forbearance modelled;
the devils cannot be won by kindness, hence 23:97), al-Baghawi
(forgiveness/turning away/patience), Ma'arif (evil with good, oppression
with justice, cruelty with mercy).

41:34-35 brought in only because Ibn Kathir and as-Sa'di both cite it on
this verse (enmity becomes a warm friend; granted only to the patient,
the owner of great fortune, in this world and the next).

Disagreement kept without a verdict: al-Baghawi/al-Qurtubi/Ma'arif read
part of it as abrogated by fighting (al-Qurtubi: fixed among believers,
a truce-verse toward the deniers) vs as-Sa'di/Ibn Kathir treating it as
standing guidance on character. Stated plainly that this juristic debate
licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6114 (Abu Hurayra) on self-control in anger,
grading sahih, marked as a general narration not attached to the verse
by any tafsir — brought only to illustrate al-Hasan's reading. Bukhari
660 (the PREP suggestion) fetched but dropped as off-theme; the
Ahmad/Tirmidhi and Abu Dawud reports in Ibn Kathir attach to 23:93 and
23:97, not 23:96, so not used. No sabab an-nuzul asserted.
