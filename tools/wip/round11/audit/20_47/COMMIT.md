Mūsā and Hārūn (AS) carry their errand into Pharaoh's throne
room: identify as two messengers (rasūlā rabbika, dual), demand
the release of Banī Isrāʾīl and an end to their torment, and
present the sign.

Sources gave: al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi on the nature of the
torment (forced labour, clay and brick, cities beyond capacity;
sons slaughtered, women spared); as-Sa'di and Ma'arif al-Qur'an
on the dual duty (call to faith + freeing the people); Ibn Abbas
via al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di and al-Baghawi on the sign (staff and
the shining hand). Ibn Kathir glosses the sign as evidence and
miracle.

The subtlety carried by five sources: "wa'l-salāmu ʿalā man
ittabaʿa al-hudā" is NOT a greeting to Pharaoh. az-Zajjāj and
al-Farrā' (in al-Qurtubi), al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar and as-Sa'di
all read it as conditional safety from Allah's punishment for
whoever follows the guidance — az-Zajjāj: "laysa bi-taḥiyya,"
not an opening nor an address. Ibn Kathir: "peace be upon you if
you follow the guidance."

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari (Dar-us-Salam 7), Ibn Abbas from Abu
Sufyan — the Prophet's ﷺ letter to Heraclius opens with this
verse's exact formula, addressed to a non-Muslim king. Sahih;
grading not upgraded.

Banī Isrāʾīl kept strictly scriptural/historical; §7 states the
verse passes no verdict on any modern people, state or conflict.

Dropped: at-Tabari (thin on 20:47); the Ibn Ishaq/as-Suddi
door-waiting stories kept only as flagged narrative material,
not fact; hadith al-Futūn (unconfirmed); "yawm al-zīna" detail.
Did not preempt 20:48–49.
