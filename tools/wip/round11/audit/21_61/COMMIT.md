After the idols are found in fragments, Ibrahim's people resolve to
produce him "before the eyes of the people, that they may testify."

Card and deep bilingual article (8 sections). Sources:

- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir (ar), al-Baghawi,
  al-Muyassar, Ma'arif and Ibn Kathir (en) — all on 21:61 except
  Ma'arif (read on 21:58, named as such).
- ala a'yuni'n-nas: at-Tabari's two glosses (on their heads / before
  their eyes) plus the Arab idiom for a public matter; Ibn Kathir's
  "greatest assembly, all the people present."
- la'allahum yashhadun kept as a live disagreement: testify AGAINST
  him as legal proof (as-Suddi, Qatada, via at-Tabari/al-Baghawi/
  al-Muyassar; they disliked seizing without bayyina) vs. witness his
  PUNISHMENT (Ibn Ishaq). al-Qurtubi's extra shades kept.
- The irony drawn by as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir: the public spectacle
  the idolaters stage becomes the stage Ibrahim wanted, the proof
  standing over the crowd (Sa'di's Musa/Pharaoh parallel, 20:59).
  21:62-64 referenced by number only, not preempted.
- Idols' helplessness (Ibn Kathir "cannot ward off harm nor help
  themselves"; Ma'arif/Kalbi on the hammer, 21:58).
- al-Qurtubi's fiqh note: no one held on a bare accusation.
- Licensing guard in both languages: the verse reports the idolaters'
  speech, passes no verdict on any living person, "wrongdoers" is
  their word and recoils on them by 21:64.

Hadith: none sound on this verse. The Bukhari/Muslim "three
instances of tawriya" report belongs to 21:63 + 37:89 (Ibn Kathir's
placement); grading "In the Two Sahihs", not upgraded, not quoted here.
Asbab: none; narrative placement written instead.
