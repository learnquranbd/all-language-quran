Beat: the patience-and-knowledge principle of 18:67-68 — why Mūsā's patience
must fail where his knowledge does not reach. Card plus 8-section bilingual
article. Fish sign (18:63, sibling) and the three incidents (18:71-82, future)
deliberately left out; 18:69's reply not preempted.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari: Mūsā judges by the ẓāhir and the reach of his own knowledge;
  al-Khiḍr's acts carry no outward proof and begin for causes whose fruit comes
  later, so their end is ghayb — which is what "lam tuḥiṭ bihi khubran" names.
  khubran = ʿilman (also al-Baghawi).
- al-Qurtubi: khabīr = knower of a thing's hidden dimensions; and prophets do
  not acquiesce to an apparent munkar, so silence was not open to Mūsā — his
  objection breaks on a virtue, not weak temper.
- as-Sa'di: patience over what one has not encompassed in bāṭin, ẓāhir, intent
  and outcome.
- Ibn Kathir (ar/en): two God-given knowledges, each holding what the other
  lacked; al-Khiḍr saw the wisdom and hidden benefit Mūsā did not.
- Ma'arif: objections driven by Mūsā's prophetic charge; Abū Ḥayyān's
  inner-realities vs rulings-on-the-apparent distinction; Qāḍī Thanāʾullāh's
  takwīn/law levels; and the guardrail — no non-prophet's kashf licenses the
  unlawful (Ibn ʿAbbās to Najda al-Ḥarūrī, via Ibn Abī Shayba).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3401 (Ubayy ibn Kaʿb), sound; only the
knowledge-distinction exchange quoted.

Disagreement kept open: al-Khiḍr's prophet-vs-walī status is not settled here;
the "majority hold him a prophet" is reported as the sources', not asserted.
