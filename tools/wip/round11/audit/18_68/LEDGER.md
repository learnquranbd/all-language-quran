# SOURCES ledger — 18:68

Beat: the patience-and-knowledge principle of 18:67–68 (why patience must fail
where knowledge is absent). Fish sign (18:63) and the three incidents
(18:71–82) are sibling/future ground and are not retold here.

## Tafsir fetched (all pre-fetched in audit/18_68)

- **at-Tabari (15)** — NAMED. Arabic clause rested on:
  "وكيف تصبر يا موسى على ما ترى مني من الأفعال التي لا علم لك بوجوه صوابها … وأنت
  إنما تحكم … بالظاهر الذي عندك، وبمبلغ علمك، وأفعالي تقع بغير دليل ظاهر لرأي عينك
  على صوابها، لأنها تبتدئ لأسباب تحدث آجلة غير عاجلة، لا علم لك بالحادث عنها، لأنها
  غيب، ولا تحيط بعلم الغيب خبرا يقول علما." Used for: Mūsā judges by the ẓāhir and
  the reach of his knowledge (mablagh ʿilmih); al-Khiḍr's acts have no outward
  proof; they begin for causes whose fruit is later (ājila not ʿājila); the
  result is ghayb; khubran = ʿilman. (§4 "The Surface and the Cause".)
- **al-Qurtubi (90)** — NAMED. Clauses: "والأنبياء لا يقرون على منكر، ولا يجوز
  لهم التقرير أي لا يسعك السكوت جريا على عادتك وحكمك" (prophets do not acquiesce
  to a munkar; silence not open to them — §5); "والخبير بالأمور هو العالم
  بخفاياها وبما يختبر منها" (khabīr = knower of hidden matters drawn out by
  testing — §2). Grammar note (tamyīz / maṣdar, Mujāhid) noted but not used.
- **as-Sa'di (91)** — NAMED. Clause: "كيف تصبر على أمر، ما أحطت بباطنه وظاهره ولا
  علمت المقصود منه ومآله" (bāṭin/ẓāhir, maqṣūd, maʾāl). §1, §2, §4.
- **Ibn Kathir ar (14)** — NAMED. Clause: "ولكن ما اطلعت على حكمته ومصلحته الباطنة
  التي اطلعت أنا عليها دونك" (al-Khiḍr knew the wisdom and hidden benefit Mūsā did
  not). §8.
- **al-Baghawi (94)** — NAMED. Clause: "أي علما" (khubran = ʿilman). §2.
- **al-Muyassar (16)** — read, not separately named (its sense — "matters hidden
  from you, from what Allah has taught me" — is covered by Ibn Kathir/at-Tabari).
- **Ibn Kathir en abridged (169)** — NAMED. "given knowledge that He had not
  given Musa, just as He had given Musa knowledge that He had not given
  Al-Khidr"; "hidden interests which I can see but you cannot." §3.
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an en (168)** — NAMED. Used: objections "triggered by the
  dictates of his mission as a prophet"; a promise of patience over the
  apparently-unlawful "not permissible for any 'Alim of Din" except as Allah sent
  Mūsā (§5); Abū Ḥayyān, al-Baḥr al-Muḥīṭ — majority: al-Khiḍr's knowledge =
  inner realities revealed, Mūsā's = rulings on the apparent (§6); Qāḍī
  Thanāʾullāh, Tafsīr Maẓharī — takwīn vs the general law, a servant/act exempted,
  law-scholars right to call it unlawful (§6); the guardrail — non-prophet
  kashf/ilhām may not be analogised to make ḥarām ḥalāl (§7); Ibn ʿAbbās to
  Najda al-Ḥarūrī via Ibn Abī Shayba — same knowledge would make it lawful, but
  it came only by prophetic revelation, ended after Muḥammad ﷺ (§7).

## Prophet-vs-walī status
Deliberately NOT settled (per beat). Ma'arif/Abū Ḥayyān's "majority hold him a
prophet" is reported as their statement, not asserted in article voice; the
takwīn/exemption reasoning is given as the tafsīr's own.
