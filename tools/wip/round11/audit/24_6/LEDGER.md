# SOURCES ledger — 24:6 (liʿān)

## Tafsir fetched (all pre-fetched in PREP.md; none re-fetched)

- **Ibn Kathir (ar, t14)** — NAMED. Arabic clause it rests on: «هذه الآية الكريمة فيها فرج
  للأزواج وزيادة مخرج ... أن يحضرها إلى الإمام ... فيحلفه الحاكم أربع شهادات بالله في
  مقابلة أربعة شهداء». Used for: "relief / a way out" (faraj wa-makhraj); brought before
  the Imam/authority; the judge (al-Hakim) administers the four oaths in place of four
  witnesses (§2.1). Also §4.2 (grace of 24:10) and §5.2 (wrath vs curse) rest on the
  English abridged Ibn Kathir (t169), same author.
- **Ibn Kathir (en abridged, t169)** — NAMED (as Ibn Kathir). Clauses: "This Ayah offers a
  way out for husbands... he brings her before the Imam... The ruler then asks him to
  swear four times"; "The wrath of Allah is mentioned specially in the case of the woman,
  because usually a man would not go to the extent of exposing his wife... unless he is
  telling the truth"; on 24:10 "He has prescribed for them a way out of their
  difficulties." Basis for §4.2, §5.2. Also carries the Ibn ʿAbbas / Hilāl asbāb (see
  hadith) and the ruling that the child is attributed to no father and neither she nor the
  child may be accused (§7.1).
- **at-Tabari (t15)** — NAMED. Clause: «تقوم مقام الشهداء الأربعة في دفع الحدّ عنه» and
  «فحلف أحدهم أربع أيمان بالله». Basis for §3.1 (the four oaths stand in the place of the
  four witnesses in averting the ḥadd; "witness of one of them" = it is upon one of them
  to swear four oaths). at-Tabari also carries the recitation variants (nasb/rafʿ of أربع
  شهادات) and the Hilāl asbāb — recitation point folded into al-Baghawi's citation to
  avoid duplication.
- **al-Qurtubi (t90)** — NAMED. Clauses: «من قذف امرأته وهي كبيرة ... هو لدفع الحد، وهي
  لدرء العذاب» (§5.2 the symmetry — he to avert the ḥadd, she to ward off the ʿadhāb);
  «اللعان أيمان لا شهادات ... قال ابن العربي: والفيصل في أنها يمين لا شهادة أن الزوج يحلف
  لنفسه» and the Mālik/Shāfiʿī vs Abū Ḥanīfa dispute, blind/non-Muslim cases, "كل من صحت
  يمينه صح ... لعانه" (§6.1–6.2, kept as a live disagreement, no position taken); «البداءة
  في اللعان بما بدأ الله به وهو الزوج» and the full form of the oath (§3.3); the two asbāb
  (Hilāl / ʿUwaymir) and «المتلاعنان لا يجتمعان أبدا» + child attributed to mother, not
  reviled (§7.1–7.2).
- **as-Sa'di (t91)** — NAMED. Clause: «سماها شهادة، لأنها نائبة مناب الشهود» and «لأن
  الغالب أن الزوج لا يقدم على رمي زوجته ... إلا إذا كان صادقا ... وخوفا من إلحاق أولاد ليسوا
  منه به». Basis for §3.2 (why the oath is called a "testimony"; the wisdoms special to
  marriage; azwāj = free women, not slaves — the "free" nuance dropped for space, noted
  below).
- **al-Baghawi (t94)** — NAMED. Clause: «قرأ حمزة والكسائي وحفص "أربع شهادات" برفع ... وقرأ
  الآخرون بالنصب أي فشهادة أحدهم أن يشهد». Basis for §3.2 (the two readings of "four
  testimonies": one making the oath what averts the punishment, the other the act to be
  performed — a grammatical, not legal, difference).
- **al-Muyassar (t16)** — NAMED (as al-Muyassar). Clause: «فعلى الواحد منهم أن يشهد أمام
  القاضي أربع مرات ... ويزيد في الشهادة الخامسة الدعوة على نفسه باستحقاقه لعنة الله إن كان
  كاذبًا». Basis for §4.1 (before the qāḍī; the fifth oath a prayer against his own soul).
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en, t168)** — NAMED (as Ma'arif al-Qur'an). Basis for §1.2 (the
  husband's unbearable position — a stranger may keep silent and lose nothing, the husband
  cannot; the general law was never shaped for him) and §7.2 (the two incidents and the
  Ibn Hajar / an-Nawawi reconciliation: Hilāl first and the cause of revelation, ʿUwaymir
  judged by the ruling already given; wording فنزل جبريل vs قد أنزل الله فيك).

## Hadith

- **Sahih al-Bukhari 4747** (Ibn ʿAbbas → Hilāl bin Umayya). URL fetched:
  https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-4747 (also USC-MSA Vol. 6, Book 60,
  Hadith 271). First ten words of the wording used: "Hilal bin Umaiya accused his wife of
  committing illegal…". Grading: Sahih (al-Bukhari's collection; not upgraded).
  Load-bearing phrases used (§2.1, §2.2, §8.1): "Either you bring forth a proof or you will
  receive the legal punishment on your back"; "By Him Who sent you with the Truth, I am
  telling the truth and Allah will reveal…"; "Allah knows that one of you is a liar, so
  will any of you repent?"; and the Prophet ﷺ separated them and ruled the child not be
  attributed to a father nor accused.
- DELIBERATELY OMITTED from the hadith: the physical-description passage ("black-eyed
  child… plump buttocks… thick legs") and "if not for the Book of Allah I would deal with
  her." Reason: the orchestrator's watch-point 3 forbids salacious detail and requires a
  grave, legal tone. This is an editorial trim of one clause, not a source problem; the
  ruling substance (separation, child protection) is preserved.

## Asbāb an-nuzūl

- CONFIRMED and USED, briefly and attributed (§2.2, §7.2). Two incidents in Bukhari/Muslim
  — Hilāl bin Umayya (Bukhari 4747, cause of revelation) and ʿUwaymir al-ʿAjlānī. Presented
  as a disagreement over sequence, reconciled by Ibn Hajar and an-Nawawi (per Ma'arif and
  al-Qurtubi), not as settled fact about which man it was. No salacious detail carried.

## Disagreements kept (no position taken)

- liʿān = oath (yamīn) [Mālik, ash-Shāfiʿī] vs testimony (shahāda) [Abū Ḥanīfa], with the
  practical consequence (blind / non-Muslim / otherwise-inadmissible spouse) and Ibn
  al-ʿArabī's reasoning — §6.1–6.2. Both sides given, verse left to settle neither.
- Recitation variants of أربع شهادات (nasb vs rafʿ) — §3.2, framed as grammatical only.
- Which incident was the sabab (Hilāl vs ʿUwaymir) — §7.2, over sequence not substance.

## Dropped (could not confirm cleanly, or trimmed for the watch-points)

- as-Sa'di's «الحرائر لا المملوكات» (azwāj = free women, not slaves): true to the text but
  a fiqh side-branch that would need its own explanation; dropped for space.
- al-Qurtubi's long fiqh chain (istibrāʾ, ʿidda, nafy al-ḥaml timing, deaf/mute liʿān,
  Abū Ḥanīfa on pregnancy): dropped; not needed for the reflection and would bury the
  balance.
- The child's later life ("governor of Egypt", ʿIkrima): true in Ibn Kathir but tangential
  and near the omitted salacious material; dropped.
- The physical-marks passage in Bukhari 4747: dropped for decency (see hadith note).

## "Licenses nothing" sentence

Carried and sharpened in §8.1, both languages: the verse lays down a COURT oath-procedure
before lawful authority; it licenses no private accusation, no public shaming, no violence,
no hand raised on suspicion, against any living person or community.
