Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the liʿān procedure. Frames it as a
merciful judicial ordinance administered before the authority/court, not private
action: it resolves the one evidentiary bind 24:4 leaves open — a spouse's
accusation with no four witnesses — through solemn oaths before God rather than
violence or an unprovable charge.

Balanced 24:6 with 24:8-9 throughout. The husband's four oaths avert the ḥadd of
false accusation from him; the wife's four counter-oaths avert the punishment
from her. al-Qurtubi's symmetry read whole (he li-dafʿ al-ḥadd, she li-dar'
al-ʿadhāb); the wife is not presented as guilty — her sworn word is set exactly
against his and, once sworn, lifts the worldly penalty entirely and leaves the
presumption in her favour.

Sources (all pre-fetched, none from memory): Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged) for
faraj/makhraj and being brought before the Imam; at-Tabari for the four oaths
standing in the place of the four witnesses in averting the ḥadd; as-Sa'di for
why an oath is called a "testimony"; al-Baghawi and at-Tabari for the recitation
variants (grammatical, not legal); al-Muyassar for the fifth oath; al-Qurtubi for
the form of the liʿān and the oath-vs-testimony dispute; Ma'arif al-Qur'an for the
husband's unbearable position and the asbāb reconciliation.

Disagreements kept, no position taken: liʿān as oath (Mālik, ash-Shāfiʿī) vs
testimony (Abū Ḥanīfa) with Ibn al-ʿArabī's reasoning; recitation variants; which
incident was the sabab (Hilāl bin Umayya vs ʿUwaymir al-ʿAjlānī), reconciled by
Ibn Hajar and an-Nawawi over sequence not substance.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4747 (Ibn ʿAbbas → Hilāl), grading Sahih, unchanged;
the physical-description passage omitted for decency and a grave, legal tone.

"Licenses nothing against any living person or community" sentence carried and
sharpened: a court oath-procedure before lawful authority — no private
accusation, public shaming, violence, or hand raised on suspicion.

Dropped (in ledger): as-Sa'di's free-vs-slave nuance, al-Qurtubi's long fiqh
chain, the child's later life, the Bukhari physical-marks clause.
