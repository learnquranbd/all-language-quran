Card plus deep bilingual article on the Prophet's ﷺ marital permissions.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, as-Saʿdi, al-Muyassar,
  Ma'arif al-Qur'an, all on 33:50.
- ujūr read as mahr (Mujahid, via Tabari/Ibn Kathir); the "wives whose dues
  you gave" are the existing wives, affirmed not newly sought.
- mā malakat yamīnuk framed in its own century: Ibn Kathir's examples
  (Safiyyah, Juwayriyah freed then married) show the trajectory toward
  manumission and lawful marriage; explicit disclaimer that it licenses
  nothing against any living person.
- khāliṣatan laka stated plainly as unique to the Prophet ﷺ (Ma'arif, Ibn
  Kathir via Ikrimah/Qatadah, as-Saʿdi); the believers' own rule (no more
  than four, with walī, witnesses, dower; 4:3) kept in view.
- Wisdom frame: marriages as burdens of prophethood (alliance, the Mothers
  of the Believers, care of widows); restraint noted (33:52 restricts him
  further; Qurtubi's khaṣāʾiṣ list). Required sentence included in both
  languages: unique station, no template for men today, no demeaning of women.

Disagreements kept (no position): scope of allātī hājarna maʿaka (hijrah to
Medina vs. Islam — Qurtubi; as-Saʿdi; Baghawi's abrogation report); whether
the gifting clause was ever acted upon (Ibn ʿAbbas/Mujahid: never; others name
Maymunah/Umm Sharik/Zaynab bint Khuzaymah/Khawla — Zamakhshari counts four).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 5113 (ʿĀʾishah; Khawla bint Hakim; the 33:51 verse),
quoted whole, graded as in the Sahih.

Dropped: the Umm Hani' proposal report (Ibn al-ʿArabi grades it very weak in
Qurtubi), so no sabab asserted; Ibn Kathir's exact mahr figures; any single
woman named as "the" one who offered herself.
