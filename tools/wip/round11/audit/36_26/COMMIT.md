Card and deep bilingual article for the believer's wish after
his people kill him: "Enter Paradise ... would that my people knew"
(36:26-27).

The centre: even inside the reward, his first thought is for his own
people, the ones who killed him, that they might know the mercy he
found and so believe. Sincere good-will (nasiha) toward one's
persecutors, surviving death.

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- at-Tabari: Allah said "Enter" the moment they killed him; the wish
  = that they know the cause (faith + patience to death) and believe.
- as-Sa'di, al-Muyassar: entry "in the moment," spoken as an honour;
  advising his people after death as in life.
- Ibn Kathir (ar/en): Ibn Mas'ud (entered alive, provided for),
  Mujahid (wajabat lahu l-janna; names Habib an-Najjar), Qatada
  (the believer is always a sincere adviser), Ibn 'Abbas (sincere in
  life at 36:20 and after death), Abu Mijlaz on 36:27.
- al-Qurtubi: al-Hasan via al-Qushayri (raised before death), the
  sawing report, bima grammar, "wajabat lak" reading, Qatada tying
  to 3:169; his own plain view kept.
- Ma'arif: angel address, barzakh "virtual entry," martyrdom hint.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3477 (also Muslim) — a prophet beaten
bloody praying "forgive my people, for they know not"; same root
('-l-m) as "would that my people knew." Cross-lit to 18:6.

Kept as disagreement: the manner/mode of his entry and death
(trampled / sawed / raised before death) left as the reports leave it.

Dropped: Ma'arif's biography (leper, Wahb b. Munabbih, the
"600 years" and Waraqah material) as unneeded isra'iliyyat;
Ibn Kathir's Antioch/disciples discussion as off the wish.
Stated plainly in both languages that 36:28-29 licenses nothing
against any living person or community; the believer sought their
guidance, not revenge.
