Adds the Tadabbur card and deep bilingual article for 23:103, read as the
losing half of the pair with 23:102: heavy scales = the successful, light
scales = those who lost their very selves, abiding in Hell.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir: a light scale means the bad deeds outweigh the good.
- as-Sa'di: the sins have encompassed the good; al-Muyassar: the heaviest such
  sin is shirk.
- al-Qurtubi: mawazin is a real Balance; the denier's deed is set in the pan in
  its ugliest form and, being light, drops him into the Fire.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: for the denier the good-deeds pan is empty (18:105); it
  gathers three reported ways deeds are weighed (the person, the scrolls, the
  solidified deeds) and holds there is no conflict between them.
- at-Tabari on khasiru anfusahum: they cheated their souls of their share of
  Allah's mercy; Ibn Kathir: the losing bargain. as-Sa'di: every other loss is
  easy beside this one, the loss of the noble self and of nearness to the Lord.

Disagreement kept: al-Qurtubi's report of Ibn Abbas that deeds are created as
weighable substances, and Ibn Furak's rejection of it — stated as unsettled.

Scope guarded per as-Sa'di and Ma'arif: the eternal abiding falls on the denier
whose sins encompass all good; the believer with the root of faith who sinned
is purified and not left forever (9:102). The verse names an eschatological
category, not living people, and licenses nothing against any person or community.

Hadith: Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2639, the bitaqa — the card of the shahada outweighs
99 scrolls of sin, "nothing is heavier than the Name of Allah." Graded hasan
gharib by at-Tirmidhi, reported as-is.

Dropped: Ibn Kathir's angel-over-the-Balance report (he graded its isnad weak);
al-Qurtubi's salawat-bitaqa and the Hudhayfa and Adam reports (grading not
established on the fetched page).
