Card and deep bilingual article on household privacy: servants and
under-age children seek leave at three ʿawrāt — before fajr, the midday
rest, and after ʿishāʾ — with free coming-and-going the rest of the day.

From the sources:
- at-Tabari & al-Qurtubi: the earlier verse (24:27) is general, this one
  particular; two readings of "your right hands possess" (Ibn Umar: males;
  others incl. Tabari's tarjih: men and women).
- Two qirāʾāt of "ثلاث عورات" — rafʿ (Madina/Basra) vs naṣb (Kufa: Hamza,
  Kisāʾī, Abu Bakr); Tabari holds both sound, Farrāʾ preferred rafʿ.
- as-Sa'di & Ibn Kathir & Muyassar: why each hour (undress/rest/sleep);
  ṭawwāfūn = constant need, so the rule is targeted not burdensome.
- al-Baghawi on "lam yablughū al-ḥulum": the young who understand such
  matters, not infants.
- Ma'arif: household vs visitor isti'dhan; adult male slave is non-mahram,
  so the phrase = slave-girls and minor slaves; the burden to teach falls
  on adults (pray-at-seven analogy).

Disagreement kept (no verdict): the ʿamal/naskh debate — some held it
abrogated; Abu Qilaba recommendation; majority (al-Qāsim, Jābir b. Zayd,
ash-Shaʿbī) firm and binding. ash-Shaʿbī and Saʿīd b. Jubayr: not
abrogated, only neglected. Ibn ʿAbbās (isnād ṣaḥīḥ per Ibn Kathir): the
satr/curtains reasoning, yet named it among three neglected verses and
still had his servant-girl ask leave.

Hadith: the cat hadith, Tirmidhi 92 (Kabsha bint Kaʿb / Abu Qatāda),
quoted whole, graded by Tirmidhi's own remark "the soundest in its
chapter" — not upgraded. Cited because Ibn Kathir ties ṭawwāfūn to it.

Asbāb: two accounts given side by side, no ranking — Mudlij→ʿUmar
(Ibn ʿAbbās, via Baghawi/Qurtubi) and Asmāʾ bint Marthad (Muqātil).

Sober-tone note shipped in both languages: the verse regulates a
household of its era and licenses nothing against any living person;
its living aim is guarding the rest and dignity of all under one roof.

Dropped: Qurtubi's "makkiyya" slip; as-Suddi's intimacy report and
Tafsir Mazhari's makruh-tanzihi graft (tone); Qurtubi's ʿishāʾ-naming
and salāt-faḍāʾil digressions (off-spine).
