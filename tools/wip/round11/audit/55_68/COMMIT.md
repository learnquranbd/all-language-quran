Card and 8-section bilingual article (1,587 EN words) on 55:68: fihima
fakihatun wa nakhlun wa rummanun. Framed in the lower pair with one
pointer to 55:62; 55:46-47 and 55:60 as pointers only; 55:70-76 untouched.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Kathir (ar; en abridged
grouped 55:62-71, used only for its 55:52 comparison), as-Sa'di,
al-Muyassar. Ma'arif (group 55:64-69) speaks only to 55:64; unused.
- Kept as a disagreement: palms and pomegranates not fakiha ("some",
  per Tabari, Qurtubi, Baghawi; Qurtubi: the zahir, a thing is not
  joined to itself) vs fakiha named for their merit (Qurtubi's jumhur,
  Baghawi's 'amma, Tabari's "others": the Arabs count them fruit;
  2:238, 2:98, 22:18 as parallels). Tabari states no preference.
- Qurtubi's two "it is said" reasons (wheat-like staple of the Hijaz to
  Yemen; fruit+food / fruit+remedy, not purely tafakkuh) and his second
  question: Abu Hanifa, oath against fakiha not broken by pomegranate or
  rutab; his two companions and the people differed. No fiqh verdict.
- Ibn Kathir: specific after general "as al-Bukhari and others
  established", singled out for nobility; 55:52 wider, fakiha indefinite
  in an affirmative clause does not generalise. Sa'di: the most special
  of the fruits.
Hadith: none. Ibn Kathir's Abd ibn Humayd (Umar) and Ibn Abi Hatim
(Abu Sa'id) reports ungraded in the fetched text and unconfirmed on
quranx; named, not quoted. Palm-of-Paradise sayings (Ibn Abbas, Sa'id
ibn Jubayr, Wahb, Abu Ubayda) named as non-prophetic, details not retold.
Asbab: none.
Dropped: names of Abu Hanifa's companions, any nutrition claim, Ibn
Kathir (en) material on 55:70-76.
