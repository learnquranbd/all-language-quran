Tadabbur 26:208 is the capstone of the surah: after seven
destroyed peoples (Nuh, Hud, Salih, Lut, Shuayb, Musa/Pharaoh,
Ibrahim's people), one line declares God's justice — no town is
destroyed but that it had warners first.

Sources (full consensus on the ʿadl reading):
- Ibn Kathir (ar+en): destruction only after iʿdhār, warning,
  messengers, and establishing the ḥujja; his cross-refs 17:15, 28:59.
- at-Tabari: "these very towns described across these surahs"; ذكرى
  as reminder for najāt, with his two iʿrāb readings kept as a
  grammatical note, not a dispute.
- al-Qurtubi: min is extra, munḏirūn = rusul. al-Baghawi: the same.
- as-Sa'di: the perfection of justice — warners call to guidance,
  forbid ruin, remind of God's days of blessing and retribution.
- al-Muyassar (208–209): 26:209's "never unjust" = never punish
  before sending a messenger.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 7283 (Abu Musa), the naked warner (al-nadhīr
al-ʿuryān) who sees the army — obey and be saved, deny and be
destroyed (fa-ahlakahum, the verse's verb). Collector's own grade.

Tadabbur: warning is itself a mercy; God does not strike the
unreached; to be warned is a gift, not a threat. §6 states plainly in
both languages that the destroyed towns license nothing against any
living person or community.

Dropped: the "dipped in the Fire" hadith (attached to 26:207, not
208); no asbāb (concluding maxim — placement written instead).

Card: themes mercy + guidance. Article 1648 EN words, 8 sections.
