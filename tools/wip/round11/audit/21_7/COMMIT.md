Card and deep bilingual article for 21:7 (al-Anbiyāʾ).

Spine: the mortality and humanity of the prophets as the surah's own
answer to the deniers' whispered sneer in 21:3 ("is this but a man like
you?") and their sign-demand in 21:5. Every messenger before was a human
man, never an angel; the objection is no objection.

Sources (all confirmed against fetched tafsir):
- Ibn Kathir (14/169): all prior messengers were human men, no angel;
  cross-refs 12:109, 64:6; 21:8 bodies that ate, none immortal (25:20,
  21:34).
- at-Tabari (15): men like their nations, not angels; ahl adh-dhikr =
  people of Torah/Gospel via Qatada; also Ibn Zayd (dhikr=Qur'an) and the
  Ali report.
- al-Qurtubi (90): reply to "hal hadha illa bashar"; why ahl adh-dhikr
  kept the prophets' record; Quraysh consulting the People of the Book.
- al-Baghawi (94): no angels sent to the ancients; even deniers of the
  Prophet won't deny the messengers were human.
- as-Sa'di (91): the ilzām — they already affirm Ibrahim (AS) as a human
  prophet, so the argument refutes itself; the angel-demand answered.
- al-Muyassar (16), Ma'arif (168): ahl adh-dhikr as scholars of the
  earlier books; may include all Jews and Christians.

Disagreement kept: ahl adh-dhikr = people of earlier scripture vs ahl
al-Qur'an (Ibn Zayd / Ali), left side by side.

No hadith: no sound marfū' narration is attached to this verse in the
sources; stated plainly rather than filled with a weak one. The Ali
"we are the people of the Reminder" report is via a chain later critics
questioned — passed on as a saying, not proof.

Dropped: Sa'di's "no woman was a prophet" inference (confirmable but
omitted for focus, off the mortality spine).

Differentiated from the near-verbatim 16:43 (taqlid/ask-the-scholars
angle): distinct opening, al-Anbiyāʾ context throughout, all 8 headings
new in both languages.
