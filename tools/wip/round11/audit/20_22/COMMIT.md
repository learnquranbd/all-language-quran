The second sign given to Mūsā: draw the hand to the side and it
comes out white "min ghayri sūʾ" — without disease — another sign
beside the staff.

Sources gave:
- janāḥ as the side / under the upper arm / armpit: Mujāhid "palm
  under the upper arm" (Tabari, Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi), al-Baghawi
  "the armpit", Ma'arif "under the armpit" per Ibn ʿAbbās (Mazharī).
  al-Qurtubi's spread on ilā (under / jayb / side / with) kept.
- min ghayri sūʾ = min ghayri baraṣ, without leprosy: unanimous,
  Tabari stacking six chains, al-Baghawi "sūʾ here means baraṣ",
  Ibn Kathir "no leprosy, harm or defect". Guarded so the whiteness
  is not misread as illness.
- The whiteness as radiant light, not pallor: al-Qurtubi/al-Baghawi
  per Ibn ʿAbbās "like the sun and moon and brighter", Ibn Kathir
  "like a piece of the moon", al-Hasan al-Basrī "as if a lamp; from
  this Mūsā knew he had met his Lord".
- Tabari via Wahb b. Munabbih: Mūsā dark-skinned, hand out white
  like snow then back to his colour — a wonder shown and withdrawn.
- āyatan ukhrā as a proof beside the staff (Baghawi, Qurtubi);
  as-Saʿdī and Ibn Kathir set the count at two via 28:32.

Disagreement kept: Abū Hurayra and Kaʿb (two wings = two hands) vs
the Arabic linguists (the two sides), Tabari forcing no verdict.

Hadith: none. No sound marfūʿ report attaches to the verse; the
citations are Companion/Successor athar. Article says the slot is
empty rather than filling it. No asbab; placement given instead.
20:23–24 left as future ground, only pointed toward.
