Card and deep bilingual article on Sulayman's leased dominion: the
wind (a month's course each half-day, two months in a day), the spring
of molten copper, and the jinn labouring before him, every element
tied to bi-idhni rabbih — power gifted and held on loan, not seized.

Sources: at-Tabari (naṣb reading preferred by consensus; a month each
way; qiṭr = melted copper made to run), al-Qurtubi (rafʿ reading leaves
the meaning intact since only Allah subjects the wind; al-Khalil: qiṭr
is molten copper), Ibn Kathir (al-Hasan's Damascus–Istakhr–Kabul route;
Ibn ʿAbbas, Mujahid, Qatadah, as-Suddi: qiṭr = nuhas), al-Baghawi and
as-Sa'di (jinn subdued, unable to refuse his order), al-Muyassar, and
Ma'arif al-Qur'an (bayna yadayhi = vassals before him, subjection by
leave not sorcery; fire burns the fire-made jinn as a stone bruises
dust-made man).

Disagreements kept open: the copper's "three days" (as-Suddi/al-Baghawi)
against al-Qushayri's doubt that it is a narrator's slip; and the Blaze
as the Hereafter (most mufassirun) versus this world (an angel with a
fiery lash, per al-Baghawi) — both stated, no position taken.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 461 (Abu Hurayra) — the ʿifrit caught in the
night and freed for the sake of Sulayman's unique kingdom (38:35);
grading left as al-Bukhari's own, nothing stretched.

Section 7 keeps the isra'iliyyat at arm's length: the flying throne, the
army-bearing vessel and the Tigris inscription are named only as lore
the mufassirun relay without endorsing. Dropped: the horse-sacrifice
recompense (belongs to 38:30-40), the staff/Temple material (34:13-14),
and Ibn Kathir's gharib "three kinds of jinn" reports.

No asbab claimed; verse read in its Dawud–Sulayman placement.
