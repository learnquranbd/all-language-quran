Card plus deep bilingual article on 33:18, during the Battle of the
Trench: Allah already knows the hinderers (al-muʿawwiqīn) and those who
whisper to their brothers "come over to us," who come to the fight only
a little.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar and
Ibn Kathir on 33:18; Ma'arif al-Qur'an and Ibn Kathir (en) for placement
and the 33:19 continuation.

Lexical core: muʿawwiqūn as those who hold others back and sap resolve
(al-Qurtubi's root ʿāqa = to divert; Baghawi/Muyassar's muthabbiṭīn;
Sa'di's mukhadhilīn); al-Qurtubi's note that "halumma" is the Hijazi
form meaning aqbil, come forward; baʾs = war, attended only as taʿdhīr,
a token appearance for show (riyāʾ, fear of disgrace). Baghawi's line
that the same little, done for Allah, would have been much.

Disagreement kept (no position): who the callers are — the munāfiqūn
(Qatada; Muqatil names Ibn Ubayy), the Jews of Banu Qurayza calling the
hypocrites, or Ibn Zayd's single two-brother scene where Jibril brought
the verse before the man could report it.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 33 (signs of a hypocrite are three), confirmed
on quranx with Bukhari's grading, and marked as a general narration not
attached to this verse — no tafsir fetched attaches a sound one.

Sensitivity: states in both languages that the verse describes the
behaviour in its siege setting, leaves the verdict to Allah (33:24), and
licenses nothing against any living person or group, nor any takfīr; read
as a mirror for the reader's own resolve.
