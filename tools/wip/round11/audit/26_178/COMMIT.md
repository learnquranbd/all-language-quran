Opens the Shuʿayb / Aṣḥāb al-Aykah passage (26:176-177). The credential
is أمين — the messenger's trustworthiness offered as the first evidence of
the message, and as the ground of the call that follows.

Sources (all fetched for this verse or its stated group):
- at-Tabari: amīn = trustworthy over the revelation he carries (ʿalā waḥyih).
- al-Qurtubi: ṣādiq, truthful in what he conveys from Allah.
- al-Muyassar: a keeper/ḥafīẓ over the message, neither adding nor dropping;
  al-Aykah = land of tangled trees; denying one messenger = denying all.
- as-Sa'di: from the credential follows "fear Allah and obey" — the order is
  the argument. (26:179 content left for that verse.)
- Ibn Kathir: the omission of "their brother" (vs Nūḥ/Hūd/Ṣāliḥ) because the
  people's name proclaimed the tree-idol they worshipped.

Disagreement kept with both sides, no position taken: whether Aṣḥāb al-Aykah
and the people of Madyan are one nation (Ibn Kathir's "most correct view,"
Isḥāq ibn Bishr, the report other than Juwaybir's) or distinct (those who read
Shuʿayb as sent to two or three nations). Ibn Kathir's conclusion attributed
to him, with "and Allah knows best."

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4770 (Safa gathering — "we have not found you telling
anything other than the truth"), quoted whole, graded sound, and flagged as
tied to a later verse, not a commentary on 26:178.

A condemned people (26:172-173): states plainly in both languages that the
passage licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Dropped: al-Baghawi (no gloss) and Ma'arif (its 26:173 Lut comment, off-target);
no sabab used; measure-and-justice ethic held for 26:181-183.
