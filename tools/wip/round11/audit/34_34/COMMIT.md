Card plus deep bilingual article on the recurring pattern the verse names:
whenever a warner (nadhīr) entered a town, its affluent (mutrafūn) led the
rejection. The article keeps to that sociological-spiritual pattern — ease
breeds the arrogance and attachment that resist a call to accountability.

Sources (all pre-fetched, confirmed before citing):
- Lexis of mutraf from taraf: Ma'arif (life of ease/comfort; the rich grown
  arrogant and careless), al-Muyassar ("immersed in pleasures and desires"),
  Ibn Kathir ("comfort, pomp, wealth, leadership"), al-Baghawi / al-Qurtubi
  ("chiefs and rich"), Qatadah ("leaders and heads in evil").
- Core mechanism from as-Sa'di: their comfort made them insolent and they
  boasted of it (bounty → insolence → boasting).
- at-Tabari on the warner and "great ones in misguidance."
- Cross-light as a consistent type (Ibn Kathir's own list): 26:111, 7:75-76,
  17:16; plus same-surah close-up 34:31-32.
- Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari no. 7 — Heraclius asks Abu Sufyan whether nobles or
  the poor follow the Prophet; the poor do, "the followers of the messengers."
  Ibn Kathir attaches this scene to the verse.

Balance/licensing sentence included (as with 28:76 Qārūn): the verse condemns
the mutraf attitude — arrogance, heedless luxury, resistance to truth — not
wealth itself; honestly earned, rightly spent wealth is not blamed, and this is
no warrant to despise the rich as a class. Anchored on 34:37 and the "Allah
looks at hearts, not forms/wealth" hadith (cited via Ibn Kathir from Muslim).
