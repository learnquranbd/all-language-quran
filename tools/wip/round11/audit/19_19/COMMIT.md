Card and deep bilingual article for 19:19: the angel tells Maryam
"I am only the messenger of your Lord, to give you a pure boy."

Sources gave a rich qirāʾa disagreement, kept as a disagreement:
- at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir (ar) set out li-ahaba ("that I may give,"
  the messenger relating his errand) against Abu Amr's li-yahaba
  ("that HE, your Lord, may give"). Ibn Kathir: both sound, each
  entails the other. at-Tabari prefers li-ahaba on the mushaf, without
  faulting the other reading.
- al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi both land the meaning in the same place:
  the giving is Allah's even when the verb is ascribed to the messenger,
  because He sent him with it. Ma'arif adds the breath as the means,
  the gift itself Allah's act.

Tawhid frame kept clear without polemic: a boy who must be given, whose
purity is granted, whose coming a messenger announces, is a servant, not
a Creator — the surah's quiet answer to the sonship claim. Did not
preempt 19:20-21; did not quote 19:35.

zakiyy read from at-Tabari (kept from sins), as-Sa'di (freed of blame,
adorned with good), al-Muyassar, al-Baghawi; 19:13 parallel (Yaḥyā's
purity) noted as a plain textual echo.

Hadith slot handled honestly: no tafsir attaches a verse-specific sound
hadith to 19:19, and §7 says so. Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (the youth raised
worshipping his Lord) is offered only as a marked general narration, not
as tafsir. Grading reported as Sahih, not upgraded.

Dropped: Ibn Kathir's "five Great Messengers" count; the "it is said"
report of Jibril's return to angelic form kept but marked as reported.
