How the tafsirs tie 22:7 to 22:5-6: Ibn Kathir (en) frames 22:5 as two proofs (creation of man, revived earth) for the resurrection, glosses 22:6, then 22:7; al-Qurtubi: 22:7 joined to "dhalika bi-anna" in wording only, with "wa li-ya'lamu" understood; at-Tabari: "wa li-tuqinu bi-dhalika". No tafsir links 22:1-2; not used.

## Glosses on the watch-point phrases
- la rayba fiha: Ibn Kathir (لا شك فيها ولا مرية), at-Tabari (لا شك في مجيئها وحدوثها), al-Qurtubi (لا شك), Muyassar (لا شك في ذلك), as-Sa'di (لا وجه لاستبعادها). "Statement, not a command": no tafsir says this; the article states the form as a textual observation (unattributed) and reports at-Tabari's drawn-out address "fa-la tashukku".
- yab'athu man fil-qubur: Ibn Kathir ar/en (restores / brings into being after nonexistence), at-Tabari (alive to the standing place of reckoning), Muyassar (for reckoning and recompense), al-Qurtubi (for reward and punishment), as-Sa'di (repays your deeds good and bad). "man for the rational dead": NO fetched tafsir comments on "man"; dropped. Glossed as al-mawta (Muyassar) / man fiha min al-amwat (at-Tabari); the article does not attribute these as comments on "man".

## Found wrong in the shipped article
- It says 22:7 "carries the same particle forward" so that all five clauses hang on dhalika bi-anna as reasons. Al-Qurtubi says the conjunction is in wording only: the Hour is not a cause of what 22:5 describes, so "wa li-ya'lamu" must be understood. The new article states this.
