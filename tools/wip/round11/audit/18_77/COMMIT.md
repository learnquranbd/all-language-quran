Card and bilingual article on the wall incident: the town that refused
Mūsā and al-Khiḍr hospitality, al-Khiḍr's free repair of the falling wall,
and Mūsā's third remark that he could have taken a wage.

Sources (fetched, confirmed clause by clause):
- at-Tabari: "yurīdu an yanqaḍḍa" = wants to fall, same root as a star's
  plunge; two readings of the wage (so they feed us / pay for the labor);
  town named al-Ayla.
- al-Qurtubi: the wall's "will" as majāz in the Qur'an (majority view),
  with the dissent of al-Isfarāyīnī and Ibn Dāwūd al-Asbahānī kept as a
  disagreement; ijāra lawful, the way of prophets; Mūsā's line as an offer
  (ʿarḍ), not the sharp objection of before; Ibn ʿAbbās — ship/boy were for
  Allah, the wall word was for himself, and that parted them; Qatāda on the
  worst town; Wahb's hundred-cubit detail, recorded without leaning on it.
- Ibn Kathir / as-Sa'di / al-Baghawi / Muyassar: refusal of hospitality,
  fa-aqāmahu, and repair variants (wiped by hand / rebuilt / clay-laid).
- Town identity left open (al-Ubulla / Antioch / al-Andalus / al-Ayla).

Hadith: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3401 (Ubayy via Ibn ʿAbbās), wall/wages portion
quoted, grading sound (not upgraded), closing with the Prophet's ﷺ wish
that Mūsā had been patient.

Held to the beat: the tension of unrewarded service vs Mūsā's human logic.
The explanation (orphans, treasure, righteous father) is 18:82 and was
deliberately withheld. Stated in both languages that the verse describes
one town's conduct and licenses nothing against any living people or place.
Dropped: the Berber-woman/curse report and the qirāʼāt variant (in ledger).
