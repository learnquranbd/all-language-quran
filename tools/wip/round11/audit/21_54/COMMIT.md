Ibrāhīm answers his people's appeal to their fathers (21:53) by naming
both generations in one verdict: "You were certainly, you and your
fathers, in manifest error."

Sources (all fetched, confirmed against the Arabic clause):
- at-Tabari: ḍalāl = going-away from the path of truth; mubīn = plain to
  whoever weighs it with reason.
- al-Muyassar / al-Baghawi: a clear distance from the truth, an obvious
  mistake in worshipping the idols.
- al-Qurtubi: worship of them is loss (khusrān) — inanimate things that
  neither benefit nor harm nor know.
- as-Sa'di: declares all of them misguided together; no error more
  extreme than shirk; what they said is not fit to be clung to.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en): answering the fathers = answering you, both off
  the straight path; the stand rests on the rushd given early (21:51)
  and the proof of 6:83.

Lesson: age, numbers and family feeling are not evidence; the test is
the truth of the thing, and saying so takes courage (taqlīd challenged).

Hadith: none — no sound narration attaches directly to 21:54; stated in
one sentence. Ma'arif al-Qur'an fetched but its text covers only 21:48,
so not named. No asbāb (placement, not occasion). No disagreement of
substance; emphases reported as complementary.

Licensing: the condemned stance is idol-worship, described as error, not
a charge against any living person or community; no verdict today. Does
not preempt 21:55–56.
