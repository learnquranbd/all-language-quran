# SOURCES ledger — 21:54

Verse: Ibrāhīm to his people — "You were certainly, you and your fathers, in manifest error" (qāla laqad kuntum antum wa-ābāʾukum fī ḍalālin mubīn).

## Tafsir fetched (all from PREP, none re-fetched)

- **Ibn Kathir (ar, 14)** — NAMED. Clause: «الكلام مع آبائكم الذين احتججتم بصنيعهم كالكلام معكم، فأنتم وهم في ضلال على غير الطريق المستقيم». Also «فلما سفه أحلامهم، وضلل آباءهم، واحتقر آلهتهم» → §1.2 (called their reasoning foolish, declared fathers misguided, belittled their gods) and §3.2 (answering the fathers = answering you; both astray, off the straight path).
- **at-Tabari (15)** — NAMED. Clause: «في ذهاب عن سبيل الحقّ، وجور عن قصد السبيل» and «مبين: بين لمن تأمله بعقل» → §2.1 (ḍalāl = going-away from the path of truth / swerving; mubīn = plain to whoever considers it with reason).
- **al-Muyassar (16)** — NAMED. Clause: «في بُعْد واضح بيِّن عن الحق» → §2.2 (clear, evident distance from the truth in worshipping the idols).
- **al-Qurtubi (90)** — NAMED. Clause: «في خسران بعبادتها؛ إذ هي جمادات لا تنفع ولا تضر ولا تعلم» → §4.1–4.2 (worship of them is loss/khusrān; inanimate things that neither benefit nor harm nor know).
- **as-Sa'di (91)** — NAMED. Clauses: «مضللا للجميع … وأي ضلال، أبلغ من ضلالهم في الشرك، وترك التوحيد» and «فليس ما قلتم، يصلح للتمسك به، وقد اشتركتم وإياهم في الضلال الواضح» → §3.1 (declares all misguided together; no error more extreme than shirk) and §5.1 (what you said is not fit to be clung to; you share the same plain error).
- **al-Baghawi (94)** — NAMED. Clause: «خطأ بين بعبادتكم إياها» → §2.2 (an obvious mistake in worshipping them).
- **Ibn Kathir (en abridged, 169)** — NAMED (English confirmation of the ar). Supplies: guidance given to Ibrāhīm from an early age, cross-ref to 6:83, denunciation of idols as that early guidance, "Both you and they are misguided and are not following any straight path." → §1.1, §1.2, §6.1.
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en, 168)** — FETCHED, NOT NAMED on 21:54. The grouped text discusses only 21:48 (furqān / light / reminder given to Mūsā), nothing verse-specific to 21:54. Dropped to avoid attributing a 54-reading to it.

## Hadith
- None used. Suggested Bukhari 660 (fetched via hadith.js, quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-660) is the "seven whom Allah will shade" narration — unrelated to 21:54. No sound hadith attaches directly to this verse; stated in one sentence in §6.1 ("No sound hadith is narrated directly on this verse; the reading rests on the passage itself and that parallel").

## Asbab an-nuzul
- None. Not used. 21:54 sits inside the Ibrāhīm narrative (21:51–56); its placement, not an occasion of revelation, is what the article uses.

## Cross-references used
- 21:53 (their appeal: "we found our fathers worshippers of them") — the taunt this verse answers.
- 21:52 (Ibrāhīm's opening question about the statues) — §4.2.
- 21:51 ("We gave Ibrāhīm his sound judgement before") — §6.1, source of the God-given rushd.
- 6:83 (the proof God gave Ibrāhīm over his people) — via Ibn Kathir, §1.2 and §6.1.

## Disagreements
- None substantive. The six mufassirūn fetched agree on ḍalāl mubīn = plain/evident error of idol-worship; they differ only in emphasis (Tabari on deviation from the path, Qurtubi on the idols' inanimacy/khusrān, Sa'di on shirk as the extreme case). Reported as complementary emphases, no position taken.

## Dropped / softened
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an — dropped as a 21:54 source (its text is on 21:48 only); noted above.
- No claim about *which* idols/statues, their number, or any narrative embellishment: not in the fetched texts, so omitted.

## Licensing
- Standing sentence included in §7 (both languages): the verse describes the idol-worship of that people and their forefathers as manifest error, licenses nothing against any living person or community, is a judgement on a stance not a charge against anyone today, and no verdict any of us is here to pass. §7.2 keeps the object as the error, not the person.

## Watch-points
- Did NOT preempt 21:55–56: §7.2 notes only that "the very next exchange still has him speaking with them" without stating their question or his affirmation of the Lord of the heavens and earth.
