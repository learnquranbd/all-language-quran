## Disagreements kept (no position)
- Place of the sajda: 41:37 ta'budun (Malik; 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud) vs 41:38 yas'amun (Ibn Wahb, ash-Shafi'i, Abu Hanifa; Ibn 'Abbas; Ibn 'Umar "the later"; Abu Wa'il, Qatada, Bakr), all via al-Qurtubi; Ibn al-'Arabi "al-amr qarib". Article notes the displayed mushaf sets ۩ at end of 41:38 (from PREP text) and says it takes no side.
- Referent of -hunna: four signs / sun and moon only / meaning of ayat (Qurtubi); Tabari: the four. Form: broken plural, no taghlib (Qurtubi, Baghawi); non-human plural takes feminine (Tabari).

## Hadith
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1041 (Dar-us-Salam), https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-1041 (saved: hadith_Bukhari_1041.txt). Abu Mas'ud. First ten words (translated from the Arabic): "The sun and the moon do not eclipse for the death". Quoted whole, Bukhari wording only. Grading: none beyond inclusion in his Sahih; stated so. Marked in the article as a general narration that no fetched tafsir attaches to this verse. Link to the verse is Qurtubi's Ibn Khuwayz Mindad note (eclipse prayer), attributed.
- Also fetched (not used): Bukhari 1042 (Ibn 'Umar), 1043 (al-Mughira; lacks "two signs").
- Sentence in article: no fetched tafsir attaches a particular hadith to this verse.

## Who prostrated to sun and moon / licensing
- No fetched tafsir names a group; Tabari reads the address as "ayyuha an-nas". Said so; licensing sentence in both languages (section 6). No religion or community named.

## Dropped / not used
- Rhetorical point that fronting iyyahu gives exclusivity: not in fetched text; not stated (Sa'di/Tabari's "alone/sincerity" used instead).
- Named sun- or moon-worshipping peoples: no fetched text; not stated.
- Details of the eclipse prayer's manner: Qurtubi only says it is disputed; not described.
- 41:38-39 content (angels' glorification, earth revived): only 41:38's closing words, for the sajda dispute.
- The names Abu Salih, Yahya b. Wathab, Talha and Zubayd, as-Sulami: covered by "and others he names".
- Ibn Kathir (en) 6:89 cross-reference (on 41:38): not used.
