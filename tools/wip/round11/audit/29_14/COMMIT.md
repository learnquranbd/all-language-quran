Card plus deep bilingual article on Nūḥ among his people: he
remained "a thousand years minus fifty" (950), then the flood
seized them while they were wrongdoers.

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- Placement/consolation after 29:12-13: at-Tabari (respite yet ruin
  for the deniers, triumph for the Prophet) and Ma'arif al-Qur'an;
  Ibn Kathir's "Allah will aid you and make you prevail."
- First messenger to the earth: al-Qurtubi (Anas report).
- The "thousand less fifty" wording: al-Qurtubi's two reasons
  (magnifying the count; the gifted-fifty-years report).
- The name Nūḥ from nawḥ (lament/weeping): al-Qurtubi.
- Unceasing calling, night and day, with patience until the duʿāʾ
  of 71:26: as-Saʿdī and Ibn Kathir; at-Tabari (increased only in
  flight).
- al-tufan range (rain / drowning / death) and its Arabic sense:
  al-Qurtubi, at-Tabari; the sky-and-earth water: as-Saʿdī.
- ẓālimūn = shirk (al-Baghawi/Ibn ʿAbbās), wronging themselves by
  kufr (at-Tabari), kufr with tyranny (al-Muyassar).
- Ibn ʿUmar on the decline of lifespans/intellect/character (Ibn
  Kathir).

Disagreement kept, no position taken: whether 950 is Nūḥ's whole
life (Qatāda: 300+300+350, which Ibn Kathir calls odd) or the span
of his calling this people (Ibn ʿAbbās, at-Tabari; Ibn Kathir's
lean), with the longer age-reckonings (Kaʿb 1020, Wahb 1400) and
Ma'arif framing the 950 as the Qur'an's fixed, certain figure.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3340 (intercession) — Nūḥ as "the first of
the messengers to the people of the earth," named "a thankful slave"
(cf. 17:3); ṣaḥīḥ.

Licensing: stated plainly in both languages that the flood is Allah's
decree on a past nation and licenses nothing against any living
person or community; no reading of present calamity as a verdict.

Dropped: the "house with two doors" image (no sound chain — flagged
as such, not asserted) and al-Qurtubi's tanbīh hadith on ʿUmar
(al-Khaṭīb: not established). Kept distinct from shipped 23:27 (the
drowned son) and 29:2-3: focus is the length of patient calling
against the brevity of the verse, and the ẓālimūn condition.
