## The shipped article (917 words, 6 sections): what was dropped or corrected
- **Flagged by the orchestrator ("the same verb"):** the shipped text as loaded now (EN and BN) actually reads "returns from the same root in another form" (BN: একই মূলের ক্রিয়া, শুধু রূপটা ভিন্ন); no "same verb" wording found in the EN or BN. The point it builds on it ("asking and obeying are placed on one continuum") is unsourced. Replaced by what the tafsirs say: at-Tabari, and "others" in al-Qurtubi, treat istajaba = ajaba; Ibn 'Atiyya (via al-Qurtubi) reads the istaf'ala form as seeking; al-Baghawi distinguishes giving (from Allah) and obedience (from the servant). The new text says only "share the root of answering but are different verb forms" (al-Qurtubi names the istaf'ala pattern).
- **Wrong:** "The commentators note the omission [of qul] and read it as part of the meaning." The fetched commentators do the reverse: al-Baghawi and the Muyassar supply the implied "say to them". The 2:189 comparison is kept only as a textual observation.
- **Wrong / conflated:** "The Prophet ﷺ called du'a the very substance of worship, in a report transmitted by at-Tirmidhi." The fetched tafsirs cite "du'a is worship" (an-Nu'man ibn Bashir); now quoted from Abu Dawud 1479.
- **Wrong attribution:** the three outcomes given as "a hadith in the Musnad of Ahmad, with a similar report in at-Tirmidhi"; Ahmad not confirmable, Tirmidhi's report has two outcomes, not three.
- **Unsourced, dropped:** 'ibadi as "a term of belonging"; qarib as an adjective stating a permanent quality; "the commentators generally read this as a condition of quality rather than eligibility"; the sandal-strap report; the last-third-of-the-night descent hadith; prostration and the last hour of Friday as times; "du'a in the mother tongue is agreed upon by the scholars"; "people who pray this way report…"; the list-on-the-phone advice.
- **Kept, now sourced:** placement among the fasting rules (Ibn Kathir, Ma'arif); the near-or-far question (now with its chain status); the haste hadith (Bukhari 6340, Muslim 2735); adhan-iqamah and the breaking of the fast as times (al-Qurtubi's list).
