Mūsā wakes in the city fearful and watchful the morning after the
unintended killing (28:15); the man he helped yesterday cries out
again, and Mūsā rebukes him: "you are a manifest ghawiyy."

Sources: Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari (+ Ibn Abbas, as-Suddi), al-Muyassar,
al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi (ar); Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en) for the
two rulings on helping the oppressed vs. backing an oppressor. Fear
glossed as fear of retaliation for the slain soul / of being handed
over by his own / of God (al-Qurtubi); yataraqqab as glancing about
and awaiting the pursuit and the city's talk.

Disagreements kept open, no verdict: who "innaka la-ghawiyyun mubin"
addresses — the Israelite of his own faction (majority; al-Baghawi:
al-awwal aswab wa-'alayhi al-aktharun) or the Copt (al-Hasan via
al-Qurtubi); the senses of ghawi; asbaha = "became" vs "entered the
morning."

Lesson: a believer's justice is not partisan — he names and withholds
help from wrong even on his own side. Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 2444,
"help your brother, oppressor or oppressed... by restraining his hand"
(quoted whole, collector's sahih, not upgraded; unused elsewhere).

Care taken: never says the prophet erred; fear is the human aftermath,
the death unintended (28:15) and already answered with God (28:16-17).
Licensing sentence carried (Egyptian oppressor-class named). The 28:19
reveal deliberately withheld as the next target verse.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's weak qila identifying the Israelite as as-Samiri.
