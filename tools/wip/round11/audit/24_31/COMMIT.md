Read as the twin of 24:30: the charge to lower the gaze and guard
chastity is given to believing men first, then to believing women with
the same verbs (wa-qul), so modesty is a shared trust, not a one-sided
burden. Article opens on that pairing (Ma'arif) and closes on the
verse's own hope — wa-tubu ila-llahi jami'an... la'allakum tuflihun —
which turns everyone, enforcer first, back to their own repentance.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir (abridged en),
Ma'arif al-Qur'an; al-Muyassar and al-Baghawi read as corroboration.

Kept open (no fatwa): illa ma zahara minha — Ibn Mas'ud (+al-Hasan,
an-Nakha'i) = the outer garment/rida'; Ibn Abbas via Sa'id b. Jubayr =
face and hands, which at-Tabari himself preferred; al-Baydawi/al-Khazin
reconcile as whatever uncovers during work. Reported as a range; shared
ground noted (fitna bars it; prayer valid with face/hands bare).

Khimar/jayb glossed from al-Qurtubi (headcover drawn over the chest
opening). Mahram list, at-tabi'in ghayri uli l-irba, and the child
glossed soberly (Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di, Ma'arif), no graphic content.
as-Sa'di's sadd adh-dhara'i drawn from the anklet clause.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4759 (Safiyya bint Shayba from A'isha, on the
khimar clause), quoted whole from quranx, grading sahih, unchanged —
framed as the eager reception of the first believing women.

Care statement in both languages: this is guidance to the believing
woman's own God-consciousness and licenses nothing against any woman —
no compulsion, no shaming, no policing in God's name.

Dropped (unconfirmed / tone): the perfume and side-of-road reports and
the describing-a-woman report from Ibn Kathir; see LEDGER.
