Card and deep bilingual article for 26:219 (wa-taqallubaka fi al-sajidin),
"and your movement among those who prostrate."

Sources gave two readings, both transmitted from Ibn Abbas, kept side by side
without the article choosing between them:
- (a) the turning of prayer itself — the body's shift through standing, bowing,
  prostration and sitting, and the move from solitary prayer into the
  congregation. From at-Tabari (Ibn Abbas, Ikrimah), as-Sa'di (nearness in
  prayer breeds humility), Ibn Kathir (Qatada: alone and in the gathering;
  so Ikrimah, Ata al-Khurasani, al-Hasan al-Basri), al-Baghawi (Muqatil,
  al-Kalbi) and al-Muyassar.
- (b) the Prophet's passing through the loins (aslab) of the prophets, prophet
  to prophet, until brought forth — from Ibn Kathir (al-Bazzar / Ibn Abi Hatim,
  two chains), al-Qurtubi (Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim) and al-Baghawi (Ata from Ibn
  Abbas; Said ibn Jubayr: the prostrating ones are the prophets).

at-Tabari's preference for (a) as the plain sense, and his "far-fetched" verdict
on (b) and on Mujahid's gloss, are reported as his, not adopted.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 741 (Abu Hurayrah, via Malik) — "You see me facing the
qiblah ... I see you from behind my back" — quoted whole, sahih by its place in
Bukhari, cited here because al-Baghawi and Ibn Kathir attach it to Mujahid's
gloss of this verse; §6 notes plainly that it supports the reading the others
set aside.

Tadabbur: the gaze over the worshipper is nearness and care (Ibn Kathir on
26:218 = "taking care of you," with 52:48, "before Our eyes"), set by the two
names in 26:217 (the Mighty, the Merciful) — intimacy, not surveillance.

Dropped: the "light through the forebears" phrasing (the texts say loins/aslab,
not nur); Mujahid's "sees those behind him" as a co-equal third reading; no
asbab (placement only); 26:220-223 left untouched.
