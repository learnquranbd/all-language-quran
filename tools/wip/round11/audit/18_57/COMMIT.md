Card plus 8-section bilingual article on "who is more unjust than
one reminded of his Lord's signs who turns away."

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi,
al-Muyassar (all ar, on 18:57); Ma'arif and Ibn Kathir abridged (en).

What the sources gave:
- aẓlam as a verdict in question-form; as-Sa'di frames the sin-with-
  knowledge as heavier than the sin of one never reached.
- nasiya mā qaddamat yadāhu = his own bad deeds; at-Tabari (via Qatada)
  adds "did not repent."
- akinnah/waqr read plainly across all six: coverings on the heart,
  Ibn Kathir's "abstract deafness to guidance" in the ears.
- The sealing kept as a consequence of the man's own prior turning
  (as-Sa'di bi-sabab; at-Tabari ṭabaʿa; al-Qurtubi bi-sababi kufrihim);
  the qadar tension left unresolved, as the mufassirūn leave it.
- "never guided" read as about a hardened, named class, not a blanket:
  al-Qurtubi (qawm muʿayyanūn; refutes the Qadariyya), al-Baghawi
  (peoples Allah knew), as-Sa'di (those who saw then went blind).

Disagreement kept: al-Qurtubi's two readings of nasiya (tark of sin vs
forgetting the punishment stored up), "the meaning is close."

Hadith: none is fixed by the mufassirūn to 18:57. The nearest sound
narration (Muslim, via Anas, on limbs testifying) belongs to 18:54 and
is marked as such. Bukhari 660 (seven under the shade) checked and
dropped as off-verse.

Licensing: §8 states plainly, both languages, that the verse licenses
nothing against any living person; the sealing is God's act, not a
human warrant. 18:58's mercy signposted, not expounded.
