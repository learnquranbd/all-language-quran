## Watch-points
- Plural darkness / singular light: only Ibn Kathir comments on it (§4), attributed; stated that the other fetched tafsirs do not. Glosses of the darknesses and the light named per source (§3).
- Taghut glosses with speakers: rivals and idols (at-Tabari, al-Muyassar); Shaytan (Qatada in at-Tabari); devils (Ibn Kathir; al-Hasan's reading at-tawaghit in al-Qurtubi); Shaytan and his party (as-Sa'di); Ka'b ibn al-Ashraf, Huyayy ibn Akhtab and heads of misguidance (Muqatil in al-Baghawi), with the licensing sentence in both languages. Soothsayers: NOT in any 2:257 text (see Dropped).
- "Out of the light" when never in it: specific reading (Mujahid or Miqsam via 'Abdah, at-Tabari 5859/5860; al-Qurtubi; at-Tabari's preference) vs general reading (at-Tabari's alternative, Ibn 'Atiyyah in al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi's second qila) kept as a disagreement, no position. Named communities (Christians, Jews; Arabs only as Ibn 'Atiyyah's example of a nation part of which believed): licensing sentence in EN and BN (§6.2), plus §5.2 for Muqatil's named men. Nothing in own voice about any present group.
- Wilayah: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi/al-Khattabi, al-Baghawi (incl. al-Hasan), al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di's two-way reading.
- Overlap: 2:255 one line, 2:256 one line (no ruling discussion; "leaves the rulings of 2:256 to that verse"), 2:258 one bridging line.

## Hadith
None. No fetched tafsir attaches a Prophetic hadith to 2:257; the article says so in one sentence (§8.2). The Ayyub ibn Khalid report (via Ibn Abi Hatim in Ibn Kathir) is used, marked as his own saying, not the Prophet's, with no grading given by Ibn Kathir (none added).

## Asbab an-nuzul
'Abdah ibn Abi Lubabah's "this verse was revealed about them" (at-Tabari 5860) and Mujahid/'Abdah "نزلت في قوم آمنوا بعيسى" (al-Qurtubi) are reported as those men's reports, inside the disagreement with Ibn 'Atiyyah and al-Baghawi's general reading; not presented as an established occasion. No al-Wahidi page fetched.

## Points in the shipped 838-word, 6-section article: dropped or found wrong
- "Taghut, from a root meaning to overflow all bounds ... idols, tyrants, soothsayers": root etymology, tyrants and soothsayers are in no 2:257 text. Dropped.
- "The scholars' definitions vary in wording and agree in substance: anything that takes for itself, or is given, what belongs to Allah alone": no fetched source. Dropped.
- "The commentators explain that the light was available to them: the clear proofs of 2:256, the sound nature every person is created upon": WRONG as attributed. The fetched commentators give the 'Isa/Muhammad report (Mujahid/'Abdah), the Jews' prior belief (al-Baghawi), and the barring/preventing reading (at-Tabari, Ibn 'Atiyyah, al-Baghawi); none mentions fitrah or 2:256's proofs. Replaced by §6-§7.
- "The commentators read the passage as one lesson in three steps": no source says this. Replaced by as-Sa'di's actual linking clause (§1.2).
- "zulumat, always plural in this idiom" and "falsehood is many: sects, superstitions, desires, ideologies": "always" and the list are not in Ibn Kathir; his words are "disbelief comes in many kinds". Kept only his wording.
- "The Same Promise Elsewhere" section (5:16, 14:1, 65:11, 33:43): no fetched 2:257 tafsir cites these. Dropped (Ibn Kathir's opening echoes 5:16's wording but does not cite it; not used).
- "Wali ... not a feeling but a working relationship ... neither sleeps": own gloss; replaced by the sourced glosses in §2.
- "Auditing Your Allegiances" (feeds followed, voices obeyed): own-voice reflection, not sourced; replaced by a shorter reflection built on as-Sa'di, at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir (§8.3).

## Dropped (could not confirm, or chosen out)

- Ma'arif's "there is darkness in friendship with those who disbelieve (kuffar)": left out; it would need its own muwalat discussion and reads as a ruling about present relations.
- At-Tabari's poetry witness (al-'Abbas ibn Mirdas) on the singular-for-plural form; the "fatr" example; the "akhbarani fulan min katibatihi" idiom. Length.

- Ibn 'Atiyyah's closing: "He judged them to enter the Fire for their disbelief, justly; He is not asked about what He does." Length.
- Ibn Kathir's opening "paths of peace" (5:16 wording, uncited by him). Not used.
