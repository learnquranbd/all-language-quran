Key kept at 4:46 (shifting words from their places; "we hear and we disobey" set
against "we hear and we obey"). 4:44-45 cited for placement; 4:47 left free.
New card and a 1,750-word, 8-section article.

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar), al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi and
al-Muyassar on 4:46, all slots on target. Ma'arif's 4:44-46 slot actually
introduces 4:47 and was not used.
Licensing early and plain in both languages: "among those who are Jews", people
around Madinah in the Prophet's time named by their deeds; licenses nothing
against any Jew or anyone living; no claim about any scripture today.
Kept as differences, no position:
- min: joined to 4:44 (Kufans, at-Tabari's preference) vs an omitted qawm/man
  (Basrans, Sibawayh, al-Farra; al-Mubarrad and az-Zajjaj object), fariq
  (al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar), li-bayan al-jins (Ibn Kathir), its misguiding
  scholars (as-Sa'di).
- tahrif: meaning and interpretation (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Nahhas) vs
  wording, meaning or both (as-Sa'di); the words as the Torah (Mujahid), the
  Prophet's description (al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi), his own answers (Ibn 'Abbas
  in al-Baghawi); the al-kalam reading of as-Sulami and an-Nakha'i.
- ghayra musma': "may you not hear" (Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn Zayd; at-Tabari, al-Nahhas,
  Ibn Kathir) vs "not accepted" (Mujahid, al-Hasan); as-Suddi's "not humbled".
- illa qalilan: little faith (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, al-Muyassar;
  al-Qurtubi calls "a few of them" far-fetched) vs a few, Ibn Salam and those
  with him (al-Baghawi).
Ra'ina/unzurna left to 2:104. Hadith: none attached to 4:46 (stated); Muslim 125
(Abu Hurayrah, 2:284-286) marked as not tied to the verse, used for the turn
from 'asayna to ata'na. Asbab: none given; not used.
Dropped: imprecations in Ibn Kathir (ar, en abr.), the en abr. "Hebrew" claim,
as-Sa'di's "tiba'uhum ghayr zakiyyah", Qatada's ambiguous gloss, unzurna glosses.
Fix: §1 no longer asserts in its own voice that min means "some of them" (Ibn Kathir reads it li-bayan al-jins); it cites al-Baghawi and al-Muyassar's "a party" and points to the grammar section.
