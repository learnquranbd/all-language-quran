The one message every messenger carried: no god but Allah, so worship Him.

21:25 answers the challenge of 21:24 ("produce your proof") from the archive of
revelation itself: not one messenger was sent before the Prophet without this
single line — lā ilāha illā anā, faʿbudūn.

Sources:
- as-Sa'di: this is the cream (zubda) and root of every message and book —
  worship Allah alone, He is the true God, worship of all else is void.
- Ibn Kathir: every prophet called to Allah alone; the fiṭrah bears witness;
  the idolaters have no proof. Cross-verse 16:36 used for content only.
- at-Tabari + al-Qurtubi both carry Qatāda: one creed, differing laws — a law
  in the Torah, in the Gospel, in the Qur'an, all resting on ikhlāṣ and tawḥīd.
- faʿbudūn glossed as exclusivity: at-Tabari "single out divinity for Me",
  al-Baghawi "declare My oneness", al-Muyassar "none worshipped in truth but Him".
- al-Qurtubi: proof is either rational or transmitted — both point one way; plus
  the qirāʾāt note (nūḥī / yūḥā), seconded by al-Baghawi.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: Qur'an and the earlier books carry no hint of shirk; even
  the altered Torah and Gospel do not suggest partners in Allah's authority.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3443 (sound) — "the prophets are paternal brothers;
their mothers differ, but their religion is one" — the image behind Qatāda's
distinction.

Kept distinct from the just-shipped 21:7 (humanity of prophets) by focusing on
the shared content of the message, not the messengers' mortality; avoided the
"ask those before you" angle of 16:36/16:43 (dropped Ibn Kathir's 43:45).
Not a condemned-group verse — no licensing sentence; 21:26–29 left untouched.
