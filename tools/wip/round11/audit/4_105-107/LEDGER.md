## Disagreements kept (both names, no position)
- What the istighfar refers to: at-Tabari (his dhanb in pleading; or an inclination only), the narration's gloss, Ibn 'Atiyyah via al-Qurtubi (no sin: defended on the apparent; seek forgiveness for others), tasbih, address-to-him-meant-for-them, al-Baghawi's three senses, as-Sa'di's conditional, Ma'arif. Article states it adopts none; never says in its own voice that he erred.
- Where the line on representation sits: as-Sa'di (forbidden where wrong is known or thought likely; permitted where none is known) vs al-Qurtubi (only after knowing the client is right).
- Who the innocent was: Labid (at-Tirmidhi) vs a Jew, Zayd ibn as-Samin (Mujahid, Ikrimah, al-Baghawi); al-Qurtubi's Jew/Muslim note on Labid; Ma'arif's harmonising.

- Tirmidhi 3036, https://quranx.com/hadith/Tirmidhi/Hadith-3036 (hadith_Tirmidhi_3036.txt). Summarised, not quoted (the full story ships with 4:110). Opens "There was a household among us called Banu Ubairiq, among". At-Tirmidhi's grading: gharib (only Muhammad ibn Salamah al-Harrani connects it; Yunus ibn Bukayr and others narrate it mursal). Note: al-Qurtubi (t90_105) quotes at-Tirmidhi as "hasan gharib"; the fetched page and Ibn Kathir both read "gharib", which is what the article gives.
- Bukhari 7169 fetched to compare wordings; not used (file deleted).

## Asbab
Used, as reports with their sources: at-Tirmidhi 3036 (graded gharib); Mujahid, Ikrimah and Ibn Zayd as at-Tabari gives them (no grading given by him); al-Baghawi through al-Kalbi from Abu Salih from Ibn Abbas (no grading given). Stated plainly that in these reports the Qur'an cleared an innocent man of another faith against men who counted themselves Muslims. Licensing sentence in both languages covers Jews, the Ansar, the Banu Zafar and the Banu Ubayriq (the guilty man's people) and anyone sharing their names.
