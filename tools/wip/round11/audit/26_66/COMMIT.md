Card and deep bilingual article on the drowning of Pharaoh's host,
the just end of the Musa narrative (26:64-66).

Spine: the same parted sea was a road of rescue for Moses and all with
him and a grave for the pursuers, one event with two destinies decided
by which side of the truth a person stood on.

Sources (all fetched, none from memory):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi: "the others" = Pharaoh and his
  people (of the Copts), drowned after Moses and his people were saved.
- as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir (ar): the completeness of both outcomes — none
  of the rescued lost, not a man of the pursuers left alive.
- Ibn Kathir (en): the sea's twelve paths (Ibn Abbas), as-Suddi's windows
  in the walls of water, the wind firming the sea bed; the episode as aid
  to the believers and a proof of God's wisdom.
- al-Muyassar: the sea stayed parted until they crossed, then closed —
  the force of thumma (rescue completed first, then the punishment).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 2004 (Ibn Abbas), the Ashura fast — the day God
rescued the Children of Israel from their enemy, which Moses and then the
Prophet marked with gratitude. Reported as being in Sahih al-Bukhari; no
grading upgraded.

Sober handling: the drowning is God's own judgment on a specific tyrant;
stated plainly in both languages that it licenses nothing against any
living person or community. Mercy-side kept: a long tyranny ended and the
oppressed walked free.

Dropped: Ibn Kathir's Ibn Mas'ud athar (not a sound attached hadith);
al-Baghawi's Sa'id ibn Jubayr tidal note (off-point); Ma'arif al-Qur'an
(its text is on 26:62, not the drowning). All listed in the ledger.

No asbab (narrative sequence, not a distinct occasion). No shipped-
neighbour overlap; 26:67-68 touched one clause each, no further narration.
