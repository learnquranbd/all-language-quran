Assigned 2:270, widened to 2:270-271 (whatever you spend or vow, Allah knows
it; open charity is good, hidden charity to the poor is better and covers some
sins); 2:265-269 and 2:272 left free. New card and a 1,741-word, 9-section
article centred on sincerity and Allah's knowing, kept apart from 2:264's
defects that void charity.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged), as-Sa'di,
al-Baghawi, Muyassar, Ma'arif on 2:270 and 2:271 (slots on target).
- Nadhr as what one binds on oneself in obedience (at-Tabari, al-Baghawi,
  al-Qurtubi, Ma'arif); vows "for Satan" as zulm (at-Tabari, as-Sa'di).
- Ya'lamuh as counting (Mujahid via at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi); the singular
  pronoun (at-Tabari, al-Baghawi, Ibn 'Atiyyah via al-Qurtubi).
- Ibn al-'Arabi's giver/receiver/onlookers via al-Qurtubi, with his condition.
Disagreements kept: secrecy for voluntary charity only (at-Tabari, Qatadah,
ar-Rabi', Sufyan, al-Hasan, Ibn 'Abbas's 70/25-fold report, most commentators
per al-Qurtubi) or for all charity (Ibn Kathir, Ma'arif); al-Mahdawi's
"changed with the times" and Ibn 'Atiyyah's reply; Yazid ibn Abi Habib's
Jews-and-Christians reading, rejected by Ibn 'Atiyyah; when openness is better
(Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di's "to the poor" condition); readings of yukaffir and
at-Tabari's choice of the jussive; min as partitive or extra.
Hadith: Bukhari 1423 (the seven shaded, whole), Nasa'i 1663 (reciting aloud
like open charity), Bukhari 6696 (vows, marked as not attached to the verse).
Dropped: "secret charity puts out the Lord's anger" (no source given), Ibn
Kathir's Ahmad narrations (Anas, Abu Dharr), ash-Sha'bi's Abu Bakr/'Umar
occasion, an-Naqqash's abrogation claim, al-Qurtubi's Muslim prayer hadith.
