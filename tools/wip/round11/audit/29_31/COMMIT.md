Card and deep bilingual article on 29:31 — the angels reach Ibrāhīm
with the good tidings and, in the same errand, the sentence on Lūṭ's town.

Sources carry a four-way agreement on al-bushrā: at-Tabari, al-Muyassar,
al-Baghawi and as-Sa'di all read it as Isaac, and beyond Isaac, Jacob —
a son and, in the same breath, a grandson. Ibn Kathir frames it as the
scene: angels as guests, Ibrāhīm's hospitality, the guests who would not
eat, his fear, then the glad tidings of a righteous son by Sarah.

The town is named Sadūm (Tabari, Muyassar, Baghawi); its people are
"wrongdoers" — Tabari and Muyassar gloss ẓālimīn as wronging themselves
by disobeying Allah (Tabari adds: denying His messenger). The article's
heart is the pairing: one visit, a birth announced and a destruction
announced, mercy and justice from one Lord on one morning.

Licensing sentence carried in both languages (§6): the verse reports a
decree Allah carried out on one named past people; it licenses nothing
against any living person or community, and the sin is named only as far
as the Qur'an names it, with no lurid detail.

No sound hadith attaches to the announcement; §7 says so plainly (per the
source gate) and places Ma'arif's off-verse Umm Hāni' report at 29:29
rather than building on it. The ten-households dispute (Tabari/Ibn ʿAbbās)
and the Lūṭ-rescue sequence are left for 29:32–35 as future ground; only
Ibrāhīm's first instinct to plead (Ibn Kathir) is kept, in §8.
