Card and deep bilingual article for the magicians' reply: "we hope our Lord
will forgive us our sins, since we are the first of the believers."

Spine: faith that, the instant it arrives, outweighs Pharaoh's threat of
mutilation and crucifixion (26:49) and turns at once to hope in God's mercy
for a sinful past.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari and al-Muyassar gloss naṭmaʿ as rajāʾ — eager hope that God forgive
  the sins that preceded their faith and not hold them to account.
- khaṭāyā read as sorcery and disbelief: at-Tabari (via Ibn Zayd), Ibn Kathir
  (adding "the magic you compelled us"), as-Saʿdi, al-Muyassar (shirk and other).
- "first of the believers" kept as two readings, left open: first of Pharaoh's
  people / at that scene (Ibn Kathir, al-Muyassar, at-Tabari via Ibn Zayd) vs.
  first believers of the age (al-Baghawi, al-Farra' via al-Qurtubi). al-Qurtubi's
  grammar (an = li-an; al-Farra's conditional) and az-Zajjaj's objection with the
  large-number report and the "small band" of 26:54 (Ibn Mas'ud) included.
- Ma'arif and Ibn Kathir (en) on the courage: former Pharaoh-worshippers professing
  faith before the tyrant, the veil of disbelief lifted.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 121 ('Amr b. al-'As — "Islam wipes out what came before it"),
used as the doctrinal basis of their hope and marked plainly as a general narration
not attached by the mufassirun to this verse.

Dropped: az-Zajjaj's exact figure kept to the ledger (prose says "hundreds of
thousands") for number-parity. Their fate left open per Saʿdi (two possibilities)
and Ibn Kathir ("so he killed them all"). Standing note added: the verse licenses
nothing against any living person or community.
