Bilqīs reads the cost of conquest and chooses to test, not fight.

Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the queen of Sheba's reply to her
chiefs' offer of war: kings enter a city by force, wreck it, and humble its
nobles — so she will verify before she commits.

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, Muyassar: entering ʿanwa = wrecking the
  town; the honoured made low by enslaving the free, killing or captivity, to
  break any standing that could resist.
- al-Qurtubi: her speech carries fear for her people, caution, and a reckoning
  with Sulaymān's gravity. as-Saʿdi/Muyassar: she talks her council off war and
  resolves to test first, acting on baṣīra, not the heat of the moment.
- Maʿārif: consultation is Sunnah (3:159); her council of 313 (Qatādah via
  al-Qurtubi). Ibn Kathir relays Qatādah's praise of her wisdom "as a Muslim and
  before that as an idolater."

Disagreement kept, no side taken: "wa-kadhālika yafʿalūn" — still Bilqīs's words
(al-Qurtubi's report + Ibn Shajara) versus Allah's own confirmation (at-Tabari,
al-Baghawi, Ibn Kathir from Ibn ʿAbbās; Ibn al-Anbari's complete-stop reading
with the al-Aʿrāf 7:110 parallel).

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2822 (al-mustashār muʾtaman), Tirmidhi's own grading hasan,
confirmed on quranx; not reused nearby.

Dropped: Wahb's bismillāh narrative (tangential); the "gift test = king/prophet"
logic held back for 27:35 (future target; 27:35 cited only as a forward pointer,
no content). No asbāb established; verse placed by context. Respectful framing of
a wise woman ruler throughout; no licensing sentence needed (not a condemned
people).
