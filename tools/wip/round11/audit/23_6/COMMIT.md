Tadabbur 23:6 — chastity guarded, and the bounds that make it praise

The fifth mark of the successful believers: they guard the farj (23:5),
and 23:6 names the sole lawful place for desire, sealed by 23:7 — whatever
lies beyond the bound is transgression.

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di gloss the exception as
the lawful wife and, for that era's legal order, mā malakat aymānuhum;
al-Baghawi that ʿalā means min and adds the condition that blame is lifted
only in the manner the Law permits. al-Qurtubi: by ijmāʿ a woman is not in
the verse, and ownership dissolves a prior marriage by fasḵ not ṭalāq.
Ma'arif: guard against unlawful liaison; desire kept under strict rein, not
gratification of the passions.

mā malakat aymānuhum handled soberly and briefly as a historical institution
of that era's law — stated plainly in both languages that it licenses
nothing today; article weight kept on chastity and self-restraint.

Disagreement kept unranked (via al-Qurtubi/Ibn Kathir): the general body of
scholars vs Ahmad ibn Hanbal on the solitary act; stated obliquely, no
position, no graphic content.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (the seven Allah shades), quoted whole, for the
man a woman of rank and beauty calls who answers "I fear Allah."

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's mutʿah excursus (off-thrust); Ibn Kathir's two weak
narrations (gharib / gharib munqatiʿ). Asbab: none established; wrote the
verse's placement instead. Card themes: character.
