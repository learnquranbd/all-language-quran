Mūsā's retort in the throne room: a tyrant's one kindness cannot cancel
the crime it was built on. Pharaoh boasts of raising Mūsā (26:18); 26:22
turns it back — that "favour" existed only because Pharaoh had enslaved a
whole people and was killing their sons.

Card: reflection + 5 questions + lesson, theme character. Article: 8
sections, 1665 EN words, bilingual.

Sources (all fetched for this verse; t169 Ibn Kathir-abridged skipped as
WRONG GROUP, covers 26:10-19):
- at-Tabari: "you enslaved" = Mujahid "subdued and put to work", Ibn
  Jurayj "subdued, overpowered, used"; the elided "and left me
  un-enslaved"; two i'rab of "an ʿabbadta".
- al-Qurtubi + al-Baghawi: the iqrar/inkar dispute kept open — Mūsā either
  concedes the upbringing then voids its worth (as-Suddi/at-Tabari/al-Farra')
  or denies it was a favour at all (Qatada); istifhām reading (al-Akhfash)
  and an-Nahhas's grammatical objection; ad-Dahhak's rebuke, "whoever's
  people are abased is abased".
- al-Muyassar: enslavement with slaughter of sons, sparing of women.
- Ibn Kathir + as-Sa'di: kindness to one man cannot offset evil to all;
  reflection shows the "favour" is the crime's by-product.
- Ma'arif: the palace itself was the cruelty's doing; prophetic dialectic,
  Mūsā owning the killing first without camouflage.

Hadith §6: no mufassir attaches one to 26:22, said so; filled with a
marked general narration — Tirmidhī 2174 (hasan gharīb, not upgraded), a
just word before a tyrannical ruler; Abū Dāwūd 4344 noted as parallel.

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or community"
guard: Pharaoh is a named Qur'anic tyrant, not a warrant against anyone
today.

Dropped: Ma'arif's exact 400-years / 630,000 figures (kept as "for
