Mūsā (AS) takes his fear straight to his Lord: rabbi innī akhāfu an
yukadhdhibūni. Framed throughout as candour and reliance, never as a flaw,
sin, or failure of faith.

Tafsir: at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi read the fear as denial in the message and
prophethood; al-Muyassar spells out the tight chest, the halting tongue, and
the charge over the Copt. Ma'arif al-Qur'an supplies the guard-rail — asking
for favourable conditions to obey is not making excuses — and as-Saʿdi reads
the verse as iʿtidhār plus a request for help to bear a heavy load. Ibn Kathir
(ar + en) carries it to God's answer, kallā, "We are with you, listening,"
glossed as His protection, care, support and help, with the Ṭāhā parallel
(20:25-26, 20:36, 20:46) and 28:35.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 7405 (qudsi), "I am with him when he remembers Me,"
mirroring innā maʿakum; quoted whole from the fetched quranx page.

Card themes: trust, duas. Closing note states plainly, in both languages,
that naming Pharaoh's people as wrongdoers condemns a historical tyranny and
licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Dropped: 26:18-20 and 26:22 material (later targets); al-Baghawi added nothing
beyond identifying the speaker; no sabab asserted.
