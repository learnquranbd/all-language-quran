Replaces the 714-word, 5-section article with a 9-section, 1,787-word
source-gated article (shipped card kept).

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, Muyassar,
Ma'arif, all on 39:9 itself. Covers the contrast with 39:8 (with the
licensing sentence), the amman/aman readings (readers named by al-Qurtubi;
at-Tabari: both correct; vocative and interrogative senses; al-Baghawi's
two construals), the four senses of qanit (Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn Shihab, Yahya
ibn Sallam, caller; Ibn 'Umar's recitation and long standing; at-Tabari's
obedience tied to the hal grammar; an-Nahhas), ana'a-l-layl (hours;
depths; Maghrib-'Isha; al-Hasan's view general), fear and hope (Sa'id ibn
Jubayr from Ibn 'Abbas; as-Sa'di; Ibn Kathir's fear-in-life, hope-at-death),
the knowers (at-Tabari, Muyassar, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir) and ulu-l-albab.

Disagreements kept: qunut as obedience vs recitation and long standing vs
humility; ana' as all hours vs depths vs Maghrib-'Isha; az-Zajjaj's likeness
vs "others" defining knowers as those who act on knowledge. Reports naming
the qanit ('Uthman, Abu Bakr, Abu Bakr and 'Umar, Ibn Mas'ud, 'Ammar,
Salman, Suhayb, Abu Dharr, the Prophet, or general) listed, none chosen.

Hadith: Muslim 756 (long qunut; general, via al-Qurtubi); Tirmidhi 983
(hope and fear at death, graded gharib by at-Tirmidhi, also Ibn Majah 4261;
via Ibn Kathir, marked as not about the verse).

Dropped: Tamim ad-Dari 100-verses report (unconfirmable), marfu' "every
qunut is obedience", partisan and qila identifications of the knowers, and
the old article's unsourced claims (two wings, no audience at night,
51:17-18 and 3:190-191 cross-refs, lubb as kernel).
