Card plus 8-section bilingual article (1,749 EN words) on the revived
dead earth turned to garden, and gratitude as its due response.

Sources (each confirmed on the fetched text for this verse):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi: jannat glossed basatin, tended orchards.
- al-Qurtubi: palm and vine singled out as a'la ath-thamar, the highest fruits.
- as-Sa'di: the two called ashraf al-ashjar, noblest of the trees.
- at-Tabari / al-Muyassar / Ibn Kathir (ar): the springs welling up, irrigating,
  set as rivers coursing to the places that need them, inside the gardens.
- Ibn Kathir (en): "their hands made it not" (36:35) = all by Allah's mercy, not
  human effort — the view of Ibn 'Abbas and Qatada.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: the Ya Sin signs work two ways, proof of power and favour.

Disagreement kept: the ma of 36:35 — negation (Ibn 'Abbas, Qatada) vs. relative
pronoun (Ibn Jarir at-Tabari, who favoured it; fits Ibn Mas'ud's recitation).
Reported as a difference, no side taken.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2734 (Anas ibn Malik) — Allah is pleased with the servant
who praises Him over a morsel and a drink. Sahih, not upgraded.

Resurrection-sign tie-back (36:33, Ibn Kathir): the dead earth revived is proof
of the power to raise the dead; provision and argument in one.

Dropped: the jannat/Paradise root echo kept as an unattributed lexical note (no
mufassir links it); asbab not used (none established); no weak report admitted.
Distinct from shipped 32:27 — stays on the garden/fruit and the "hands did not
make it" humbling, not the driving of water.
