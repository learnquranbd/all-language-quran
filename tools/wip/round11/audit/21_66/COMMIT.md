Ibrahim (AS) turns his people's own admission (21:65, that the idols
cannot speak) into the question of 21:66: then do you worship, instead
of Allah, what neither benefits you at all nor harms you?

Sources: Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawi read it as the plain test — no benefit
if you worship it, no harm if you leave it. al-Muyassar's belittling voice
(66-portion only) sharpens the absurdity. at-Tabari drives it past speech:
the idol could not keep harm off itself nor name its attacker, "are you not
ashamed?", and relays Ibn Ishaq — what cannot defend its own body cannot
defend yours. as-Sa'di sets the rebuke "before the assembled heads of the
people"; al-Qurtubi weighs the single word qala, cutting off their raving.
Ibn Kathir (en): "He defeated them in argument and left them no way out,"
tied back to 6:83, "Our proof which We gave Ibrahim" — the argument is a
God-given hujjah, not private wit.

Hadith: none attached. No gathered tafsir ties a sound hadith to this
verse's argument, and the article says so. The Bukhari/Muslim "three lies"
narration belongs to 21:63, not here, and is set aside; not quoted or
graded. Ma'arif's bulk (tauriyah/metaphoric attribution) is 21:63 material,
noted only to keep the article from ever calling a prophet's words untrue.

Did not preempt 21:67 (uff / use reason): Muyassar's grouped 67-text
excluded. Licensing section states the verse condemns the stance of
worshipping the powerless and licenses nothing against any living person or
community. Card theme: tawheed. Lesson: weigh anything you give worship,
fear or hope to by one test — does it truly hold power over your good and
harm, or is it as powerless as stone?
