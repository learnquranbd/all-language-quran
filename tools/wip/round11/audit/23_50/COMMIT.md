Isa and his mother named one sign, then sheltered on a rabwah of
level ground and flowing water.

Sources gave:
- The sign (ayah, singular for two): at-Tabari from Qatada — born
  without a father, so the singular though both are named; al-Baghawi
  — "their affair is one sign" or each a sign, cf. 18:33.
- What the sign is: Ibn Kathir's fourfold creation (Adam, Hawwa, Isa,
  the rest); as-Sa'di adds cradle-speech and signs by his hand;
  Muyassar the plain gloss. Kept to the Qur'anic frame, no polemics.
- rabwah = raised ground (Ibn Abbas via Dahhak; Mujahid a level hill;
  al-Qurtubi, at-Tabari on the verb 'awa).
- qarar = level/settled ground (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, al-Baghawi);
  Qatada's "fruits" reading kept with at-Tabari's own reservation.
- ma'in = flowing water visible to the eye (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid,
  Qatada, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi); Ibn Kathir + as-Sa'di link it to
  the stream of 19:24, as-Sa'di to the palm of 19:25.

Disagreement kept open: WHERE the rabwah is — Ramla/Palestine
(Abu Hurayra), Damascus + its Ghuta (Ibn al-Musayyab, Ikrimah from
Ibn Abbas, Mujahid), Bayt al-Maqdis (Qatada, Ka'b), Egypt (Ibn Zayd;
Wahb, which Ibn Kathir calls far-fetched). No verdict in the article's
own voice; Ibn Kathir's and at-Tabari's preferences reported as theirs.

Hadith: none sound. The only place-report (Murrah al-Bahzi, "you will
die at the rabwah") is graded gharib jiddan by Ibn Kathir and its
chain is broken in at-Tabari; stated as weak, claimed as no proof.

Dropped: any location verdict; at-Tabari's poetic shahid for ma'in.
