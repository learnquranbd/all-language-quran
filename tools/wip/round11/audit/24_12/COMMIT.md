Tadabbur 24:12 — think well, and refuse to carry the charge

Spine: the duty of good opinion (husn az-zann) and refusing to
spread an unverified charge. The verse rebukes those who repeated
the ifk against ʿĀʾishah (RA), Mother of the Believers, whom Allah
cleared in 24:11-20. Handled with reverence: incident stated factually,
vindication only, no salacious detail.

From the sources:
- Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari: the verse is a reproach/disciplining of the
  believers; lawla = halla (Baghawi, Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir).
- Ibn Kathir, Qurtubi: measure the charge against yourself — if it
  would not befit you, the Mother of the Believers is worthier of being
  cleared.
- bi-anfusihim "of their own selves": one body (Ma'arif, at-Tabari,
  Baghawi via al-Hasan); "of their brethren" (an-Nahhas via Qurtubi/
  Baghawi); parallels 49:11, 4:29, 24:61 (Ma'arif; Mujahid via Tabari).
- Inner + outer duty: heart's good opinion + the tongue's "this is a
  manifest lie" (Ibn Kathir); the obligatory opinion (as-Sa'di); faith
  demanded outright rejection, ruling that rejecting an unproven charge
  is obligatory (Ma'arif/Mazhari).
- Ma'arif's person-shift point ("the believers" not "you").
- Qurtubi's principle: a doubtful report of corrupt/unknown origin does
  not strip a Muslim's faith, righteousness or chastity, however it spreads.

Hadith: Bukhari 6064 (Abu Hurayra RA, "Beware of suspicion...") quoted
whole, Sahih. Ifk hadith Bukhari 4141 / Muslim 2770 (both Sahih) cited
for the vindication only. Abu Ayyub & Umm Ayyub (RA) as the exchange
some hold the verse was revealed about; Ibn Kathir: also said Ubayy ibn
Kaʿb (RA).

Carries the "licenses nothing" sentence: the verse condemns rumour-
spreading and licenses nothing against anyone accused (and no doubt of
ʿĀʾishah RA, whom Allah declared innocent).

Dropped: as-Sa'di's "subhanaka" gloss (that is 24:16's wording, not this
verse's). Headings kept clear of shipped 24:2, 24:22, 24:35.
