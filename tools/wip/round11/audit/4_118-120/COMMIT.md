Assigned 4:119, widened to 4:118-120 (the appointed share; I will mislead them, stir
hopes in them, have them slit the ears of cattle and alter Allah's creation; he promises
and stirs hopes, and promises only delusion). 4:117 and 4:121-124 left free; 4:122 and
4:123 cited once as sisters. New card and a 1,755-word, 9-section article.

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar), al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi and al-Muyassar on
4:118, 4:119 and 4:120. at-Tabari is shifted one slot back: his 4:119 is under 4:118, and his
4:118 is under 4:117 (fetched as t15_117). Ma'arif's group covers only 4:116.
Centre: wa la-umanniyannahum. al-Qurtubi's hopes fitted to each person's desire, Ibn
Kathir's leaving repentance, put off and delay, al-Baghawi's three readings and his
"adorning, nothing of misguiding is in his hands", as-Sa'di's hope of the guided's reward.
4:120 through at-Tabari (14:22, 8:48, 24:39), al-Qurtubi with Ibn 'Arafah on ghurur, and
as-Sa'di (the promise includes threat, 2:268). al-Baghawi's f-r-d and "whatever Iblis is
obeyed in is his share" carry the application.
Kept as a difference, no position: khalq Allah as castration (Ibn 'Abbas, Anas, 'Ikrimah)
vs Allah's religion and fitrah (Ibn 'Abbas in another report, Mujahid, an-Nakha'i,
Qatadah, as-Suddi, ad-Dahhak, with 30:30) vs tattooing (al-Hasan, also reported for
religion). at-Tabari's tarjih and his ears argument are given as his; as-Sa'di and the
Muyassar hold both.
No ruling on any present-day procedure. The reader is sent to a qualified scholar, and
contempt for any living person or community is ruled out in both languages.
Hadith: Muslim 2865 ('Iyad, "I created My servants hunafa'", whole); Bukhari 4886
(Ibn Mas'ud, under the tattooing reading only, as the tafsir uses it); Tirmidhi 464 (the
witr du'a, hasan by him; as-Sa'di closes on its words).
Dropped: al-Qurtubi's extra 'Iyad clause "and commanded them to alter My creation" (not
in Muslim 2865), Muqatil's 999 of 1,000, the Mujahid-'Ikrimah exchange, Shakir's
footnote, the unverified Abu al-Ahwas report, Tawus, and Bukhari 1358 (cut for length).
