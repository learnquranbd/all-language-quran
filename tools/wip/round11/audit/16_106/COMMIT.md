Card and deep bilingual article for 16:106 (the willing apostate condemned;
the one compelled with a heart at rest in faith excepted).

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi: the verse's occasion is
  ʿAmmar ibn Yasir (Ibn ʿAbbas, Qatada, Abu Malik, ash-Sha'bi), compelled at
  the well of Maymun; the Prophet's "How do you find your heart?" / "If they
  return, then return." Reported by the mufassirun with no grade attached; kept
  as "reported," not upgraded.
- al-Baghawi + al-Qurtubi: Yasir and Sumayyah martyred, the first two killed in
  Islam (Ibn ʿAbbas; Mujahid). Given as tafsir report, no grade attached.
- as-Sa'di / Ibn Kathir / al-Qurtubi / Muyassar: "opened his breast to
  disbelief" = willing apostasy; the exception is the forced tongue over a firm
  heart. al-Qurtubi: no one has power over that opening but Allah.

Disagreements kept as disagreements:
- Uttering kufr under compulsion is a genuine concession (rukhsah), yet bearing
  the torture and refusing is the better course — al-Qurtubi's consensus that
  choosing death is greater in reward, Maliki (Ibn Habib, Sahnun) vs Iraqi
  positions on non-kufr coerced acts. Coerced divorce: Malik/Shafiʿi (void) vs
  Abu Hanifa (effective).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3612 (Khabbab ibn al-Aratt, the "sawn in two" report),
quoted whole; sound; adduced by al-Qurtubi here for the steadfastness side.

Dropped: al-Kalbi's naming of specific individuals as apostates (avoids a takfir
statement in the article's own voice); Muqatil/Mujahid alternative occasions;
Tirmidhi's gharib "Paradise longs for three." Standing licensing sentence kept
prominent: the verse licenses nothing against any living person or community and
gives no takfir verdict on anyone living.
