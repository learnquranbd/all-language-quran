Assigned 3:70, widened to 3:70-71 (why deny Allah's signs while you witness;
why mix truth with falsehood and conceal it knowingly). 3:67-68 and 3:76-77
ship; 3:69 and 3:72-75 left free, 3:72-73 used as context. New card and a
1,742-word, 8-section article on witnessing, labs and kitman, and the truths
we know and blur.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir (ar + en), as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi,
al-Muyassar, Ma'arif on 3:70 and 3:71 (all slots on target).
- What they witnessed: truth from their Lord (at-Tabari); the description in
  their book (Qatada, ar-Rabi', al-Baghawi); the Prophet himself (as-Suddi);
  Islam as the religion (Ibn Jurayj); earlier prophets' signs (al-Qurtubi's
  "it is said"); confided among themselves (as-Sa'di).
- Ma'arif: knowing does not make kufr permissible otherwise; it sharpens it.
- as-Sa'di's pair: 3:70 their own going astray, 3:71 misleading others; his
  turn to scholars in general carries the application.
Kept as differences: labs as tongue vs heart (at-Tabari), Judaism and
Christianity mixed with Islam (Qatada, ar-Rabi', Ibn Jurayj), Torah vs what
was written by hand (Ibn Zayd, al-Muyassar); al-Baghawi's three readings.
Asbab: at-Tabari's Ibn Ishaq report from Ibn Abbas (three named men, morning
belief and evening denial, 3:71 to 3:73), chain doubt stated, no grading;
Mujahid on 3:72 via Ibn Kathir as context.
Hadith: none attached to the verses (stated). Bukhari 2079 (the two traders
who speak truth and make clear), marked general.
Licensing: verses address some People of the Book in Madinah then and license
nothing against any living person or community. 2:42 cited only as al-Qurtubi
and at-Tabari point back to it.
Dropped: al-Qurtubi's taktumu grammar note, Ibn Kathir en's generalising
heading, the 3:73-74 material.
