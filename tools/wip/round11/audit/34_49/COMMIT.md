Card and deep bilingual article on the arrival of truth and the total
impotence of falsehood: bāṭil can neither originate anything nor bring
back what has gone.

Sources (all fetched for 34:49; nothing from memory):
- al-ḥaqq read across the range the mufassirūn give: the Qur'an (Qatada
  via at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi), the Qur'an and revelation (at-Tabari),
  the Qur'an and Islam (al-Baghawi), the Qur'an and the great Law
  (Ibn Kathir, al-Muyassar), "the owner of the truth / the Book of
  proofs" (an-Nahhas via al-Qurtubi), and as-Saʿdi's truth-plain-as-the-sun.
- mā yubdiʾ … mā yuʿīd kept as the genuine two readings, no position:
  (a) al-bāṭil = Iblīs / the idols, who can neither create nor resurrect
  (Qatada, as-Suddi, Muqatil, al-Kalbi; Ibn Kathir calls it true but not
  the main intent); (b) once truth lands nothing of falsehood is left to
  begin or return (Ibn Kathir's preferred, al-Muyassar, as-Saʿdi, and
  al-Qurtubi's interrogative mā, cf 69:8). yubdiʾ/yuʿīd treated as the
  originate-and-restore pair per at-Tabari's glosses.
- 34:48 (yaqdhif bi'l-ḥaqq) and 21:18 frame "coming" as a throw that
  shatters (Maʿarif's qadhf note; Ibn Zayd via at-Tabari).
- 17:81 referenced as the twin verse; 34:49's distinct note is the
  creation-verb pair, not merely "vanishing". 17:81 is not shipped.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4287 (sahih, not upgraded) — Ibn Masʿud: the
Prophet ﷺ striking the 360 idols of the Kaʿba at the Conquest reciting
17:81 and 34:49. Chain (Mujahid > Abu Maʿmar > Ibn Masʿud) matches the
tafsir. Verse-apt.
