as-Sa'di reads "qad sadaqta" in three clauses: Ibrahim (AS) did what he was commanded, steeled
his resolve, carried out every preparatory step, and nothing remained but drawing the knife
across his son's throat — the command is praised as fulfilled one clause before the one act
that never happened. Al-Baghawi marks a sentence break after "sadaqta ar-ru'ya" and reads the
closing clause, citing Muqatil, as its own rule: the pardon from the slaughter is itself the
reward, repeated for every doer of good, not a one-time exception for a prophet. At-Tabari,
al-Qurtubi and al-Muyassar converge on the same widening, from Ibrahim's (AS) obedience to a
reward (deliverance from hardship, here and in the Hereafter) named for every muhsin. Ibn Kathir
(ar) reads the closing clause as the same "way out" mechanism of 65:2-3, and separately reports
that a group of usul al-fiqh scholars cited this verse and story as evidence that abrogation
before the time of acting is valid, against a faction of the Mu'tazila — kept as a named
disagreement, no position taken. Ma'arif al-Qur'an adds the servant's-side version of the same
mechanism. Ibn Kathir's English abridgment supplies a narrative layer: a report from Ibn Abbas
(RA) that the call came as the son's shirt was being removed for a shroud, and a separate report
from as-Suddi that the knife had already been drawn but did not cut, because of a copper sheet
between blade and skin — both kept, neither chosen between.

Hadith used: Sahih Bukhari, Dar-us-Salam 660 (Abu Huraira, the seven whom Allah will shade),
fetched and quoted to its first ten words, no grading claimed beyond its place in Sahih Bukhari,
and explicitly marked in the article as a general narration not drawn from this story, cited
only to illustrate as-Sa'di's definition of al-muhsinin.

Dropped: the entire Isma'il/Ishaq identity debate carried in the English Ibn Kathir file,
including the ram's-horns-in-the-Ka'bah report and Ibn Ishaq's argument from 11:71 — per the
brief, that question belongs to 37:112, so the article never names the son and says so plainly.
No asbab an-nuzul claim; none of the fetched tafsir proposed one for this verse.
