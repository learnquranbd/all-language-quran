Card and deep bilingual article for 21:3: heedless hearts and the
whisperers' two self-cancelling charges.

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi: lahiya = sahiya/ghafila, hearts
  turned from dhikr; Qurtubi traces it to lahaytu (to leave off and
  forget). as-Sa'di widens it to bodies at play and sets the opposite
  beside it.
- Qurtubi and al-Baghawi fix alladhina zalamu = those who did shirk;
  at-Tabari names them zalimun for deed and speech. Abu Ubayda's addad
  note (asarru = hid or made public) kept as a genuine ambiguity.
- First charge "just a man like you": Ibn Kathir (far-fetched that a
  human be singled out with wahy), at-Tabari (like you in form),
  Qurtubi (eats food, walks the markets), al-Baghawi (they demanded an
  angel), as-Sa'di (the whisper as a bid for leadership).
- Second charge "magic": Ibn Zayd via at-Tabari; as-Sa'di's campaign
  ("flee him and drive people away"); Ma'arif on the bind — they could
  not deny the Qur'an's power, so they relabelled it. Contradiction
  drawn out: Qurtubi's sihr = dressed-up unreality vs. "only a man."
- "While you see": Qurtubi's three readings + tawbikh; as-Sa'di's
  sharpest point (they knew he was truly the Messenger; obstinacy drove
  them).

Licensing: §7 states in both languages that the zalimun are the Makkan
deniers, that the verse describes a mindset and licenses nothing against
any living person or community, and that the account is Allah's, not
ours (as-Sa'di).

Kept 21:3 to mockery and heedlessness; did not give 21:7's mortal-
messenger answer and did not preempt 21:4-6.

Hadith: no sound narration attaches to 21:3. Bukhari 660 (checked) is
unrelated; the Bukhari surah-virtue report (Ibn Mas'ud) is noted once as
a virtue of the whole surah, not this verse. Asbab: none asserted;
placement used.
