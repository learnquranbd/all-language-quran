Card and deep bilingual article on the commissioning of Mūsā (AS):
God calls him directly and sends him to "the wrongdoing people,"
named in the next verse as the people of Pharaoh.

Sources give:
- at-Tabari: al-qawm al-ẓālimīn = the disbelievers, the people of
  Pharaoh; "qawm Firʿawn" (26:11) is accusative apposition explicating
  the wrongdoers of 26:10.
- al-Qurtubi: nidāʾ = address by name; two readings of "idh" — an
  implied "recite to them" (via an-Nahhas, with 26:69 as evidence) and
  an implied "remember." Kept as a difference, no position taken.
- al-Baghawi: the call at the tree and the fire; their ẓulm runs two
  ways — kufr against themselves and the enslavement of Israel.
- as-Saʿdi: God repeats Mūsā's story more than any other for its
  wisdoms; the wrongdoers exalted themselves and their chief claimed
  lordship.
- al-Muyassar: the mission is the question "will they not fear God and
  leave their kufr?" — reform, not doom.
- Ibn Kathir: called from the right side of the Ṭūr, spoken to, chosen,
  sent.

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2174 (Abū Saʿīd al-Khudrī), the greatest jihad is a
just word before a tyrannical ruler — graded ḥasan gharīb by Tirmidhī,
marked in-text as a general narration not attached to the verse.

§4 carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or
community" caution in both languages.

Dropped: Maʿarif (168) — nothing on 26:10 in fetched text; Ibn Kathir's
26:12-19 material withheld to avoid pre-empting the 26:12 / 26:22
targets. No sabab established; verse placement given instead.
