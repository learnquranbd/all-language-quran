Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the opening charge against ʿĀd:
the people denied "the messengers" (plural) though one man, Hūd, stood
among them — to deny one messenger is to deny them all.

Sources (all fetched for 26:123):
- at-Tabari: "ʿĀd denied the messengers of God sent to them."
- al-Muyassar and as-Sa'di: denying their one messenger Hūd was denying
  all the messengers, the call being one in its foundations and goal.
- al-Qurtubi: the feminine verb kadhdhabat is for al-qabīla / al-jamāʿa
  (the tribe as one collective body); "as has already come before" ties
  this opening to the Nūḥ passage at 26:105.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged), 26:123 framing only: al-Aḥqāf near
  Ḥaḍramawt / Yemen, a people after Nūḥ (al-Aʿrāf 7:69), great strength
  and stature, wealth, gardens and springs — and with all that, shirk.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3443 (Abu Hurayrah), "the prophets are paternal
brothers … their religion is one" — authentic by its collector, quoted
whole, grounding the deny-one = deny-all idiom.

No disagreement among the sources on this verse. al-Baghawi not used
(verse only); Ma'arif skipped (WRONG GROUP, 26:111–120).

Deliberately held back for later batches: 26:124 (brother Hūd / alā
tattaqūn), 26:127 (no wage), 26:128–130 (monuments, palaces, tyranny).
The surah frame is described generically, not as ʿĀd's own instance.

Dropped: ʿĀd of Iram / lofty pillars (89:6–8) — not in the fetched
tafsir, so not asserted. Carries the "licenses nothing against any
living person or community" sentence in both languages.
