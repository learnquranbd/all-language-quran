The verse names two endings for the emigrant on Allah's road — killed, or
died — and joins them with a single "or." The card and article build the
lesson on that pivot: the setting-out and the intention are what Allah
honours, so the one who dies undramatically on the path is promised the same
good provision as the one struck down in battle.

Sources (all fetched for this verse, none from memory):
- at-Tabari: "good" = noble/generous, the provision = abundant reward; best
  of providers = He who spreads His bounty over the people of His obedience.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): killed = in Jihad, died = ḥatfa anfihi on
  one's bed without fighting; cross-refs 4:100; provision = Paradise that
  brings joy to the eyes.
- as-Sa'di: reward due whether he dies on his bed or is killed; provision in
  the Barzakh then Paradise (rūh wa rayhan); second reading — worldly rizq is
  guaranteed too, and it came true for the early Muhajirun.
- al-Muyassar / al-Baghawi: provision = the Garden that never ceases; Baghawi
  adds the reading that it is 3:169 ("alive with their Lord, provided for").
- Ma'arif: rewarded for certain; if not in this world, then the Hereafter.

Asbab (attributed, from tafsir): at-Tabari (Companions disputed the ruling on
one who dies in Allah's path) and al-Qurtubi (deaths of Uthman ibn Maz'un and
Abu Salama ibn Abd al-Asad prompted it; verse equalized the two).

Disagreement kept open (al-Qurtubi): the Shariah's apparent sense gives the
slain a special merit, yet others held the two fully equal — stated as a
difference, no verdict.

Hadith: Sahih Bukhari 2788 (Umm Haram), quoted from one collection, graded
sound — she died falling from her mount, not killed, yet the Prophet ﷺ had
already placed her "among the first."

Dropped (see ledger): Fadalah ibn Ubayd and Salman al-Farisi narrations (no
confirmable canonical grading/number here) and the quttilū qira'at note.
