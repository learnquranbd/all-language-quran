Card plus an 8-section bilingual article on individual moral
accountability: no bearer bears another's burden, and even a beloved
relative cannot take on your load on that Day.

Sources (all fetched for this verse, nothing from memory):
- at-Tabari: "no sinning soul carries another's sin"; the
  muthqalah-is-feminine / nafs grammar point; warning benefits those who
  fear unseen, not sealed hearts; tazakkā as cleansing from kufr by tawbah.
- as-Sa'di: the hereafter is unlike the dunya where friend helps friend;
  fear in secret and open; the tazakkā content list (riyā', kibr, lying…).
- al-Qurtubi: the 'Ikrimah father/son/wife scenes and al-Fuḍayl ibn
  'Iyāḍ's mother scene; reads tazakkā as ihtadā (tied to 17:15).
- al-Baghawi + al-Muyassar: the relative named (father, mother, son,
  brother); Ibn Abbas's bare exchange.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): the whole verse set on the Day of
  Resurrection; each preoccupied with himself.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: reconciles 29:13 (twofold burden of the misleader
  does not lighten the misled's) and keeps intercession-by-leave outside
  the verse's refusal.

Refrain cross-light verified in data/quran-json: the phrase recurs at
6:164, 17:15, 39:7, 53:38. Refs kept to single verses (no ranges) for the
Bengali hyphen-range gate.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (the seven whom Allah shades) — the secret
giver and the one who wept remembering God alone — used to illustrate
"fearing the Lord unseen" and marked explicitly as NOT a sabab of this verse.

Dropped: ṣadaqa jāriya counter-example (no fetched source); al-Qurtubi's
extended i'rab and his Jew/Christian 'Ikrimah variant (unused). No scholarly
disagreement on this verse, so none manufactured. A light note records that
the verse licenses nothing against any person, family or community.
