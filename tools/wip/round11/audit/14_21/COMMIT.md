Card and eight-section bilingual article for 14:21 (1,794 EN words, 19
paragraphs): all come forth before Allah, the weak ask the arrogant whether
their following buys anything, and the leaders' reply that panic and patience
are now the same.

Sources: Tabari (with Ibn Jurayj, Ibn Zayd), Qurtubi, Sa'di, Ibn Kathir ar/en,
Baghawi (with Muqatil), Muyassar named. Ma'arif (168) is grouped 14:8-17 and
not used.

"Law hadana Allah": reported without a position. Qurtubi's three meanings
(faith; road to Paradise and rescue as "it is said"); Tabari's guidance to a
way of escape today; Muyassar and Baghawi on faith withheld; Ibn Kathir's
decree gloss; Sa'di's "we led you astray as we went astray" (echo of 28:63).
The article states that no fetched commentator weighs it as an argument.

Disagreements kept: who "they" are (all creation per Ibn Kathir and Muyassar,
the disbelievers per Tabari); where the exchange happens (in the Fire per Ibn
Kathir with 40:47-48, 7:38-39, 33:66-68; on the plain per Sa'di and Muyassar).
34:31-33 and 2:166-167 in the sister-verses place; 14:22 a single pointer.

Panic and patience reports: Ibn Zayd (Tabari's chain, weeping then patience);
al-Qurazi (patience then panic; Tabari's editor calls the chain ruined);
Muqatil via Baghawi (no chain); Qurtubi's "narrated from the Prophet" (no
chain or grading, not relied on). Hadith: none sound attached; said once.

The article states that the verse describes the Hereafter and licenses no
contempt for any living person or community.

Dropped: an "excuse, not a proof" framing (in no fetched text); Baghawi's long
Qurazi narrative beyond the one line used.
