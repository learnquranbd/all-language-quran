Card and deep bilingual article on the oath the Quraysh swore: that if a
warner ever came they would be more guided than any earlier nation — and the
flight (nufur) that answered when the warner finally came.

Sources (all confirmed before citing):
- at-Tabari and as-Sa'di on jahda aymanihim as the most solemn, strained oaths.
- al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi on the setting: the Arabs had faulted and cursed the
  People of the Book for belying their messengers, and longed for a prophet of
  their own — then fled the one they were given.
- Scope of "ihda al-umam" kept as a disagreement: the Jews and Christians
  specifically (al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi) vs every nation
  ever sent a messenger (Ibn Kathir, via ad-Dahhak). No position taken.
- nufur glossed as aversion plus flight (at-Tabari), distance from guidance
  (al-Baghawi), fresh error and obstinacy (as-Sa'di).
- Ibn Kathir's cross-references 6:156-157 and 37:167-170 (the same self-excuse)
  and, for context, 35:43's istikbar and the plot that recoils on its author.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari no. 1 (intentions), narrated 'Umar, quoted whole and
marked as a general principle, not a narration tied to the verse; paired with
35:38 (Knower of the breasts).

Dropped: Bukhari 660 (seven-shade hadith) — off-theme. Ma'arif tafsir — its text
is on 35:41, not this verse. No independent asbab asserted.

Licensing disclaimer in both languages: the verse records what those idolaters
did and licenses nothing against any living person, community or faith; the
mirror is the reader's own untested promises.
