Opens Surah Ta-Ha: the Qur'an was not sent down to distress the
Prophet. Card plus 8-section bilingual article, source-gated.

Sources give two readings of li-tashqa, kept as a live disagreement:
bodily exhaustion of long night prayer (Mujahid's rope image and 73:20,
in Tabari and Ibn Kathir; feet-swelling in Qurtubi and Ma'arif) versus
inward grief over the people's refusal (Qurtubi's gloss tied to 18:6).
Neither is chosen. Qatadah's "mercy, light, a guide to Paradise" and
as-Sa'di's "the whole din made easy, food for hearts, rest for bodies"
carry the positive side; Muyassar frames it as no burden beyond one's
power. Qurtubi's grammar of the lam and the root sense (anaa/taab, from
Qurtubi and Baghawi) anchor the meaning of the word.

20:1: the two letters left where the salaf left them — Ibn Kathir defers
to al-Baqarah; no meaning asserted. Balance section guards both sides:
ease that does not cancel striving, "your soul has a right over you" as a
limit in both directions. 20:3 named as the answer, not expounded.

Hadith: no sound narration is the direct occasion of the verse; stated
so. Included the general Sahihayn report Ibn Kathir attaches — Bukhari
71 (Mu'awiya), "whomever Allah wants good for, He gives understanding in
the religion" — quoted from Bukhari, marked general, not the sabab.

Occasion reports (Quraysh/ad-Dahhak, Baghawi's "it is said", Abu Jahl and
an-Nadr in Qurtubi) reported as unchained glosses, not established sabab,
with the mursal noted. Dropped: the en-abridged file's "Ibn Mas'ud" for
the yufaqqih hadith (Arabic Ibn Kathir has Mu'awiya, matching Bukhari 71).
