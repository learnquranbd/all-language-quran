Card and deep bilingual article on the two dwellings of an-Nahl 16:80:
the settled home as a place of rest (sakan) and the portable hide-tent
found light on the day of travel and the day of encampment.

Sources (all fetched for 16:80, none from memory):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir (ar+en), al-Baghawi,
  al-Muyassar, Ma'arif al-Qur'an.
- Animal mapping confirmed from the text: wool = sheep, fur = camels,
  hair = goats (Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi; al-Qurtubi masʾala 3 reads it
  as a grant of permission, and explains cotton/linen's absence).
- al-Qurtubi's house definition, quoted also by Ma'arif, which ties the
  verse to sakan in 30:21 and to peace of body and heart.

Disagreements kept as disagreements:
- athāth = wealth (Ibn ʿAbbas) / goods (Mujahid) / clothing (another
  chain); Ibn Kathir rules the correct sense broader than any one.
- "for a time" (ilā ḥīn): until death (Mujahid, al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi),
  until the furnishings wear out (al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi), until an
  appointed term (Ibn Kathir, al-Muyassar, at-Tabari). No source carried
  an "until the Hour" reading, so it was dropped.

Hadith: none attached to the dwelling theme in the fetched tafsir; said
so plainly rather than force an unrelated narration (the carrion-hide
fiqh reports in al-Qurtubi are noted, not used).

Kept on dwelling/shelter; did not preempt 16:81 or 16:83.
