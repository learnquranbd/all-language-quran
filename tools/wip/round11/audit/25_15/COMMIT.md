Closes the Hellfire scene of 25:11-14 with a deliberate either/or: is that
Fire better, or the Garden of Eternity promised to the muttaqin? Built the
lesson on the choice a life is actually making, made visible by naming the two
ends at once.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari: address to the deniers of the Hour; khuld = bliss that endures and
  does not perish; promised to whoever feared his Lord by obedience; jaza' =
  requital for deeds, masir = the destination they come to in the Hereafter.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): the Fire scene "that" points back to; the
  as-Saffat 37:62 Zaqqum parallel (same rhetorical either/or); 25:16 wa'dan
  mas'ula = a binding pledge that must inevitably come to pass; the "no eye has
  seen" description of Paradise.
- al-Qurtubi: how the Fire can be "weighed" though no good is in it (Sibawayh,
  an-Nahhas, Hassan ibn Thabit; the "dwellings" reading; the "according to your
  own belief, O disbelievers" reading); the three referents of "that" (the Fire,
  25:10, 25:8).
- as-Sa'di: the folly of choosing the harmful over the beneficial; whoever
  upholds taqwa is already promised it; masir = a refuge they return to and abide
  in forever.
- al-Muyassar: the God-conscious = those who fear their Lord's punishment.
- al-Baghawi: jaza' = thawab, masir = marja'.

Disagreements kept: al-Qurtubi's several readings of "that" and of the rhetorical
"better" are presented as layers/options, not resolved.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3244 (Abu Hurayra) — "I have prepared for My pious
slaves what no eye has seen..." with its own recitation of 32:17.

Dropped: Ma'arif al-Qur'an (grouped file treats only 25:9's objections, nothing
for 25:15). No asbab (none established; placement used instead). Tone kept
hopeful — the Garden framed as a promise, not the verse read as a threat.
