Card and bilingual article on the one question that settles nothing:
when is the Hour? The verse hands it back — its knowledge is only with
Allah, even the Prophet ﷺ was given no date — then turns the asker
around: perhaps it is near. Kept focused on the questioners and on
nearness as a summons to readiness, deliberately distinct from the
shipped 31:34 (the five keys), which it only cross-references.

Sources:
- at-Tabari: plain sense of the question; innamā / none knows but He;
  wa mā yudrīka = wa mā ash'araka; nearness paraphrase.
- al-Qurtubi: who asked (the hostile, out of istib'ād wa takdhīb);
  hiding the date does not invalidate prophethood; the two-fingers
  hadith; qarīban-not-qarība grammar (sā'ah as al-yawm, 7:56 parallel)
  and the alternate layta reading; hidden so the servant stays ready.
- al-Muyassar: askers' motive istib'ād wa takdhīb.
- as-Sa'di: motives (isti'jāl and takdhīb); the pivot — timing carries
  no benefit; what matters is who earns reward or punishment.
- al-Baghawi: gloss of mā yudrīka ("you do not know it").
- Ibn Kathir: Meccan precedent vs this Medinan verse; nearness
  parallels 54:1, 21:1, 16:1.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: mocking askers; live as if it is very close; the
  long worldly life will later seem short against Qiyamah.

Hadith (both Sahih al-Bukhari, sahih by placement, quoted whole/clause):
- Bukhari 6503 (Sahl), "I and the Hour like these two," in §5.
- Bukhari 50 (Jibril, Abu Hurayra), "the answerer knows no more than
  the questioner," in §4 (five keys kept out for 31:34 distinctness).

Disagreement kept: range of who asked (mocking deniers vs the merely
impatient) and the two readings of la'alla (statement vs wish) — left
open with both, no position forced.

Dropped: nothing substantive; no sabab asserted as graded.
