Replaces the 728-word, 6-section article with a 1,767-word, 8-section deep article
on Musa (AS) at the well of Madyan: he watered for the two women, turned to the shade
and said "My Lord, I am in need of whatever good You send down to me".

Sources: Ibn Kathir (ar and abridged en), at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi,
the Muyassar and Ma'arif al-Qur'an, all on 28:24.
- As-Sa'di: he watered seeking no wage, only the face of Allah; the shade marks midday heat.
- 'Umar's report via Ibn Abi Shayba (rock only ten men could lift, one bucket), with
  Ibn Kathir's own "isnad sahih"; marked as a Companion's report. Ma'arif's version
  and its link to "the strong" of 28:26.
- The samura tree (as-Suddi, Ibn Mas'ud); Ibn Mas'ud's camel visit to the tree.
- His hunger only as the reports give it (Ibn 'Abbas, Ibrahim, Qatada; seven days per
  al-Qurtubi and Ma'arif), with al-Qurtubi's lesson on how slight this world is to Allah.
- Khayr as food (Mujahid, Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn Zayd; al-Qurtubi: all the mufassirun), and
  al-Qurtubi's range of khayr (2:180, 100:8, 44:37, 21:73).
- Al-Baghawi on the lam meaning ila (faqir lahu / faqir ilayhi).
- Kept as named views: food (majority) vs any good (Muyassar, as-Sa'di) vs Abu Bakr
  ibn Tahir's inward reading, with al-Qurtubi's stated preference reported as his.
- Adab: allusion not outright asking (al-Qurtubi), asking by one's state (as-Sa'di),
  the women hearing it (at-Tabari, 'Ata' ibn as-Sa'ib).
No Prophetic hadith is attached to the verse by any fetched tafsir; said so in one line.
Dropped from the old article as unsourced: the faqar etymology, a claimed dispute over
the tense of anzalta (wrong: al-Qurtubi reports unanimity on food), the 35:15, 21:83
and 12:86 parallels, and word-order claims. Card unchanged.
