Mūsā's commissioning at Ṭuwā: the first word is tawḥīd. The verse
declares "I am Allah, there is no god but Me," then gives two commands,
worship Me and establish the prayer for My remembrance.

Sources: Ibn Kathir calls the declaration the first obligation on every
mukallaf. As-Saʿdī grounds it in Allah's perfection in names and
attributes and names the two halves, ulūhiyya of the Lord and ʿubūdiyya
of the slave. At-Tabari, Muyassar, Ibn Kathir and al-Baghawī read
fa'budnī as worship Me alone; as-Saʿdī widens it to every kind of
worship, outward and inward. As-Saʿdī and Ma'arif explain why the prayer
is named apart from worship: its rank, and that it is the pillar of the
faith whose neglect marks the disbeliever.

li-dhikrī kept as a live three-way disagreement, all attributed:
(a) pray in order to remember Me — Mujāhid via at-Tabari (his preferred,
grammatical), Muyassar, Ibn Kathir, as-Saʿdī; (b) so that I remember you,
praising you on high — al-Qurtubī, who also calls the prayer itself dhikr
(62:9); (c) pray whenever you remember a missed prayer — Ibrāhīm
an-Nakhaʿī via at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawī, al-Qurtubī.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 597 (Anas RA), quoted whole, which itself
recites 20:14 — the direct link the mufassirūn build on; al-Qurtubī's
qaḍā' obligation for the general body of scholars noted.

Ma'arif's structural reading kept every prophet's mission on tawḥīd
without preempting 20:15. Dropped: al-Qurtubī's ordering-of-prayers fiqh,
Zuhrī's variant reading, the parallel Sahihayn wording (no hybrid).
Card themes: tawheed, prayer.
