Card and deep bilingual article for 23:75 (surah al-Mu'minun), on the
hardened heart that relief does not soften: mercy given to a will already
set on transgression only returns it to transgression.

Sources (all confirmed against fetched tafsir on 23:75):
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): their hardness in kufr; even if God relieved
  their ailments and made them understand the Qur'an they would not submit.
  The "law" belongs to God's knowledge of what will not be, how it would be
  if it were; Ad-Dahhak from Ibn Abbas: everything with "law" never happens.
  Cross-refs 8:23 and 6:27-29 that Ibn Kathir himself adduces.
- at-Tabari: durr = drought, dearth, hunger and emaciation; "in their
  transgression" = their insolence and boldness against their Lord;
  yaʿmahun = they go to and fro. Ibn Jurayj: the affliction is hunger.
- al-Muyassar, al-Baghawi: durr = drought and hunger / barrenness;
  lajju = they carried on; yaʿmahun = bewildered and floundering; "they did
  not desist."
- al-Qurtubi: two readings kept as a difference — worldly relief (via Ibn
  Jurayj) vs. being returned to the world from the Fire and tested afresh;
  both land on persistence. as-Suddi: the transgression is disobedience.
- as-Sa'di: intensity of their rebellion; they pray under affliction to be
  relieved, then persist once relieved; the ship motif (sincere at sea,
  transgressing back on land). This is the article's spine, turned to
  self-examination.

Hadith: none. No fetched tafsir attaches a sound narration to 23:75; stated
in one sentence in the article rather than filling the slot with a weak or
unattached report (SOURCE GATE §2).

Asbab: not used; the affliction is left generic (drought/hunger) as the
mufassirun leave it — no dated Quraysh-famine event imported.

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or community"
statement in both languages (§8).
