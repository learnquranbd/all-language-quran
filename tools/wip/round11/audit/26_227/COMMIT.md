Tadabbur 26:227 closes Surah ash-Shu'ara' on the exception that
rescues the believing poets, then warns the wrongdoers.

Card + deep bilingual article (8 sections, 1,690 EN words), fully
translated into Bengali from the idea.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir: the verse is an istithnā' from 26:224-226,
  not a condemnation of poetry as such; four marks name the rescued
  (īmān, righteous deeds, dhikr kathīr, intiṣār after being wronged).
- as-Sa'di: the rescued poetry itself becomes a righteous deed —
  praising faith, defending the religion, teaching, urging character.
- al-Qurtubi: intiṣār is bounded redress "by truth and within Allah's
  limits"; exceeding it is vindicating falsehood. Prophet's rule kept:
  answer with truth only, no attack on lineage. Muqatil: the mushriks
  began it.

Disagreements kept (both names, no position):
- abrogation (ad-Dahhak←Ibn Abbas) vs exception (al-Mahdawi, the Sahih).
- dhikr kathīr "in speech" vs "in poetry" (Ibn Kathir: both sound;
  at-Tabari: in every state).
- the warning: Mecca's mushriks (at-Tabari) vs general to every
  wrongdoer (Ibn Kathir's sahīh view; Qatadah).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3212 quoted whole — "Answer on my behalf;
O Allah, support him with the Holy Spirit" (confirmed on quranx).

Asbab: Hassan/Ibn Rawaha/Ka'b narration reported as the commentators'
understanding, not as an established sabab — Ibn Kathir's mursal +
Makki caution carried in both languages.

Sensitive clause handled: the "licenses nothing against any living
person or community" sentence carried in both languages; intiṣār framed
as lawful defence, never aggression, revenge, or vigilantism.

Dropped: variant reading munqalab/munfalit, the 'Umrat al-Qada' rajaz,
the long nasab narration, and a second hadith — listed in LEDGER.
