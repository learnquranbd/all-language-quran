Card and 8-section bilingual article (1,772 EN words) on 56:91: fa-salamun
laka min ashab al-yamin. 56:88-90 framed as the dying person's ranks (Ma'arif);
56:92 touched only in the licensing sentence; 56:93-96 not discussed.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir (ar, en group 56:88-96),
as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar, Ma'arif (group 56:88-94).
- Addressee of laka kept open: the dying believer (Muyassar, Ibn Kathir,
  as-Sa'di, at-Tabari) or the Prophet (al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi).
- Who gives the salam: angels ('Ikrimah via Ibn Kathir, with 41:30-32),
  Allah and the angels (Qatadah via at-Tabari), fellow companions of the
  right (as-Sa'di); Ibn Zayd's wording differs between at-Tabari and
  Ibn Kathir, both shown.
- al-Qurtubi's three places of greeting (ad-Dahhak; Ibn Mas'ud's saying,
  marked chainless and a Companion's word; grave; rising) and his own
  "all three".
- Salam as safety: Muyassar, Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di, Muqatil via al-Baghawi;
  at-Tabari's stated preference (dropped innaka).
- Grammar: Basran/Kufan views and the du'a sense via at-Tabari; al-Bukhari's
  musallam gloss via Ibn Kathir; al-Mubarrad, al-Akhfash, az-Zajjaj on fa.
- Licensing sentence in both languages; no living group named.
Hadith: none attached to 56:91 in any fetched note; stated in one sentence.
Asbab: none.
Dropped: two minor grammarian views in at-Tabari (length); al-Qurtubi's
referent for "before he arrives"; own-voice claims on salam and 56:86-87.
- Review fix: Ibn Kathir's 'Ibn Jarir leaned towards it' sits after the prayer remark, so the article leaves open which reading it points to; comparison shortened.
