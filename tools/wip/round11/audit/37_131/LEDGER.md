# LEDGER — 37:131

## SOURCES (tafsir IDs fetched, this verse)

- t14 Ibn Kathir (ar) — fetched, named. Full text: "وقوله تعالى 'إنا كذلك نجزي المحسنين إنه من عبادنا المؤمنين' قد تقدم تفسيره والله تعالى أعلم." Used to show he cross-refers to his own earlier comments on the refrain (after Nuh/Ibrahim) rather than re-explaining; no new content here.
- t15 at-Tabari — fetched, named. Clause used: "إنا هكذا نجزي أهل طاعتنا والمحسنين أعمالا" — ties the reward to both obedience and the quality of deeds.
- t16 al-Muyassar — fetched, named (covers 37:129-132 grouped). Clause used: "وكما جزينا إلياس الجزاء الحسن على طاعته، نجزي المحسنين من عبادنا المؤمنين" — reads kadhalika as pointing back to Ilyas's own reward first, general rule second.
- t90 al-Qurtubi — fetched, named. Full text: "أي نبقي عليهم الثناء الحسن. والكاف في موضع نصب؛ أي جزاء كذلك. أو نجزيهم بالخلاص من الشدائد في الدنيا والآخرة." Used as two readings within his own commentary (good mention preserved / deliverance from hardship in both worlds) — presented as his own range, not a dispute with another named scholar.
- t91 as-Sa'di — fetched, named. Full text: "فأثنى الله عليه كما أثنى على إخوانه صلوات الله وسلامه عليهم أجمعين." Used directly: Allah praised Ilyas as He praised "his brothers" (the other prophets), tying the verse explicitly to the surah's prophet-to-prophet refrain.
- t94 al-Baghawi — fetched, named. Full text is the bare Qur'anic clause only, no comment added ("إنا كذلك نجزي المحسنين"). Used to note the thinness/lack of dispute, not for any substantive reading.
- t168 Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en) — fetched, read (covers 37:130-132 grouped). Content is entirely about the etymology of the name "Il Yasin" / "Ilyasin" (Arabic practice of adding ya'+nun to non-Arab names, e.g. Sina/Sinin). It does NOT make the point that the reward in 37:131 is a general law extended to any muhsin with prophets as supreme examples — that specific framing is not in the fetched text. Per the brief's explicit instruction not to assume this point, it was dropped from attribution to Ma'arif; the "general law, not exclusive to prophets" point used in the article's closing section rests only on the grammar of the plural al-muhsinin itself, stated in the article's own voice, not attributed to any named mufassir.
- t169 Ibn Kathir (en, abridged) — fetched, read (covers 37:123-132 grouped). Confirms the same cross-reference pattern as t14: "We have already discussed the meaning of this above." Used as the English-language corroboration of t14, not cited as a separate substantive voice.

## DROPPED

- Ma'arif al-Qur'an's "general law, not exclusive to prophets" framing suggested as a watch-point — checked, and the fetched t168 text does not contain it (it only covers the Il-Yasin name etymology for 37:130). Not attributed to Ma'arif anywhere in the article. The structurally similar point (al-muhsinin is a plain plural, unrestricted to prophets) is kept in the article's closing section but stated as a grammatical observation in the article's own voice, not sourced to any named mufassir.
- No disagreement between named scholars was found or manufactured. Al-Qurtubi's two glosses (good mention preserved / deliverance from hardship) are presented as his own two readings, explicitly not framed as a dispute with at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di or Ibn Kathir, since none of them contradicts it.

## DISAGREEMENT KEPT OPEN

None. All five Arabic mufassirun fetched (Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di) and al-Baghawi's bare quotation converge on the same reading: this is the surah's standing reward-refrain, applied once more, now to Ilyas (AS). The article states this convergence plainly (section 6, "A Settled Line, Not a Dispute") rather than inventing a disagreement the sources do not contain.
