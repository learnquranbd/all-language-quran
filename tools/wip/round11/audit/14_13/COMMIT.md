Card and eight-section bilingual article for 14:13 (1,676 EN words, 20
paragraphs): the peoples' oath of expulsion or return to their creed, and
their Lord's word to the messengers, We will surely destroy the wrongdoers.

Sources: Tabari, Qurtubi, Sa'di, Ibn Kathir ar/en, Baghawi, Muyassar named.
Ibn Kathir en (169) is grouped 14:13-17; only its 14:13 part is used, all of
it also in the Arabic (14). Ma'arif (168) covers 14:8-17 but its fetched text
treats 14:8 only, so it contributes nothing and the article says so.

"Return" question: the 14:13 commentators do not take it up; Qurtubi points
to al-A'raf, so Qurtubi and Ibn Kathir were fetched on 7:88. Answers given by
name, no position: tasirunna "become" (Qurtubi), the followers had been
disbelievers (Qurtubi, "it is said"), 'awd as beginning (az-Zajjaj via
Qurtubi), addressed to the messenger but meant for his followers (Ibn Kathir).

Disagreement kept: aw as "unless/until" (Tabari, Baghawi, Muyassar) against
aw of choice (Ibn al-'Arabi via Qurtubi); Qurtubi records both.
Also: Sa'di on "our land" and the messengers' plain right in it; Ibn Kathir's
parallels (27:56, 17:76, 8:30) and Makkah opened; Tabari's two senses of
al-zalimin. 7:88 and 14:14 are single pointers.

Hadith: none attached to the verse by any fetched tafsir; said in one line.
The article states that the verse describes past peoples and licenses no
expulsion, coercion or hostility by anyone today.

Dropped: the "disbelievers' assumption" answer (not in any fetched text);
169's 14:14-17 material; Shu'ayb's story beyond the 7:88 parallel.
