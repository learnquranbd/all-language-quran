Replaces the 694-word, 6-section article with a 1,770-word, 9-section article.

All eight fetched tafsirs are named. Al-Qurtubi takes insan as the genus (O son
of Adam) and reports, with "it is said", Muqatil's al-Aswad b. 'Abd al-Asad,
Ubayy b. Khalaf, and all disbelievers. He glosses kadh as work and earning, with
lines from Ibn Muqbil and another poet. Al-Baghawi: effort in good or evil until
it leaves its mark on the doer. Kadih ila rabbika: at-Tabari "working toward
your Lord", Ibn Kathir, al-Muyassar and as-Sa'di "sa'in"; ad-Dahhak from Ibn
'Abbas "returning, without fail"; al-Qutabi "toiling in your livelihood until you
meet your Lord"; Ma'arif "to meet your Lord". At-Tabari's exhortation and
Qatada's "your kadh is weak" are both quoted.

Disagreement kept: fa-mulaqih is "your Lord" (at-Tabari, ad-Dahhak/Ibn 'Abbas,
al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di, Ma'arif) or "your deed" (Ibn Kathir first, al-Qurtubi's
qila, al-Baghawi "its recompense", Ma'arif's kadh), plus al-Qurtubi's "the book of
your deed". Ibn Kathir calls the two views mutually entailing. No position taken.

Hadith: Bukhari 4939 ('A'isha on the easy reckoning of 84:8, attached by Ibn
Kathir) quoted whole; Muslim 2876 confirmed for "also records". Ibn Kathir's
at-Tayalisi narration from Jabir ("do what you wish, for you will meet it") is
cited as his witness, marked ungraded and unchecked. 84:7-12 has one section
with the licensing sentence; 84:12-13 and 69:19 get one line each.

Dropped from the old article: the unattributed lexicographers' gloss, the
claim that the cognate accusative intensifies, the hybrid paraphrase of the
'A'isha hadith credited to both Sahihs with no number, and the "easy means passed
over" gloss. Root k-d-h occurring only here was checked against the Qur'an text.
