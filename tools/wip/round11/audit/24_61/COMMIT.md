Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the two eases of the verse:
the lifting of haraj from the blind, lame and ill, and the wide
permission to eat in the homes of kin and friends, together or apart.

Sources (all fetched for 24:61):
- Ibn Kathir (ar): names the ikhtilaf over WHY the disability clause
  sits here — jihad exemption ('Ata' al-Khurasani, Ibn Zayd), scruple
  over eating with the disabled (Sa'id b. Jubayr, Miqsam; ad-Dahhak),
  Mujahid (taken to relatives' houses), as-Suddi (owner absent).
- al-Baghawi: the mirror view (the disabled drew back from the healthy),
  al-Hasan's jihad reading and his grammatical break at "the ill", the
  al-Harith b. 'Amr / Malik b. Zayd occasion for "your friend".
- as-Sa'di: religion with no haraj, eased to the limit; "your houses" =
  children's houses; keys = agency/guardianship (rejects the slave
  reading, two arguments); "greet yourselves" = one another, the umma as
  one person; mubaraka/tayyiba gloss.
- al-Muyassar, Ma'arif (en, permission conditioned on the owner's
  certain pleasure, not abrogated), Ibn Kathir abridged (en).

Disagreement kept open: the reason for the disability clause (duty-
exemption vs. table-scruple) is left unresolved, both sides named.

Hadith: Abu Dawud 3764 (Wahshi b. Harb), quoted whole under "together
or apart"; no grading shown on the fetched quranx page, said so, not
upgraded. Ibn Majah 3287 ('Umar) named for the principle only.

Dropped: the tashahhud-derivation report (Ibn Kathir flags Muslim
differs); al-Bazzar/Anas counsels (collection not confirmable on a
fetched page); Tabari and Qurtubi fetched but not cited by name.

Disability clause handled as inclusion, not pity: God addresses the
blind/lame/ill directly and lifts weight off them, names them before
kin, and the article states the verse gives no ground for treating them
as lesser guests or objects of charity.
