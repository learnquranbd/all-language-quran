Zakariyya's response to the glad tidings: how will I have a boy
when my wife is barren and I have reached extreme old age (itiyya).

The commentators read the question as wonder, not doubt: al-Qurtubi
states it is "not denial but astonishment at Allah's power" to bring
a child from a barren woman and an aged man; at-Tabari reads it as
seeking confirmation of the manner (min ayy wajh), his proof being
that Zakariyya himself had just asked for the heir; Ibn Kathir and
as-Sa'di frame it as joy after the plea was answered. at-Tabari's
as-Suddi narration of a whisper is reported but not adopted, and the
prophet is never portrayed as doubting.

Lexical core on itiyya: the withering/drying of great age — the dried
stick "ata ya'tu" (at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi), the thinning
of the bone (Mujahid, Qatada, Muyassar riqqat al-azm), Ibn Kathir's
"no fertility left," al-Qurtubi's triad kibar/yubs/jafaf, Ma'arif's
'dryness of the bones.'

Disagreement kept: itiyya as general old age (Ibn Abbas, ad-Dahhak)
vs. the specific bodily withering (Mujahid), with Ibn Kathir's note
that it is "more specific than old age"; plus the qira'at itiyya/asiyya
recorded as two dialects. Qatada's ~70-odd years included.

No sound hadith attaches to this verse in the eight tafsir fetched;
stated in one sentence in the article. Not an asbab verse; not a
condemned-group verse. 19:9 and 19:10 left as future ground.

Angle: faith meeting the humanly impossible — Allah's power runs over
the means, so the question asks how, not whether.
