Tadabbur 18:37 — the believing companion's rebuke inside the parable of the two
men (18:32-44): a card and an 8-section bilingual article.

Sources (all fetched for this verse, none from memory):
- at-Tabari: speaker is the poorer companion of 18:34; yuḥāwiruhu = addressing
  him; the three stages (turāb = Adam, nuṭfa = both parents, sawwāka = a sound
  male); akfarta aimed at denying being remade after becoming rufāt.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): the question is a denunciation (inkār) of juḥūd
  rabbihi and of the conceit that deceived him; Adam from clay, offspring from
  despised water; the origin is knowledge no one can honestly refuse.
- al-Muyassar: nuṭfa of the two parents; "who could begin creation can bring it
  back" (resurrection argument).
- al-Qurtubi: the believer's name is disputed (Yahūdha / Tamlīkhā) — kept as a
  disagreement; what he admits is more wondrous than the resurrection he denies.
- as-Sa'di: God moved him stage to stage; his wealth came by God's grace, not his
  own strength; ingratitude and resurrection-denial are one movement.
- al-Baghawi: turāb = origin, sawwāka = a well-formed male.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: wealth is no certificate of divine favour — it reaches the
  sinful and disbelieving alike; standing before God turns on faith and deeds.

Disagreement kept: the believer's name, left unsettled.

Hadith: none. No sound hadith attaches directly to 18:37; the treasure-of-Paradise
(Abu Musa) and "mā shāʾ Allāh" (Anas, Shuʿab al-Īmān) reports the tafsir carry
belong to 18:39 and are noted as such, not used — avoids preempting 18:38-39.

Dropped: those two narrations from the hadith slot; nothing asserted unconfirmed.
The arrogant garden-owner is handled as a parable type; the article states in both
languages that the verse licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Bengali ≤1 em dash/paragraph, number-parity clean.
