Two-ayah card (28:41-42) read as one arc: Pharaoh and his clique made imams
who call to the Fire and go unaided on the Day (41), then pursued by a curse
in this world and left among the maqbūḥīn on the Day (42).

Sources (all fetched on these verses):
- at-Tabari: imams the arrogant take as models, calling to the deeds of the
  people of the Fire; "not helped" reverses the mutual aid they had in dunya;
  on 42, disgrace/wrath/ruin/evil-name, with Qatada = Hūd 11:99.
- al-Qurtubi: leaders followed in kufr bear their own burden plus their
  followers', so punishment is greater; God commands servants to curse them.
- as-Sa'di: feeblest to repel the punishment, no wali/nasir but God; maqbūḥīn
  gather the detestation of God, of creation, and of themselves.
- al-Muyassar: leaders to the Fire imitated by people of kufr/fisq — and this
  "making" is BECAUSE of their kufr, denial of the messenger, persistence
  (anchors the qadar-is-just framing, not arbitrary).
- al-Baghawi: chiefs/heads; "not shielded from the punishment"; maqbūḥīn as
  the destroyed (Abu Ubayda) and disfigured (Ibn Abbas).
- Ibn Kathir: called the Creator-deniers who followed them; dunya disgrace
  joined to Hereafter humiliation (cf 47:13); cursed on believers' tongues.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: "call to fire" as metaphor vs Kashmiri's view that the
  deed is its own reward (18:49, 99:7).

Qur'an-internal contrast drawn in its own voice: the same word أئمة + جعلنا
used for guides in 32:24 and the oppressed-made-leaders in 28:5 — leadership
that summons upward vs downward. Attributed to no mufassir.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2674 (whoever calls to guidance/misguidance bears the
like of the followers' reward/sin), fetched on quranx; marked as a general
narration no fetched tafsir ties to the verse, used only for al-Qurtubi's
burden principle. Avoided the nearby stock hadith.

Carried the "licenses nothing against any living person, leader or community"
sentence in both languages: the verdict is Pharaoh's party's alone.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's naṣb-of-yawm grammar and his minor "lesson for the
insightful" gloss. No asbāb (narrative continuation, not an occasion).
