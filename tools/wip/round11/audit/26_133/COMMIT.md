Card and deep bilingual article for Hud's word to Aad: God "supplied
you with grazing livestock and children" — the first item in his
gratitude-argument (26:132-135), where provision is a summons to
thank and fear the Giver, not a licence for heedless security.

Sources (all fetched for this verse, none from memory):
- at-Tabari: amadda read as "aided/supplied"; anam = herds, banin = sons.
- al-Muyassar: anam = camels, cattle, sheep; banin = children.
- as-Sa'di: banin = a plenty of offspring, wealth and children multiplying;
  points above all to the males, reported as that society's valuation.
- al-Qurtubi: the pivot — He made this subject (sakhkhara) to you and favoured
  you, so He must be worshipped and thanked, not denied.
- Ibn Kathir: targhib/tarhib method, the conditional warning, "it did not
  benefit them."

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2963 (look to those below you, that you not belittle
God's favour) — sound by Muslim's own standard, marked as general guidance on
gratitude and NOT attached to the verse by any mufassir. Chosen to avoid the
nearby Bukhari 660 and Bukhari 3443 (26:123).

Fairness: states in both languages that Aad were condemned and destroyed
(26:139) yet this verse lists God's gifts to them while they could still turn,
and licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Dropped: al-Baghawi's gardens/rivers and al-Muyassar's springs (26:134, not
preempted); the Ma'arif/Tirmidhi construction hadith (belongs to 26:128-129);
any lexical claim about amadda's morphology not in the fetched text.
