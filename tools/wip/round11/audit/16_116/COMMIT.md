Card and deep bilingual article on the verse forbidding self-authorized
rulings: do not say "this is lawful and this is unlawful" to invent lies
about Allah. Angle kept distinct from the shipped 16:114 article (halalan
tayyiban / the four forbidden foods) — here the subject is arrogating
legislation and iftira' 'ala Allah al-kadhib.

Sources (all fetched for this verse): at-Tabari and al-Baghawi on the
masdar reading (they permit/forbid only for the tongue's lie); at-Tabari
on the qira'at of al-kadhib (accusative by consensus, al-Hasan al-Basri's
genitive kept as a difference); al-Qurtubi mapping the two labels onto the
bahirah/sa'ibah and the carrion they allowed, plus his principle that
tahlil and tahrim belong to Allah alone; Ibn Kathir on the jahiliyya
customs and the widening to anyone who rules by whim; al-Muyassar and
al-Baghawi on the false attribution to Allah; as-Sa'di ("do not forbid and
permit from your own selves"; Allah will expose their disgrace); Ma'arif
on why 16:115's innama does not cap the haram (cf. 6:145).

Reports: no sound marfu' hadith attaches directly to the verse — said so
in the article rather than forcing one. Used al-Qurtubi's Salaf athar
instead (Ibrahim an-Nakha'i via ad-Darimi; Malik's caution; the ijtihad
qualifier with riba), marked as reports of scholars' scruple, not
Prophetic rulings. Bukhari 660 fetched and dropped as off-verse.

Licensing sentence included in both languages: the verse condemns the
habit, passes no verdict on any living person or community, and the wide
reading is turned toward the reader's own tongue.
