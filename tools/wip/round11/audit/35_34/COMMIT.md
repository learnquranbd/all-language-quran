Tadabbur 35:34 — the first words of the saved are hamd, and the grief
they name as lifted (al-ḥazan) is what Paradise is partly defined by.

Card: 125-word reflection on the people of Paradise praising Allah first
for removing their grief; 5 questions; themes gratitude + mercy.

Article, 8 sections, bilingual:
- al-ḥazan read wide — Ibn Kathir (fear of all that is dreaded; the
  worries of dunyā and ākhira) and at-Tabari (fear of the Fire, dread of
  death, dread of need; no single kind singled out). Qatada: they toiled
  in fear in the dunyā.
- al-Baghawi's catalogue of early readings (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatada, Muqatil,
  ʿIkrima, al-Qasim, Saʿid b. Jubayr, az-Zajjaj).
- Disagreement kept: at-Tabari leans to the general sense; al-Qurtubi
  (citing ath-Thaʿlabi) ties al-ḥazan to the three classes of 35:32 and
  calls that the sound reading. Both reported, no verdict imposed.
- as-Saʿdi: no grief from any deficiency; bliss ever-increasing — Paradise
  defined by the absence of grief.
- Maʿārif: the dunyā as the home of sorrows; al-Jassās on the believer's
  standing concern.
- The two names as the heart: Ghafūr covered the shortfall, Shakūr prized
  and multiplied the little (Ibn ʿAbbās via Ibn Kathir; al-Muyassar;
  as-Saʿdi's pairing — saved from all dreaded, given all desired).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6463 (Abu Hurayra) — deeds do not save; only
Allah's mercy — confirmed via hadith.js (first fetch timed out, retried),
quoted whole, grading Sahih. Ties to Ibn Kathir on 35:35 ("our deeds are
not equivalent to this").

Dropped: the Ibn ʿUmar "no waḥsha … al-ḥamdu lillāh" narration (Ibn Abī
Ḥātim/at-Tabarani; chain through ʿAbd al-Raḥmān b. Zayd b. Aslam, weak,
not in the six books) — not asserted. No sabab; placement used instead.
