Card and deep bilingual article on 25:8 — the disbelievers' false
test of prophethood: they demand an angel, a treasure, or a garden,
and dismiss the followers as duped by a "bewitched man." The lesson:
a message is judged by whether it is true, not by the wealth or
spectacle around its bearer.

Sources: Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di,
al-Baghawi (Arabic), Ibn Kathir abridged (English). The treasure =
wealth to spend, sent from heaven so he need not seek a living
(Baghawi, Sa'di "without effort", Ibn Kathir); the garden travels
with him and feeds him (Ibn Kathir, Tabari, Sa'di).

Disagreement kept: the qirā'a of يأكل / نأكل — Madinah/Basrah/some
Kufans read yāʾ (he eats), general Kufans and Hamzah/al-Kisāʾī read
nūn (we eat); Tabari and Qurtubi (via an-Nahhas) prefer yāʾ but keep
nūn as valid. مسحور kept open across bewitched (Muyassar), deceived,
and turned-from-truth (Baghawi), with bewitched foremost.

الظالمون: Sa'di — they spoke from wrongdoing, not confusion, knowing
his sound intellect. Carries the plain "licenses nothing against any
living person or community" statement in both languages.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2564c ("Allah does not look to your faces and
your wealth but to your hearts and deeds"), quoted whole and marked
as a general narration NOT attached to the verse by any tafsir.

Dropped: the "suḥr = lungs / a man who merely eats" gloss (in no
fetched tafsir); Qurtubi's naming of ibn az-Zibaʿrā as speaker (weak,
not needed). No sabab confirmed; placement written instead.
