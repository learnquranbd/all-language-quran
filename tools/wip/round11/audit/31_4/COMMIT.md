Opening Sūrat Luqmān, 31:4 describes the muḥsinīn named in 31:3: those who
establish prayer, give zakāh, and are certain of the Hereafter. The article's
angle is the triad as the definition of iḥsān at the sūrah's opening, with
yaqīn as the root that binds the two outward acts together.

Sources (all fetched for this verse, nothing from memory):
- al-Qurtubi: alladhīna is the ṣifa of al-muḥsinīn (rafʿ by qaṭʿ / naṣb by
  implied aʿnī); discussion already passed in al-Baqara — so 31:4 is still the
  same sentence, defining the muḥsin, not a new group.
- at-Tabari / Ibn Kathir / al-Muyassar: iqāmat aṣ-ṣalāh = establishing the
  obligatory prayer within its limits and times (plus nawāfil, Ibn Kathir);
  zakāh made obligatory in wealth, to its deserving recipients; Ibn Kathir adds
  joining kinship ties and sincerity (no riyāʾ, no wanting thanks).
- as-Saʿdī: yaqīn = complete knowledge, al-mūjib li'l-ʿamal — the certainty that
  necessitates action; two excellent deeds; zakāh purifies the giver and
  prefers love of Allah over love of wealth. Spine of the "root" section.
- Ma'arif al-Qurʾān: zakāh enjoined in this Makkī verse — the injunction
  preceded the Hijra; niṣāb/collection organized the second year after; prayer
  and zakāh paired throughout (Ibn Kathir on 73:20).

Hadith: Jibrīl hadith, Sahih al-Bukhārī no. 50 (sahih, not upgraded) — defines
all three marks (islām: prayer + zakāh; īmān: the Resurrection = yaqīn; iḥsān:
worship as if you see Him) and closes with the Prophet reciting 31:34, from
this very sūrah.

Referenced the shipped 29:45 ("Establish, Not Perform") in one line without
