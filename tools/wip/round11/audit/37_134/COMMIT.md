The rescue closes the Lut episode's opening half: "when We saved him and his
family, all" (ajma'in). At-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di and al-Baghawi all
confirm the same plain sense with no real divergence — one act of Allah,
deliverance for the household, punishment for the town. As-Sa'di's own
addition is the mechanism (they traveled by night and were saved);
al-Baghawi's note is the bare verse, repeated without comment.

Read against 37:135's exception ("except an old woman"), the apparent tension
is resolved the classical way: ajma'in affirms every actual believer in the
household was brought out whole; the exception then names the one person who,
despite sharing the house, was never part of that saved group.

Al-Qurtubi fills in the narrative — night departure (cross-ref 11:81),
Jibril's role lifting and inverting the cities, a stone that "caught up with
his wife, who was with him, and killed her." Al-Muyassar instead frames her
end in terms of her disbelief (li-kufriha), not the stone. Both are kept, not
ranked, reconciled through the Qur'an's own words at 11:81 ("except your
wife... what befalls them will befall her") rather than forcing one account
over the other. 66:10 added as Allah's own characterization (ghadarat) of why
she wasn't saved. Dropped: al-Qurtubi's city-count/population figures — his
own report, not load-bearing.

Licensing guard applied in two places given the sensitivity of a
destroyed-people story, even though no specific wrongdoing is itemized here.

Hadith: Bukhari 660 (the seven shaded on the Day) checked per the stock
fallback, quoted in full, found unrelated to Lut or this rescue, and dropped
with that said plainly in the article. No tafsir fetched attaches a hadith to
this verse. No asbab applies; none claimed.

(destruction and the ruins), 37:139+ (Yunus).
