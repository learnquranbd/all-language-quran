Tadabbur 37:125 — Do you call upon Ba'l and leave the best of creators?

Ilyas's confrontation with his people over Ba'l, kept as a genuine four-way
named dispute rather than forced to one answer:

- rabb/lord, Yemeni dialect (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatada, as-Suddi —
  Ibn Kathir ar, at-Tabari, al-Baghawi)
- a specific idol, source of the name Ba'albakk ('Abd ar-Rahman b. Zayd b.
  Aslam from his father, ad-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd — at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir ar)
- a worshipped woman, Ibn Ishaq from unnamed people of knowledge, reported
  by at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir only as "it is said" — kept as minority
- a king, Tha'lab's third camp — al-Qurtubi only, kept as minority

Al-Qurtubi's two Ibn Abbas chains (idol via al-Hakam b. Aban/Ikrimah; rabb
via Ata' b. as-Sa'ib/Ikrimah) are shown resolved by an-Nahhas — "an idol they
had taken as a rabb" — so the two reports read as complementary, not a raw
contradiction, without that reconciliation collapsing the larger dispute.

Al-Qurtubi's Muqatil-sourced idol description (gold, twenty cubits, four
faces, 400 attendants, Satan said to speak from inside it) is kept as a
single-source detail, clearly attributed, kept small.

At-Tabari's Ahab/famine narrative is used only as far as the famine, the
idols' failure to answer, and the people's non-repentance after rain — all
marked as reported narration. Its ending (Ilyas taken up, clothed in
feathers and light, becoming part-angel) was read and dropped entirely, not
even alluded to — unconfirmed, theologically loaded isra'iliyyat.

Ma'arif al-Qur'an and al-Qurtubi both anchor the khaliq-vs-sani distinction:
only Allah creates from nothing, so "ahsan al-khaliqin" never sets up real
rivals. As-Sa'di's contrast (the idol's total powerlessness vs. Allah's
visible and hidden provision) closes the piece.
