# LEDGER — 37:125

## SOURCES (tafsir IDs fetched, this verse)

- t14 Ibn Kathir (ar) — fetched, named. Clause: "قال ابن عباس، ومجاهد، وعكرمة، وقتادة، والسدي: (بعلا) يعني: ربا... وقال عبد الرحمن بن زيد بن أسلم، عن أبيه: هو اسم صنم كان يعبده أهل مدينة يقال لها: بعلبك... وقال ابن إسحاق: أخبرني بعض أهل العلم أنهم كانوا يعبدون امرأة اسمها بعل."
- t15 at-Tabari — fetched, named (rabb camp with full chain list; idol camp via ad-Dahhak/Ibn Zayd; woman camp via Ibn Ishaq; the Ahab/famine narrative via Ibn Ishaq←Wahb b. Munabbih). Clause used for the drought narrative stops at: "فلم ينـزعوا ولم يرجعوا، وأقاموا على أخبث ما كانوا عليه." The narrative's continuation — Ilyas asking to be taken away, and the horse-of-fire / feathers-and-light / "إنسيا ملكيا أرضيا سماويا" ending — was read in full and deliberately NOT used anywhere in the article, per the standing rule against unconfirmed, theologically loaded isra'iliyyat.
- t16 al-Muyassar — fetched, read (covers 37:124-126 grouped). Content is a plain restatement overlapping the rabb/idol material already sourced from Ibn Kathir/Tabari/Baghawi; not separately cited in the article to avoid redundant attribution.
- t90 al-Qurtubi — fetched, named. Clauses used: Tha'lab's three camps ("فقالت طائفة: البعل هاهنا الصنم. وقالت طائفة: البعل هاهنا ملك. وقال ابن إسحاق: امرأة... والأول أكثر"); the two Ibn Abbas chains ("وروى الحكم بن أبان عن عكرمة عن ابن عباس... صنما" / "وروى عطاء بن السائب عن عكرمة عن ابن عباس... ربا"); an-Nahhas's reconciliation ("والقولان صحيحان، أي: أتدعون صنما عملتموه ربا"); the Muqatil idol description ("مقاتل: صنم كسره إلياس وهرب منهم. وقيل: كان من ذهب، وكان طوله عشرين ذراعا، وله أربعة أوجه... أربعمائة سادن...").
- t91 as-Sa'di — fetched, named. Clause: "ينهاهم عن عبادتهم صنما لهم يقال له بعل... صنم لا يضر، ولا ينفع، ولا يخلق، ولا يرزق، بل لا يأكل ولا يتكلم."
- t94 al-Baghawi — fetched, named. Clause: "وهو اسم صنم لهم كانوا يعبدونه، ولذلك سميت مدينتهم بعلبك. قال مجاهد وعكرمة وقتادة: البعل الرب بلغة أهل اليمن."
- t168 Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en) — fetched, named. Clause used: the khalq-vs-making distinction, and the explicit line that calling a writer's or painter's work "creation" is "simply not permissible."
- t169 Ibn Kathir (en, abridged) — fetched, read; corroborates t14/t15 (rabb camp with same five names, idol camp via 'Abdur-Rahman b. Zayd and ad-Dahhak). Used as the English-language cross-check for t14/t15; not cited as a separate voice since it restates the same ar report.

## DROPPED

- At-Tabari's narrative ending: Ilyas asking Allah to be taken from his people, then being approached by "a horse of fire," being clothed in feathers and light, and becoming (the narrative's own phrase) "a human who is angelic, earthly and heavenly." Read in full, dropped entirely per standing instruction — unconfirmed, semi-legendary isra'iliyyat with a theologically loaded claim (a prophet's part-angel transformation). Not alluded to in any form, including as a "some say."
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an's aside that "some people think Hubal... was this very Ba'l" — a speculative identification with a pre-Islamic Hijazi idol, not corroborated by any other fetched source and not tied to a named scholar beyond "some people think." Dropped as too thin to attribute.
- The precise mechanics of the Tabari drought narrative beyond the famine/idol-test/rain sequence (e.g., Ilyas's concealment, the widow and her son al-Yasa' being healed) — read, but left out as narrative detail not needed to make the point and at risk of over-elaborating isra'iliyyat-pattern material; only the famine-as-sign and idols-fail-to-answer core was kept, clearly attributed as "reported."

## DISAGREEMENT KEPT OPEN

Four named readings of Ba'l, none resolved in the article's own voice:
1. rabb/lord, Yemeni dialect — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, Ikrimah, Qatada, as-Suddi (Ibn Kathir ar, at-Tabari, al-Baghawi).
2. a specific idol, source of the name Ba'albakk — 'Abd ar-Rahman b. Zayd b. Aslam (from his father), ad-Dahhak, Ibn Zayd (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir ar).
3. a worshipped woman — Ibn Ishaq from unnamed people of knowledge, reported by both at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir only as "it is said."
4. a king — Tha'lab's third camp, al-Qurtubi only.
Separately noted: al-Qurtubi's two Ibn Abbas chains (idol vs. rabb) and an-Nahhas's reconciliation ("an idol they had taken as a rabb") — used to show the two Ibn Abbas reports are not a raw contradiction, without collapsing the larger four-way dispute.
