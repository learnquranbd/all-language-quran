The white hand: the second sign given to Moses before Pharaoh, and the
clause that guards it from being read as disease.

Sources (all fetched for 27:12, none from memory):
- at-Tabari, al-Baghawi: the jayb is the breast-opening of a sleeveless
  wool midraʿa; Mujahid (the palm alone), Ibn Masʿud (the wool jubba).
- at-Tabari glosses "min ghayri suʾ" as "min ghayri baraṣ" (without
  leprosy); as-Sa'di "no leprosy, no defect, but a whiteness whose
  radiance dazzles"; al-Muyassar "white as snow"; al-Baghawi/Ibn Kathir
  "like a piece of the moon / a flash of lightning". Article states
  plainly, both languages: a shining miracle, not leprosy or any disease.
- "among nine signs": Ibn Kathir (staff + hand = two of nine, tied to
  17:101). Enumeration kept as reported views, not a fixed list:
  Ibn Zayd via at-Tabari; al-Qurtubi; al-Muyassar — shared core, moving
  edges. an-Nahhas / al-Mahdawi / al-Farra' on the grammar.
- fāsiqīn: at-Tabari (disbelievers), al-Qurtubi (left God's obedience),
  as-Sa'di (shirk, insolence, arrogance). Carried the sentence that the
  verse licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2733 (Safwan b. ʿAssal, "nine signs"), his own grading
hasan sahih — used as one marked sentence explaining 17:101, not attached
to 27:12. Bukhari 4981 dropped as stock (reused in 3 nearby drafts). No
commentator attaches a hadith to 27:12 itself; said so in one sentence.

Tadabbur: God equips His messenger with overwhelming, clear proof before
sending him to tyranny; the sign is made to leave no honest excuse.
Did not touch 27:13–27:14 (future ground).
