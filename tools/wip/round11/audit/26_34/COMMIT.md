Pharaoh, unable to deny the staff-serpent and the shining hand (26:32-33),
relabels the sign "magic" and Mūsā (AS) "a learned magician" (26:34), spoken not
to Mūsā but to the chiefs around him. Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the
tyrant's deflection: reframe truth as trickery, discredit the messenger, keep the
court and the throne.

Sources (all confirmed on fetched text, nothing from memory):
- at-Tabari: al-mala' = the nobles of his people around him; the charge = "he
  enchanted his staff so you saw it a serpent"; ʿalīm = knowledge of and insight
  into magic.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): sāḥir ʿalīm = accomplished master; Pharaoh
  "promoted it as magic, not miracle," then incited the chiefs; proof had been
  established rationally before he resorted to relabelling.
- al-Muyassar: he spoke to the nobles "out of fear that they would believe";
  māhir, skilled. "Drive you out" cited as 26:35, not 26:34.
- as-Sa'di: he spoke "opposing the truth and the one who brought it."
- al-Qurtubi (no commentary for the verse) and al-Baghawi (verse only) fetched
  but not named. Ma'arif (168) skipped — wrong group.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 91a (Ibn Mas'ud), "pride is disdaining the truth and
contempt for the people," used and clearly marked as a general narration not
attached to this verse; article states no tafsir attaches a hadith here.

Irony (a true miracle named magic) drawn as the article's own reflection, not
attributed to any mufassir. Carries the "licenses nothing against any living
person or community" note for Pharaoh as a named tyrant. Does not pre-empt
26:36/44.
