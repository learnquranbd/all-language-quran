# SOURCES 3:164

## Key and window
Key kept as 3:164 alone. 3:165 (qul huwa min 'indi anfusikum) was not taken in: no tafsir
on it was fetched, and it is left free as its own key (3:161-163 ghulul also left free).
3:165 is cited only as Qur'an text (its question "from where is this?" and "it is from
yourselves"), with no reading attributed and no 4:78-79 discussion.

## Disagreements kept
- Who the believers are: Arabs specifically (Aishah report in al-Qurtubi; al-Muyassar; the
  "every clan but one" reasoning + 62:2 in al-Baghawi and al-Qurtubi) vs all believers
  (al-Baghawi: by faith and compassion, not lineage; al-Qurtubi: one of them, a human, set apart
  by revelation; both 9:128). No position taken.
- in: emphatic (wa laqad kanu; Ahsanul Bayaan: lightened inna) vs negative in + la = illa
  (al-Qurtubi, Kufan grammarians).
- al-kitab: Qur'an (then ayatihi = signs in creation) or writing (as-Sa'di). al-hikmah: Sunnah
  or putting things in place / secrets of the Shari'ah (as-Sa'di).

## Hadith
- No fetched tafsir attaches a sound Prophetic hadith to 3:164; the article says so in one
  sentence. (The Aishah statement in al-Qurtubi is a mawquf report, given with his chain and no
  grading.) The ghulul hadiths in t169 belong to 3:161 and are not used.
- Bukhari 4330 ('Abdullah ibn Zayd ibn 'Asim, Hunayn sermon to the Ansar). URL
  https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-4330 (saved hadith_Bukhari_4330.txt).
  First ten words (Arabic of the quoted part): يَا مَعْشَرَ الأَنْصَارِ أَلَمْ أَجِدْكُمْ ضُلاَّلاً فَهَدَاكُمُ اللَّهُ بِي وَكُنْتُمْ مُتَفَرِّقِينَ.
  In Sahih al-Bukhari; no separate grading given or added. Marked in both languages as NOT tied
  to this verse. Quoted as one contiguous segment of Bukhari's own wording (the sermon's opening
  three lines and the refrain كلما قال شيئا قالوا الله ورسوله أمن), translated from the Arabic;
  the rest of the narration (sheep and camels, shi'ar/dithar, wait for me at the Hawd) is
  summarised as setting or left out. No Muslim variant mixed in. The root link amann / manna
  is a plain lexical observation in the article's own voice.
