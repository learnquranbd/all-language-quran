## Disagreements kept as disagreements
- Who spoke: the seventy of 7:155 (Ibn Ishaq, as-Suddi, ar-Rabi'; al-Baghawi; al-Qurtubi as `قيل`) vs the people at large on the Tablets' return (Ibn Zayd, in at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir). At-Tabari: no report establishes any cause.
- What the sa'iqa was: death / a sound / a cry from the sky / fire / the quaking (Qatada, ar-Rabi', Marwan, as-Suddi, Ibn Ishaq).
- Real death or swoon: as-Sa'di both open; at-Tabari death in 2:56 despite his 7:143 point; al-Qurtubi rejects the figurative "knowledge after ignorance" reading.
- wa antum tanzurun: four readings (at-Tabari, Urwa, as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi's `وقيل`).
- Duty after revival: al-Mawardi's two views, al-Qurtubi's preference given as his.

## Seeing Allah (watch-point)
The verse's fault is given as the condition on belief (as-Sa'di, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Furak). This-world impossibility from Ibn Kathir (Ibn Jurayj) and Ma'arif. 7:143 used only as the tafsir uses it: at-Tabari for the meaning of sa'iq (a swoon, Musa recovered), Ibn Kathir to reject the claim that the seventy saw Allah. 75:22 and 75:23 quoted as Qur'anic text only, as a Hereafter scene, no argument built on it. Al-Qurtubi's `فأكثر المبتدعة على إنكارها ... وأهل السنة والسلف على جوازها` NOT used: only "scholars differed, he defers it to al-An'am and al-A'raf". No sect named.

## Hadith
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6312, https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-6312 (saved as hadith_Bukhari_6312.txt). Hudhayfa. Arabic first ten words: `كَانَ النَّبِيُّ صلى الله عليه وسلم إِذَا أَوَى إِلَى فِرَاشِهِ قَالَ "بِاسْمِكَ`. English used (my rendering of that Arabic, whole): "when the Prophet ﷺ went to bed he would say, In Your name I die and I live; and when he rose he would say, Praise be to Allah, who gave us life after He had caused us to die, and to Him is the rising." Grading: in al-Bukhari's Sahih; no separate grading. Marked in the article as a general narration, not tied to the verse.
- No fetched tafsir attaches a hadith to 2:55 or 2:56; the article says so in one sentence.
