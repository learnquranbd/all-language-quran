Two-verse entry covering both the delegation (85) and the driving (86).

The contrast is the whole point: the muttaqin gathered ila ar-Rahman as a
wafd — an honoured, mounted delegation received under the name of mercy —
against the mujrimin driven (sawq) to Hell wirdan, parched, like thirsty
cattle to a watering-place that turns out to be the Fire. Card and an
8-section bilingual article, 1,570 EN words.

Sources (all fetched for the verse): at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir gloss wafd as
the mounted arrivers; al-Qurtubi's envoy-to-a-king sense and the Arab
delegation that comes with glad tidings and awaits gifts; the ila ar-Rahman
ellipsis (to His Paradise / abode of honour, cf. 37:99); as-Sa'di on the
delegate's hope and good expectation; al-Baghawi's jama'at. For 86, Ibn
Kathir's wirdan = 'itashan (Ata', Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, al-Hasan, Qatada) and
al-Muyassar's "on foot, thirsty." Ibn Kathir's link to the boast of 19:73
("which party is better in standing") frames the reversal.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6527 (Aisha, gathered barefoot/naked/uncircumcised)
used as al-Qurtubi uses it — GENERAL to the resurrection, not attached to the
wafd, placing the mounts at the mawqif after the bare gathering.

Grading kept honest: the vivid she-camel / gold-saddle narrations are graded
weak (Ibn al-Arabi: isnad unsound) or "very strange" (Ibn Kathir prefers the
mawquf); §7 states plainly that no fully sound hadith is quoted whole on the
honoured arrival itself.

Licensing: the mujrimin are a condemned class in the verse; §8 carries the
standing sentence in both languages — licenses nothing against any living
person or community, names no one now. 19:87 (intercession/covenant) named
only as future ground, not explained.
