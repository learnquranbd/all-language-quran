Assigned 4:97, widened to 4:97-99 (those the angels take while wronging themselves;
"we were weak in the land"; was not Allah's earth wide; the truly weak men, women and
children; 'asa and 'afuwwan ghafura). 4:100-101 left free. New card and a 1,750-word,
9-section article.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir (ar + en), as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi,
al-Muyassar on 4:97, 4:98 and 4:99, all slots on target; Ma'arif's slot is 4:96 only.
No takfir: §4 gives the commentators' judgements by name, then licenses nothing against
anyone living, no leave to call any Muslim a disbeliever or anyone an inmate of the Fire,
with as-Sa'di's cause/impediment rule. §8 reports the hijrah fiqh by name; in its own
voice the article tells no reader to emigrate, judges no Muslims anywhere, and sends the
question to a qualified scholar.
Kept as differences, no position:
- their state at death: died Muslims wronging themselves (al-Qurtubi, "qila" apostasy
  view judged weaker) vs hypocrites (ad-Dahhak) vs killed as disbelievers (Ibn Jurayj).
- hilah as money (as-Suddi) / plan, provision, strength (al-Baghawi) / every means
  (al-Qurtubi); sabil the road to Madinah (Mujahid, as-Suddi) vs every way (al-Qurtubi).
- hijrah still binding on the able who cannot practise (Ibn Kathir, as-Sa'di;
  al-Qurtubi's land of sins) vs abrogated after the conquest (al-Baghawi's "qila").
'Asa from Allah binding (Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di); al-Qurtubi's "no sin to
pardon" answer; at-Tabari on 'afuww and ghafur. Closing door: at-Tabari's Ibn 'Abbas
report (29:10, then 16:110), given as a report.
Hadith: Bukhari 4596 (occasion, whole, translated from the Arabic without the English
interpolations); 4588 (Ibn 'Abbas and his mother, whole); 4598 (the qunut for 'Ayyash,
Salamah, al-Walid and the weak, whole, Mudar line licensed); 2783 (no hijrah after the
Conquest, whole, the hadith al-Baghawi cites).
Dropped: Abu Dawud's Samurah report (unconfirmed, judges by residence), as-Suddi's
al-'Abbas report and "kafir until he emigrates" line, al-Qurtubi's unsourced "fled with
his religion a hand-span" narration, the 'Ali ibn Zayd du'a variants.
