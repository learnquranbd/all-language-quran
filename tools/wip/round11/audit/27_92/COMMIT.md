Closes the commanded three-part declaration of Surah an-Naml (27:91-93):
worship the Lord of this city, be of those who submit, and recite the
Qur'an. The article keeps to 27:92 — reciting as conveying, and the
division of responsibility between the caller and the result.

Sources (all pre-fetched, none re-fetched):
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en): "recite" = recite to the people, convey it to
  them; "I am a conveyer and a warner"; the example of the messengers
  who delivered and were cleared, their nations' reckoning left to Allah
  (anchored in 13:40 and 11:12).
- at-Tabari: guided = follows and believes, its benefit is safety from
  punishment here and hereafter; straying = denying; the speech to
  Quraysh — accept and you win for yourselves, refuse and you offend only
  against yourselves — "I have conveyed and advised you".
- as-Sa'di, al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar: the benefit of guidance returns to
  the guided; "guidance is not in my hand at all"; "nothing is upon me
  but the conveying".
- al-Qurtubi: supplies the governing verb ("I was commanded to recite"),
  plus the qira'a note (al-Farra' / an-Nahhas) on "wa an atluwa".

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3461 (Abdullah ibn Amr), "Convey from me, even
if it is a single verse", quoted whole; sound by inclusion in the Sahih.
Not used elsewhere in the module.

No disagreement kept (the mufassirun agree here). al-Baghawi's lone
abrogation note (nasakhatha ayat al-qital) dropped as a contested,
single-source claim. The la ikraha resonance NOT asserted — no fetched
tafsir makes it. Closes on 27:93 (praise, and the signs Allah will show).
Not a condemned-people verse, so no licensing sentence.
