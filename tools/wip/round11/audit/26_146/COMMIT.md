Card plus an 8-section bilingual article on Ṣāliḥ's question to Thamūd:
will you be left secure in what is here? The verse opens the list of
blessings in 26:147-149 (gardens, springs, crops, palms, carved homes);
the article keeps to the illusion of being "left secure" and does not
preempt the she-camel (26:155) or the hamstringing (26:157).

Sources (all confirmed on the verse):
- at-Tabari, al-Baghawi: "here" = this world; secure from the punishment.
- al-Muyassar: settled in this world, secure from punishment, ruin and
  death; names the people's attitude, ashirīn baṭirīn (insolent, swollen).
- al-Qurtubi: Ibn ʿAbbas — they were long-lived, buildings outlasted by
  their lives; support from 11:61; Ṣāliḥ rebukes the dream of no death.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): the blessings named as a reminder before a
  rebuke; 26:148 date-palms ripe and soft (Ibn ʿAbbas); 26:149 skill vs
  extravagance reconciled as carving done in play, not need.
- as-Sa'di fetched but held no commentary text; not named.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6416, "Be in this world as if you were a stranger
or a wayfarer," quoted whole; Ibn ʿUmar's additional lines marked as his
own words. Sound by Bukhari's inclusion, not upgraded. Chosen to avoid the
nearby stock narrations (Bukhari 660; Bukhari 3443, used at 26:123).

The charge is not prosperity but heedless security amid it coupled with
denial; §6 draws that line explicitly. §8 carries the standing sentence
that Thamūd are a condemned people and the verse licenses nothing against
any living person or community.

Dropped a 26:139 refrain reference that was not in the fetched sources;
