Card and deep bilingual article on the promise that the righteous
inherit the land, written in the Zabūr after the Dhikr.

Sources kept two live disputes rather than resolving them:
- What is the Zabūr and the Dhikr? Named readings held open: the Psalms
  of Dāwūd with the Torah as the Dhikr (Ibn ʿAbbās, ash-Shaʿbī, al-Ḥasan,
  Qatāda); all revealed books with the Dhikr as Umm al-Kitāb / what is in
  heaven (Saʿīd b. Jubayr, Mujāhid, Ibn Zayd, as-Saʿdi, Muyassar); and
  narrower views (Zayd b. Aslam's First Book, ath-Thawrī's Preserved
  Tablet, ad-Ḍaḥḥāk, and a report making the Zabūr the Qur'an). at-Tabari's
  own tarjīh (books after the Umm al-Kitāb; the alif-lām and scrolls-of-
  Ibrāhīm arguments) reported as his choice, which Ibn Kathir confirms.
- Is the land this earth or Paradise? Both kept: al-Qurtubi's and Maʿārif's
  land-of-Paradise reading (with 39:74 and the after-Qiyamah argument) set
  beside the earthly / istikhlāf reading (as-Saʿdi, 7:128, 24:55, 40:51),
  the Banū Isrāʾīl view, and the conquest view via ʿAlī b. Abī Ṭalḥa.

The righteous servants: as-Saʿdi (obeyed commands, shunned prohibitions),
al-Qurtubi's majority (the Muslim community); the possessive "My" noted.

No sound hadith is attached to 21:105 by any fetched tafsir — stated
plainly. Bukhari 660 (the seven Allah shades) used only as a general
portrait of the righteous, explicitly marked not tied to the verse.

Added the plain caveat, both languages: the verse states a decree of who
inherits and licenses nothing against any living person or community.

Dropped: any Psalm 37 parallel (no tafsir raised it); Imam Razi as a
named source (not fetched); the Mahdi/future-control detail from Ruh
al-Maʿani (not fetched directly).
