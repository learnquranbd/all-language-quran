Card and bilingual article on the sanctity of life, the heir's authority,
and the measured limit on retaliation.

Sources (all fetched per the source gate):
- at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di: "illā bi'l-ḥaqq" named narrowly — disbelief
  after Islam, adultery after marriage, retaliation for a life; as-Sa'di adds the
  armed aggressor who cannot be repelled otherwise, and reads the soul as every
  soul (young/old, free/slave, Muslim, covenant-holding non-Muslim).
- The heir's sulṭān: three choices (qiṣāṣ, diya, pardon) per Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari,
  al-Baghawi, al-Qurtubi; as-Sa'di's conditions (deliberate, wrongful, parity) and
  the walī's controlling leave.
- Disagreements kept without a verdict: what sulṭān grants — the whole choice
  (Ibn Abbas, ad-Dahhak, ash-Shafi'i) vs. retaliation only (Qatada, Malik/Abu
  Hanifa); and the referent of "he is supported" — the heir (at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir)
  vs. the slain (Mujahid via al-Baghawi). Also the yā'/tā' recitation split.
- "lā yusrif": three faces of excess (kill only the killer; not two-for-one; no
  mutilation) against the Jāhiliyya practice Ma'arif describes.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6878 (Ibn Mas'ud), the three cases, quoted whole and
graded as recorded in the Ṣaḥīḥ; directly explains "except by right".

Placement: ad-Dahhak's note that this is the first Meccan passage on killing.

Licensing sentence included in both languages: divinely-ordered justice through
lawful authority, no warrant for private vengeance against any living person or
community.

Dropped for want of confirmation: the "world lighter than a believer's blood"
report and Ma'arif's further narrations (gradings unverified); Ibn Kathir's
Mu'āwiya inference and the male-walī dispute (tangential). See LEDGER.
