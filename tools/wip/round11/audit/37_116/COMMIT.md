At-Tabari glosses "nasarnahum" as support against Pharaoh delivered by drowning
him and his court; al-Muyassar names the outcome as might, victory and total
dominance; al-Baghawi confirms the inclusive referent (Musa, Harun AS, and
their people) and names the defeated side with the classical term for Egypt's
population, softened in the article to a historical paraphrase to avoid
conflation with the modern Coptic community. Ibn Kathir (ar and the grouped en
abridgment) supply the before/after: prior killing of sons, enslavement of
women, forced menial labor, then the reversal — eyes settled against the
enemy, land and wealth seized. As-Sa'di adds a detail no other fetched source
carries: the victory included watching Pharaoh drown.

Grammar note, not a live dispute: Tabari reports "some grammarians" explaining
the plural "-hum" (for two people) by a leader's pronoun standing for his
following, then dismisses it as needless since 37:115 already named "them both
and their people." Qurtubi separately reports al-Farra's dual-as-plural
reading against the view Qurtubi calls correct, the same inclusive conclusion
by a different route. Both kept, neither treated as a real disagreement.

Ma'arif al-Qur'an frames the passage as a brief indicator of an episode told
in full elsewhere, grouping Allah's gifts into two kinds (bestowal vs. rescue
from harm) — used to keep "victory" read as the whole deliverance arc, not a
single battle. 117-122 left as forward ground, not elaborated.

No hadith: none of the eight tafsirs fetched attaches one here; said so in the
article rather than borrowing a general Exodus narration. No asbab raised.
Dropped: "humu" as an emphatic pronoun (unconfirmed anywhere fetched); any
cross-reference to 21:48 beyond Ibn Kathir's own gesture toward it; any echo
of 2:50's "you were watching," not fetched for this verse. Licensing guard
included: the drowning describes a closed episode, nothing against the living.
