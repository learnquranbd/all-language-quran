# LEDGER 37:116

## Tafsir fetched / used
All eight files were already fetched into this audit folder; all eight were read and named.

- **at-Tabari (t15, ar)** — used. Confirmed clause: `ونصرنا موسى وهارون وقومهما على فرعون وآله بتغريقناهم، فكانوا هم الغالبين لهم`. Also used for the grammar note: `وقال بعض أهل العربية: إنما أريد بالهاء والميم في قوله (ونصرناهم) موسى وهارون...` and Tabari's own resolution `فإنه لا حاجة بنا إلى الاحتيال به... لأن الله أتبع ذلك قوله (ونجيناهما وقومهما من الكرب العظيم) ثم قال (ونصرناهم) يعني: هما وقومهما`, including the recap of the oppression `يذبحون أبناءهم، ويستحيون نساءهم`.
- **al-Qurtubi (t90, ar)** — used. Confirmed clause: `قال الفراء: الضمير لموسى وهارون وحدهما، وهذا على أن الاثنين جمع... وقيل: الضمير لموسى وهارون وقومهما، وهذا هو الصواب؛ لأن قبله ونجيناهما وقومهما.`
- **Ibn Kathir (t14, ar)** — used. Confirmed clause: `ثم بعد هذا كله نصرهم عليهم، وأقر أعينهم منهم، فغلبوهم وأخذوا أرضهم وأموالهم وما كانوا جمعوه طول حياتهم`, and the prior-oppression recap `من قتل الأبناء واستحياء النساء واستعمالهم في أخس الأشياء`.
- **al-Muyassar (t16, ar)** — used. Full text (57 chars): `ونصرناهم، فكانت لهم العزة والنصرة والغلبة على فرعون وآله.` Used for "might, victory, dominance" (three words).
- **as-Sa'di (t91, ar)** — used. Confirmed clause: `ونصرهما عليه، حتى أغرقه اللّه وهم ينظرون` — the "while they watched" detail, and the "two servants and messengers... prophethood, message, calling to Allah" framing.
- **al-Baghawi (t94, ar)** — used. Confirmed clause: `(ونصرناهم) يعني: موسى وهارون وقومهما، (فكانوا هم الغالبين) على القبط.` The defeated side's identity ("al-qibt") was deliberately rendered in the article as "the people who had ruled over the Israelites in Egypt at that time" rather than transliterated, to avoid any reader conflating a classical demographic term for ancient Egypt's population with the modern Coptic Christian community — nothing it names is disputed, the wording was softened for clarity, not dropped.
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an (t168, en, grouped 114-122)** — used. Confirmed: "the third event relating to Sayyidna Musa... Here it serves as an indicator... two kinds [of favor]: positive favors that bring benefits... negative favors that save from loss or harm."
- **Ibn Kathir abridged (t169, en, grouped 114-122)** — used. Confirmed the English parallel of the ar entry: killing sons, sparing women, menial labor, then prevailing, seizing land/wealth/lifetime's gathering, then the Torah (cross-ref to 21:48, not elaborated — 21:48 itself was not separately fetched and is not quoted, only Ibn Kathir's own cross-reference to it is reported).

Not fetched / not used: Ibn Ashur, ar-Razi, Ibn al-Qayyim (not in the API, not independently confirmed elsewhere, so not named).

## Hadith
None. All eight tafsirs argue 37:116 from the Arabic and the surrounding verses only; none attaches a hadith to this verse. Stated plainly in the article (§7, "One Arc, Told Here in Brief") rather than filled with a general narration about the Exodus/drowning borrowed from elsewhere. No hadith.js call was made since there was no candidate number to confirm.

## Asbab an-nuzul
Not used. None of the fetched tafsirs raise an asbab an-nuzul for this verse — it narrates a past event rather than responding to a contemporary occasion, so none was expected or sought.

## Grammar note (watch-point)
Presented as a mild technical note, not a live dispute, per the brief: Tabari reports "some grammarians" (ba'd ahl al-'arabiyyah) explaining the plural "-hum" by the Arabic convention of a leader's pronoun standing for his following, then dismisses the device as unnecessary since 37:115 already named "them both and their people." Qurtubi separately reports al-Farra's reading (dual-as-plural, pointing to the explicit duals in 117-118) against the view Qurtubi calls correct (the inclusive one, same basis as Tabari's). Both commentators converge on the same substantive answer via different grammatical routes — reported as such, no position taken beyond what each commentator states.

## Dropped
- Al-Baghawi's literal naming of the defeated as "al-qibt" — softened to a historical-identification paraphrase (see above) rather than transliterated as "Copts," to avoid modern misreading. This is a word-choice softening, not a dropped claim; the underlying attribution (Musa, Harun, and their people vs. Pharaoh's people) is still sourced to Baghawi.
- Any cross-reference to Surah 21:48 beyond noting that Ibn Kathir himself draws it — 21:48 was not independently fetched, so nothing beyond Ibn Kathir's own gesture toward it is claimed.
- Any explicit numbered reference to the fuller drowning narrative elsewhere in the Qur'an (e.g., 26) — not needed per the brief, so the article says "elsewhere in the Qur'an" without a number rather than rebuilding or pointing at a specific earlier shipped article.
- A claim that as-Sa'di's "wa hum yanzurun" echoes or quotes 2:50's "wa antum tanzurun" — both share a watching motif but are different verses with different wording; not claimed as a cross-reference since 2:50 was not fetched for this verse and the wording differs.
- Any grammatical claim about "humu" functioning as an emphatic/restrictive pronoun — not confirmed in any fetched tafsir, so dropped rather than asserted from memory.
