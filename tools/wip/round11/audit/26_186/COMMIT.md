al-Aykah answer Shuʿayb (AS) in two moves: "only a man like us," then
"we think you a liar." The verse keeps this one on their second move —
condemnation on ẓann (mere suspicion), not knowledge — and does not
restate the recurring "bashar mithlukum" objection as its theme.

Sources (all fetched for this verse or its stated group):
- at-Tabari: basharun mithlunā = "you eat and drink [as we do]"; naẓunnuka =
  naḥsabu, "we reckon you among those who lie in what you say" — a hunch.
- al-Muyassar: "how are you singled out, not us, with the message?"; the lie
  they allege is in his claim of risāla. (26:187 demand left for that verse.)
- al-Qurtubi: the suspected lie pinned narrowly to the claim of messengership.
- Ibn Kathir: "you deliberately lie… and Allah has not sent you" — denies the
  Sender too. (Grouped 26:189 Day-of-Shadow material left for that verse.)
- as-Sa'di, the spine: the objection is the perennial shubha of deniers; the
  messengers answered it with 14:11; "we think you a liar" is jarāʾa, ẓulm and
  qawl zūr "folded over its opposite" — they held yaqīn of his truth and called
  it doubt, so their report of suspicion is itself a lie from them. Shuʿayb is
  khaṭīb al-anbiyāʾ, whose people were certain of his truthfulness.

Disagreement kept, no position taken: what the alleged lie fastens onto —
the office (al-Qurtubi, al-Muyassar) or all his speech / the Sender
(at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6066 (Abu Hurayra) — "suspicion is the most false of
speech," quoted whole, sound by the collector's placement, flagged in both
languages as a general teaching NOT revealed about this people.

A condemned people: states plainly in both languages that the verse licenses
nothing against any living person or community.

Dropped: al-Baghawi (verse text only, no gloss); Maʿarif (its text is 26:183
short-measure material, off-target); no sabab used.
