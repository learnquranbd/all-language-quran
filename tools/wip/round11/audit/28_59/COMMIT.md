Two conditions govern the destruction of a town, and this verse
names both: a messenger must first be raised in its mother town
reciting Allah's signs, and only then, if its people persist in
wrongdoing, does ruin come. Warning before judgement, guilt before
sentence.

Sources (all fetched for 28:59, none from memory):
- as-Sa'di and Ibn Kathir read the first clause as Allah's wisdom,
  mercy and justice: He does not punish before establishing the
  proof by sending messengers, and destroys none unjustly.
- al-Qurtubi: He does not treat His knowledge of a people as a
  charge against them; the sending of messengers settles it. Cites
  11:117 — destroying people who were setting things right would
  itself be wrong.
- at-Tabari (via Qatada) with Ibn Abbas on the second clause: no
  town is destroyed for its faith, only for its wrongdoing.

Dispute kept open, both sides named, no position taken: umm al-qura
= Makkah specifically (at-Tabari/Qatada, Muyassar, Ibn Kathir, and
al-Qurtubi's first reading) vs. the chief/greatest town of any
region (al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di, Ma'arif, al-Hasan, and al-Qurtubi's
second reading; Ibn Kathir reports it from az-Zamakhshari and Ibn
al-Jawzi as "not far-fetched"). Why the centre: messengers go to
the notables in the cities, and the message spreads outward.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 335 (Jabir) — "I have been sent to all
mankind" — quoted whole from one collection, grading Sahih,
unaltered. Ties Ibn Kathir's universal-messenger reading to the
Prophet's own words.

Licensing sentence included: the destruction is Allah's decree on
past nations as Lord and Judge, and licenses nothing against any
living person or community.

Dropped: Ma'arif's moon-sighting fiqh digression (off-verse); no
specific nations asserted beyond the fetched texts.
