Card plus a deep bilingual article on 32:4 — the Creator who rose
over the Throne, and the tawhid that follows from it.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari: no walī to manage your affair and defend you, no shafīʿ
  to intercede for you; so take Him alone as protector; none overpowers
  Him; the closing call is to single Him out and cast off the andād.
- al-Qurtubi: creation shown so they would hear and ponder the Qur'an;
  the "six days" readings laid side by side — Sunday–Friday, ordinary
  days (al-Hasan), each day a thousand years (Ibn ʿAbbās), six thousand
  years of the Hereafter's reckoning (ad-Dahhak); points to the scholars'
  long discussion of istiwāʾ without taking a position here.
- al-Muyassar: created in six days for a wisdom He knows though He could
  say "Be"; istawā = rose high, befitting His majesty, no modality, not
  likened to creation (the salaf formula, verbatim in the text).
- as-Sa'di: could create in an instant, but He is rafīq ḥakīm; a rising
  befitting His majesty over the Throne, the roof of creation; all
  intercession is His; He alone deserves every kind of worship.
- Ibn Kathir: no intercessor except after His permission — the key that
  reconciles "no shafīʿ" with the shafāʿa verses.

Disagreement kept (no position): length of the "six days".

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 6304 (li-kulli nabiyyin daʿwa) — the Prophet ﷺ
reserves his answered supplication as intercession for his ummah: proof
that intercession is granted by Allah's leave, not wielded over Him.

Sensitive verse (divine attributes): istiwāʾ affirmed per the salaf
(no tashbīh, no taʿṭīl); the early-imams formula stated; the taʾwīl
(istawlā) named among Muslims with respect and no adjudication; a creed
note that this carries no charge against any Muslim holding that view.

Dropped: the creation-days narration as a basis for "six days" — reported
only as a grading dispute (al-Bukhārī held it defective, likely from
Kaʿb al-Aḥbār); Bukhari 660 (off-theme); al-Qurtubi's thumma=wāw note;
Ma'arif's 32:3 and Ibn Kathir's 32:5 grouped material.
