Card and deep article on the resurrection scene: the true promise
draws near, the disbelievers' eyes lock open in terror, and their
cry corrects itself from "we were heedless" up to "we were
wrongdoers."

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi, Ibn Kathir (ar+en), as-Sa'di,
  al-Muyassar. al-wa'd al-haqq = the Day of Resurrection God promised
  His servants (Tabari, Sa'di). shakhisa = eyes fixed wide, barely
  able to blink, from the terror witnessed (Muyassar, al-Kalbi via
  Qurtubi/Baghawi; Ibn Kathir, Sa'di on the cause).
- Grammar disagreement kept, no position: the opening waw read as
  extra so "aqtaraba" answers "idha" (Farra'/Kisa'i via Tabari,
  Baghawi, Qurtubi) vs. jawab elided, waw kept (Basrans, az-Zajjaj
  via Qurtubi); plus Kisa'i's option that the staring is the jawab.
  "hiya" read as standing for the eyes or as a grammatical prop
  (cf. 22:46) — either way the eyes are the staring subject.
- Confession: ghaflah placed in the dunya (Tabari, Ibn Kathir);
  Sa'di reads it as immersion in worldly play, not mere ignorance;
  bal turns it upward to zulm = misplaced worship (Baghawi, Qurtubi)
  and disobedience (Tabari); admitted when it no longer profits
  (Ibn Kathir).

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2901 (Hudhayfah ibn Usayd, ten signs of the
Hour) used only as a run-up bridge and explicitly tied to the
preceding Gog/Magog sign (21:96); stated plainly that no separate
sound narration attaches to 21:97's confession itself.

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or
community" sentence in both languages. Gog/Magog kept to a bridge;
21:98 referenced lightly, not pre-empted.

Dropped: Ibn Kathir's Qamar 54:8 cross-ref (trimmed for scope);
the long Gog/Magog hadith cluster on 21:96 (not this verse).
