Card and deep bilingual article on the disbelievers' two-part charge
against the Qur'an: that Muhammad ﷺ invented it (iftarāh) and that
"another people helped him" (a'ānahu qawmun ākharūn), answered by God's
verdict "faqad jā'ū ẓulman wa zūrā".

Built on the collusion/borrowing edge, distinct from the shipped 23:38
(plain fabrication charge). Eight sections: the two-move accusation; who
the "others" were said to be; the Arabic-Book answer to a foreign-tongue
informant; the unlettered Prophet; the verdict named twice; the shifting
charge; what the verse does not license; the door left open (25:6).

Sources: at-Tabari and al-Baghawi (Mujāhid: the "others" = the Jews);
al-Qurtubi (speaker = an-Naḍr b. al-Ḥārith via Ibn ʿAbbās; individual
names Abū Fukayha/ʿAddās/Jabr, referred back to an-Naḥl → 16:103);
al-Baghawi's overlapping-but-different name list and al-Ḥasan's ʿUbayd
al-Ḥabashī, kept as disagreeing reports; al-Muyassar and as-Sa'di on
inimitability; Ibn Kathir (ar+en) on ummī/al-Amīn, the shifting charge
(with 17:48) and 25:6 as an invitation; al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī's remark.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3 (ʿĀ'isha, "mā anā bi-qāri'"), quoted whole and
marked as onset-of-revelation, not a sabab of 25:4.

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or community"
guard in both languages: an early report of who was rumoured to inform
the Prophet ﷺ gives no warrant to suspect Jews or People of the Book as
a class today.

Dropped: Ma'arif (168, off-verse on 25:2); no individual informant name
asserted as fact. Gates clean: 1679 EN words, 8 sections.
