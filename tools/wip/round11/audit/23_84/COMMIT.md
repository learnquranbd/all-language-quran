Tadabbur 23:84 — to whom belongs the earth: the tawḥīd they conceded and would not follow

The first of the surah's three cross-examination questions (23:84–89):
the mushrikūn concede that Allah owns and controls everything, yet still
worship what owns nothing — belief that stops one step short of its own logic.

Sources:
- at-Tabari: addressed to the Hereafter-deniers of his people; God then
  informs the Prophet they will admit the earth is Allah's in ownership.
- al-Baghawi: the addressees are the people of Makkah; "if you know" means
  its Creator and Owner.
- al-Muyassar: the plain paraphrase of the question.
- as-Sa'di: argues from the tawḥīd of rububiyya they affirm against the
  tawḥīd of worship they deny; enumerates the creation (animal, plant,
  inanimate, seas, rivers, mountains); reads afala tadhakkarun (23:85) as a
  return to what is settled in the fitra; the godhood of an owned thing is
  "the most false of falsehoods."
- Ibn Kathir: the rububiyya/uluhiyya contradiction, with their own words in
  39:3 ("that they may bring us nearer to Allah").
- al-Qurtubi: the one question announces four things — Lordship, oneness,
  dominion that does not pass away, power that does not change.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 2856 (Mu'adh ibn Jabal), quoted whole — Allah's
right over His servants is that they worship Him and associate nothing.
Grading Sahih, not upgraded.

No asbab used (none established for 23:84); the verse's placement is used
instead. Ma'arif (168) text was about 23:76 (famine/Abu Sufyan), dropped.
Kept the card free of scholar names, hadith numbers and asbab.
