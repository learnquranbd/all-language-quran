Card plus 8-section bilingual article on the believing man's tawhid
argument: if only ar-Rahman can send harm, idols who can neither avert His
decree nor rescue are worthless to rely on.

Sources (all confirmed against the fetched text for this verse):
- Ibn Kathir: the opening a-attakhidhu is istifham inkar/tawbikh; the idols
  own no command, "none removes it but He" (10:107).
- at-Tabari: durr = harm and hardship; the two distinct failures — their
  pleading cannot lift the harm, nor can they extract him from it.
- al-Qurtubi: reads durr narrowly as sickness, and la yunqidhun as rescue
  from the affliction he is in. Kept as a narrower reading, not a correction.
- al-Baghawi: the question's force is refusal; the idols have no intercession
  at all (not a weak one — none).
- as-Sa'di: none intercedes with Allah except by His leave.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: al-madinah = a large city; the man's name (Habib, a
  carpenter) given as historical narration from Ibn Ishaq, marked as such.

Disagreement kept: durr as sickness (al-Qurtubi) vs harm/hardship generally
(at-Tabari, al-Baghawi) — stated as a difference, no position taken.

Intercession reconciled: the verse denies the false objects' intercession
(two complementary angles), not the sanctioned shafa'a by Allah's leave.

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2516 (Ibn Abbas), graded hasan sahih by at-Tirmidhi —
marked as a general narration, not attached to the verse by the tafsir.

Dropped: 36:26-27 (death/Paradise) left to another drafter; no count of
messengers asserted. Sensitivity note: licenses nothing against any living
person or community.
