The an-Naml opening of the fire: Mūsā, travelling from Madyan toward
Egypt with his family, loses the road on a dark winter night and perceives
a fire, hoping to bring back news of the way or a burning brand to warm by.

Sources (all fetched for 27:7):
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi: idh hangs on "One Wise and Knowing" (27:6); the story
  is offered to the Prophet as a trace of that wisdom. ānastu = saw/sensed
  from afar. Word-study of shihāb (any light) and qabas (taken from embers).
- Ibn Kathir: lost the road in night and dark; fire blazing by the mountain;
  "he returned with great news and kindled a great light" — the hinge.
- as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir: this verse is the threshold of revelation, his being
  chosen, Allah's speaking to him. Theophany itself reserved for 27:8-9.
- al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di: journey Madyan→Egypt; depth of winter;
  khabar = directions about the lost way; taṣṭalūn = warmth from cold.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'ān: taking natural means is not against trust in Allah;
  the plural of respect for his wife alone; the use of "ahl" over her name.

Disagreement kept: the qirāʾāt of shihāb qabas (Kūfa tanwīn vs Madīna/Baṣra
iḍāfa) — both sound, neither preferred (§4.2).

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2344 (reliance like the birds that go out hungry, return
full), Tirmidhi's own grading hasan ṣaḥīḥ, used as a general narration not
tied to the verse. Bukhari 660 (the seven shaded) rejected as stock and only
faintly related.

Dropped: ānasa/uns etymology (not in the glosses); the 20:10 "hudan" contrast
(text not fetched); Ibn ʿAbbas's "Light of the Lord" and the staff/white hand
(reserved for 27:8-12).
