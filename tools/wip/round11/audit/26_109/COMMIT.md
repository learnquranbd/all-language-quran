Nūḥ's disinterested call: "I ask you no payment; my reward is
only upon the Lord of the worlds." The one instance kept
Nūḥ-specific — sincerity and a hand off the audience's purse.

Card plus 8-section bilingual article (1,732 EN words).

Sources (all confirmed against fetched text, SOURCE-GATE):
- at-Tabari: the reward is from Allah alone, "not from you, nor
  from any of Allah's creation" — the people ruled out of the
  account.
- al-Qurtubi: "lā ṭamaʿ lī fī mālikum" — no greed for your
  wealth; recompense only upon the Lord of the worlds.
- as-Sa'di: the line removes an obstacle (intifāʾ al-māniʿ), the
  fear of a heavy loss (al-maghram al-thaqīl); what Nūḥ does want
  is their good and their walking the straight path.
- al-Muyassar: no fee for conveying the message; the Lord is
  al-mutaṣarrif fī khalqihi, who disposes of His creation.
- Ibn Kathir: reward saved with Allah, not cancelled; his
  truthfulness, sincerity and amāna now made clear.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: the refrain brackets the verse (26:108,
  26:110); either of two traits — honesty, or teaching without a
  fee — earns obedience.

Disagreement kept: wages for teaching religion — early
generations impermissible, later generations allowed under need
(Ma'arif, tracing to 2:41). No position taken.

Hadith: Sunan Abī Dāwūd 3664 (Abū Hurayrah) — knowledge sought
for worldly gain forfeits Paradise's fragrance. Fetched page
exposed no collector grading; none claimed, not upgraded.
Rejected the stock niyyāt and the seven-shaded hadith (used at
26:95) to stay distinctive.

Dropped: 36:21 (not in any fetched tafsir). No-wage refrain left
undeveloped for 26:127 (Hūd); 26:115 left as its own target.
Fair-framing caveat carried in both languages: the verse is
Nūḥ's speech and licenses nothing against any living person.
