Card and deep bilingual article on the posture of rejection: the verses
were recited to you, plainly and repeatedly, yet you turned back on your
heels — a retreat from guidance that has actually reached you, which the
verse and Ibn Kathir treat as graver than never having heard.

Sources (all fetched for this verse):
- at-Tabari: idiom nakasa 'ala 'aqibihi is general (returning whence one
  came); belied and turned away out of dislike of hearing; Mujahid
  tastakhirun, Ibn Abbas tudbirun; addressees = ahl Makka.
- al-Qurtubi: al-ayat = the Qur'an; ad-Dahhak "before you were killed";
  the phrase is isti'ara for al-i'rad 'an al-haqq; Ali's variant reading
  'ala adbarikum / tankusun with damma.
- as-Sa'di: following the Qur'an = advancing, turning away = descending
  to asfal safilin (the forward/backward spine).
- Ibn Kathir (ar+en): "the greatest of their sins"; when called you
  refused; link to Ghafir 40:12.
- al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar: retreat backward / shrinking from hearing,
  believing, acting.

Disagreement kept: the shade of tankisun (tastakhirun vs tudbirun) held
side by side, unresolved, as at-Tabari sets it.

Hadith: no marfu' hadith attaches to 23:66's wording — said plainly. The
an-Nasa'i / Ibn Abbas athar on the arrogance and night-talk of 23:67
(tahjurun) is used, marked mawquf and tied to the next verse, no grading
invented.

Rejecters clause carried in both languages: the verse describes the
Quraysh deniers of that time and licenses nothing against any living
person or community. Bridge to 23:67 (pride around the House) kept to one
section; the lesson stays on 23:66's turning-back.

Dropped: Ma'arif's Badr/famine material (belongs to 23:64).
