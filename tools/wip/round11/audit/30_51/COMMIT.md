Card and deep bilingual article on 30:51 — the fickle, ungrateful
heart: the same people who rejoice at the rain (30:48-50) would, at a
single yellowing wind, fall straight into kufr/ingratitude. Faith
pinned to the harvest is at the mercy of the next wind.

Sources gave:
- at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di, al-Muyassar: a wind
  (dry / cold / corrupting / harmful) spoils the matured crop, green
  turns yellow, and they deny the blessing that fed them.
- yakfurūn held in both its senses — al-Muyassar names them side by
  side: disbelief in Allah AND kufr al-niʿma (ingratitude); Ibn Kathir
  and al-Baghawi gloss it as denying the earlier favour.
- al-Qurtubi: the referent of "hu" disputed (cloud vs crop/Ibn Abbas's
  trace), and the past-tense ẓallū standing for the future on
  al-Khalīl's authority; al-Muyassar's "makathū" and al-Baghawi's
  "ṣārū" for the settled, persistent turn.
- as-Sa'di on the speed — forgetting past blessings and rushing into
  denial; Ibn Kathir's cross-reference to 56:63.

Disagreements kept: the wind's nature (cold vs scorching/dry) and the
referent of "hu" (cloud vs crop) are left as differences, not resolved.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 2999 (Suhayb, "Strange are the ways of a
believer"), used as a general narration on the believer's temper and
explicitly marked as not tied to this verse.

Dropped: the "eight winds" mawqūf report and the ʿĀd-wind marfūʿ
(Ibn Kathir grades its rafʿ munkar) — both omitted, see ledger.

Includes the standing note that the verse describes a human tendency
and licenses nothing against any living person or community.
