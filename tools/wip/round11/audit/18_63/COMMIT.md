Card and bilingual article on the fish sign and the servant's forgetting,
the beat of the Mūsā–al-Khiḍr narrative at this verse (18:60–82).

Sources gave:
- The fish as the appointed marker: where the revived fish slipped into the
  sea is where the servant of God would be found (al-Muyassar, as-Saʿdī).
- The Spring of Life (ʿAyn al-Hayāt) motif: Ibn Kathīr and al-Qurtubī (the
  latter via Bukhārī from Ibn ʿAbbās, and Tirmidhī/Sufyān) report the spring's
  water reviving the dead fish; the wudū-drop account given as one strand,
  closed with "and God knows best."
- "mā ansānīhu illā al-shayṭān": at-Tabarī's grammar (Satan made me forget to
  mention the fish), the Ibn Masʿūd reading, and al-Qurtubī's framing of the
  sentence as the servant's apology — the adab of naming the true source.
- The readings of ʿajaban kept as a live disagreement: the servant's words vs
  a fresh exclamation vs God's report; Mujāhid/Ibn ʿAbbās/Ibn Zayd on Mūsā's
  amazement (at-Tabarī); al-Baghawī records both attributions without deciding.
- sarab vs ʿajaban reconciled (Maʿārif): tunnel-path for the fish, wonder for
  Mūsā and his servant; the Bukhārī detail of the water standing "like an arch."

Hadith: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3401 (Ubayy ibn Kaʿb), the fish-sign portion only;
graded as the collection gives it, not upgraded. Identifies the attendant as
Yūshaʿ ibn Nūn.

Disagreements kept: whose wonder ʿajaban voices (servant vs prophet); left open.

Dropped: Ibn ʿAṭiyyah's half-fish sighting; the exact name of the river; any
statement that a prophet erred (18:61's plural handled as idiom + tafsīr note).

Defect fixed: PREP's hadith number (Bukhari 660) is the wrong hadith — used the
correct Moses–Khidr narration, Bukhari 3401.
