Card and deep bilingual article on 34:28, built around kāffatan —
the universality of the Prophet's ﷺ mission to all mankind and all
ages, unlike earlier prophets sent each to his own people.

Sources gave:
- Qurtubi: kāffatan = 'āmmatan by taqdīm/ta'khīr (to all people);
  Ma'arif: kāffah is a ḥāl of nās, fronted to accent universality.
- Tabari / Baghawi: to Arab and non-Arab, red and black, not to his
  own people's idolaters alone. Ibn Kathir: Muhammad b. Ka'b ('āmmatan),
  Qatadah (honour = obedience), Ibn Abbas (contrast 14:4 → jinn and men),
  "sent to the black and the red."
- Ma'arif: reach across time too (every generation to Qiyamah) and the
  finality argument (Qur'an under Allah's protection, so no prophet after).
- Sa'di: task limited to bashāra/indhār; "the matter is not yours";
  "most do not know" = no sound knowledge, ignorant or obstinate.

Disagreement kept (grammar, no position): kāffatan as a word about the
people (sent to all of them) vs. about the Messenger — Zajjaj's jāmi'
(gatherer), or kāffan (restrainer), hā' for mubālagha, from kaff ath-thawb.
Dominant sense named as the universality of the message.

Hadith: Bukhari 335 (Jabir), the five gifts, quoted whole; the fifth
("every prophet to his people, I to all mankind") is the verse's own
proof — in the two Sahihs, sound by the collectors, grading not upgraded.

Dropped: Tabari's "sābiq al-'Arab" narration (general, not verse-attached).
Asbab: none established; verse placement used instead. Not a condemnation
verse; no licensing sentence. All gates clean (1646 EN words, 8 sections).
