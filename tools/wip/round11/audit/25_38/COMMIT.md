Card and deep bilingual article for the roll-call of destroyed nations:
ʿĀd, Thamūd, the companions of ar-Rass, and the many generations
between whom only God can number.

Sources gave a clear spine (as-Sa'di): the passage warns Makka's
deniers with nations they knew and whose ruins some of them saw on
their travels; what stopped them believing was expecting no
resurrection. ʿĀd/Hūd/barren wind and Thamūd/Ṣāliḥ/the rajfa from
al-Qurtubi; Ibn Kathir notes the fuller stories sit in al-Aʿrāf.

Disagreement kept open, not resolved: WHO were aṣḥāb ar-Rass. Named
the sides from the fetched tafsirs — a village of Thamūd (Ibn ʿAbbās),
al-Falaj in al-Yamāma (Qatādah), a people who cast their prophet in a
well (ʿIkrimah), the people of Yā-Sīn/Antioch (Kaʿb, as-Suddī), the
pine-tree worshippers (ʿAlī), Wahb's herdsmen sent Shuʿayb, Saʿīd b.
Jubayr's Ḥanẓala b. Ṣafwān, a remnant of Thamūd at the abandoned well
of al-Hajj 22:45. Anchored on the word (rass = any unlined well / dug
pit) and on Ma'arif's plain verdict that no sound tradition settles it;
noted at-Tabari's own tentative lean to aṣḥāb al-Ukhdūd (Burūj). No
identification asserted.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3651 (khayr al-nās qarnī), ṣaḥīḥ, cited by
Ibn Kathir from the Two Sahihs — used for the meaning of qarn (a living
generation), not as a comment on the destroyed peoples.

The "black slave / lone believer" report (mursal via Muhammad b. Kaʿb)
carried but marked plainly: gharāba/nakāra/possible idrāj per Ibn
Kathir, and rejected by at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi as this people since
ar-Rass were destroyed while these believed. Not a sound attached
hadith.

Carried the destroyed-peoples caveat in both languages: the verse
describes what the text describes and licenses nothing against any
living person or community; the ruin was God's judgement, no warrant
for any human hand.

Dropped (in ledger): al-Kalbī's "they ate their prophet", Adharbayjān,
snow/Banū Asad, the suḥq reports, Rabīʿ b. Khuthaym anecdote.

No asbāb established; verse placement used instead.
