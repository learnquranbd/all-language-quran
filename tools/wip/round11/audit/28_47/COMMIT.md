Card plus deep bilingual article on 28:47, the excuse-removal verse:
Allah sends messengers so that none, when punishment falls for
what their hands sent ahead, can plead "You never sent us a warner."

Sources (all confirmed against fetched text):
- Grammar: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi all read the muṣība
  as ʿadhāb/niqma, and qaddamat aydīhim as "what they earned."
  al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi both note the apodosis of lawlā is
  elided; both supply "We would have hastened the punishment."
  al-Qurtubi: the hands are named because most earning is by them;
  fanattabiʿa is subjunctive as jawāb at-taḥḍīḍ.
- Core reading: Ibn Kathir (ar+en), establishing the proof and
  cutting off the excuse; as-Sa'di, "to repel their argument and
  cut off their claim." Ibn Kathir's cross-verses 4:165, 6:156-157
  and 5:19 carried over whole; at-Tabari independently ends on
  "lest mankind have a plea against Allah after the messengers."
- Disagreements kept: who "them" are (al-Qurtubi: Quraysh, or the
  Jews) left unresolved; al-Qushayri's deeper reading of the ellipsis
  (the era grew long but is no excuse) quoted via al-Qurtubi; the
  ʿaql-obliges-faith argument reported, not settled.
- Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 7416 (al-Mughira), "none loves the
  excuse (al-ʿudhr) more than Allah, and for that He sent the
  warners and bringers of good news" — the verse's own logic.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's unquoted pointers to 20:134 and 17:15 (not in
fetched text); Muyassar/Ma'arif fetched but not cited. No sabab
attaches; verse placement used. §8 states plainly the verse licenses
nothing against any living person or community.
