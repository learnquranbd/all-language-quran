# SOURCES ledger — 23:38

Verse: إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا رَجُلٌ ٱفْتَرَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًۭا وَمَا نَحْنُ لَهُۥ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ
"He is not but a man who has invented a lie about Allah, and we will not believe him."
Speaker: the mala' (eminent notables) of 23:33 — the disbelieving chiefs who denied the
Hereafter. This is the close of their speech.

## Tafsir fetched (all from PREP, not re-fetched)

- **at-Tabari (15)** — USED. Arabic clause the attribution rests on:
  "قالوا ما صالح إلا رجل اختلق على الله كذبا في قوله: مَا لَكُمْ مِنْ إِلَهٍ غَيْرُهُ وفي وعده إياكم أنكم إذا متم وكنتم ترابا وعظاما أنكم مُخرجون … وقوله هُوَ من ذكر الرسول وهو صالح".
  Used for: (a) the "lie" = the messenger's tawḥīd (23:32) AND his promise of resurrection
  (23:35); (b) naming the prophet Salih / placing the scene among Thamud; (c) "huwa = the
  messenger"; (d) §5 the reversal rests on at-Tabari identifying the alleged "lie" as his tawḥīd.
- **Ibn Kathir ar (14)** — USED. Clause: "افترى على الله كذبا أي فيما جاءكم به من الرسالة
  والنذارة والإخبار بالمعاد". Used for: the "lie" covers the message, the warning, and the news
  of the maʿād (resurrection).
- **Ibn Kathir en abridged (169, grouped 23:31–40)** — USED. Clauses: "they refused to follow
  him because he was a human being like them, and they refused to follow a human Messenger";
  "It was said that this was 'Ad … Or … Thamud, because Allah says فأخذتهم الصيحة"; closing
  "so let those who hear this beware of disbelieving in their Messengers". Used for §2 (refusal
  of a human messenger), §3 ('Ad-vs-Thamud disagreement + Sayhah reason), §8 (closing lesson).
- **al-Baghawi (94)** — USED. Clause: "(وما نحن له بمؤمنين) بمصدقين بالبعث بعد الموت" and
  "(إن هو) يعني الرسول". Used for: "we will not believe him" = will not believe in resurrection
  after death; huwa = the messenger.
- **al-Qurtubi (90)** — USED. Clause: "إن هو إلا رجل يعنون الرسول … افترى … أي اختلق". Used
  for: huwa = the messenger; afterā = ikhtalaqa (invented from nothing).
- **al-Muyassar (16)** — USED. Clause: "وما هذا الداعي لكم إلى الإيمان إلا رجل اختلق على الله
  كذبًا، ولسنا بمصدقين ما قاله لنا". Used for: the plainest paraphrase (the one calling you to
  faith … we do not believe what he told us).
- **Ma'arif al-Qur'an en (168, grouped 23:37–41)** — USED, on 23:37. Clause: "This is the
  argument which the unbelievers advance when denying belief in the Day of Judgment. Those who
  deny it publicly are confirmed infidels, but … many Muslims also act in a manner that the same
  denial can be felt from their deeds and actions." Used for §6.
- **as-Sa'di (91)** — EMPTY (1 char). Not used.

## Hadith

- **Sahih al-Bukhari, DarusSalam Hadith 660** (USC-MSA Vol.1, Bk.11, No.629).
  URL fetched: https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-660 (via hadith.js).
  First ten words (EN used): "Allah will give shade to seven on the Day when…"
  Grading: in Sahih al-Bukhari (no separate per-hadith grade claimed; not upgraded).
  Status: **NOT attached to 23:38 by any tafsir** — explicitly presented in §7 as a general
  narration on the reality of the Day the deniers rejected, not as verse-linked exegesis. No
  classical commentary fetched attaches a sound hadith to this verse; §7 says so in one sentence.

## Asbab an-nuzul
- None. Not used. The verse is the tail of a narrative passage (23:31–41); §1 gives its
  placement instead. No asbab claimed.

## Disagreement kept (not resolved)
- Identity of the people/prophet: at-Tabari names Salih (→ Thamud); Ibn Kathir reports it was
  said to be 'Ad or Thamud, leaning Thamud from the Ṣayḥah of 23:41. Kept as an open
  disagreement in §3; the article does not settle it.

## Licensing sentence
- Carried in §8 in both languages: the verse records this people's words and their judgment and
  "licenses nothing against any living person or community"; not a label for a doubter, a
  questioner, or a stranger. Wording is fresh, distinct from shipped 23:14/23:20/23:27.

## Dropped (could not confirm / declined)
- The §5 "reversal/irony" (their own shirk is the real iftirā' about Allah) is framed as the
  article's own reflection on the verse's structure, grounded in at-Tabari's gloss that the
  alleged "lie" was the messenger's tawḥīd. It is NOT attributed to any named mufassir as an
  "irony," because none of the fetched texts frames it that way.
- No weak or unverified narration used to fill the hadith slot.
