Hārūn's warning, spoken before Mūsā returned: this calf is only a
trial; your Lord is ar-Raḥmān; follow me, not the Sāmirī.

Sources give a consistent picture that exonerates Hārūn (AS). Qurtubi
fixes min qablu as before Mūsā's coming and return, glosses futintum as
ubtulitum wa udliltum (tried and led astray) with bihi on the calf, and
records the two readings of "follow me" (follow me in His worship / obey
my order not the Sāmirī's — OR follow me in setting out toward Mūsā and
leave the calf); kept as a live disagreement. Baghawi parallels on
min qablu and on obeying in abandoning calf-worship. Muyassar adds the
test's purpose: to distinguish the believer from the disbeliever, and
reads the command as following the call to Allah's worship and His law.
Ibn Kathir names ar-Raḥmān as the One who created and decreed all,
Owner of the glorious Throne, Doer of what He wills, and glosses
"follow me" as in what I command / leave what I forbid; his abridged
English (with 20:91) reports the people opposed Hārūn and nearly killed
him. Sa'di supplies the no-excuse point: a shubha arose, but Hārūn had
already forbidden them, named it a fitna and named ar-Raḥmān, so no
excuse remained — the warning removes the hearer's excuse. Ma'arif
gives the three-faction split (12,000 loyal, per Qurtubi).
