Card and bilingual article on the two denials and the proof against
many gods: no son (walad) and no partner-god (sharīk), then the
rational disproof the mufassirūn call dalīl at-tamānuʿ.

Sources named only where fetched on this verse:
- at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, as-Saʿdi, Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged),
  al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar — all on 23:91.
- Spine: were there several gods, each would carry off its own
  creation (Ibn Kathir, at-Tabari, as-Saʿdi, Qurtubi, Baghawi,
  Muyassar) and they would vie for mastery like earthly kings
  (Baghawi, Qurtubi, Muyassar); the loser is no god, so plurality
  collapses (at-Tabari, as-Saʿdi).
- Empirical premise: the observed order of the cosmos. Ibn Kathir
  cites 67:3; as-Saʿdi argues from the unbroken run of sun, moon
  and planets. Ibn Kathir also frames the mutakallimūn's two-wills
  version of the tamānuʿ proof.
- Qurtubi's join kept: what refutes a partner refutes a son too,
  since an heir disputes dominion as a partner does.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4482 (Ibn ʿAbbās, qudsī) — the claim of a
son is an "abuse" God answers with subhānī, the verse's own tanzīh
root. Quoted whole, one collection, no grading upgraded.

Dropped: 21:22 (no fetched tafsir cites it); Maʿārif 168 (its text
covers 23:88, not this verse); no asbāb (none confirmed — placement
in the 23:85–90 sequence used instead). §8 states plainly the verse
corrects a claim, not a warrant against any person who holds it.
