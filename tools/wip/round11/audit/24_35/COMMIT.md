Replaces the 843-word, 6-section article with a 1,784-word, 9-section one built only
on the eight tafsir files fetched for 24:35.

Sources: at-Tabari reads the verse as following 24:34 and chooses "Guide of the people
of the heavens and the earth" (with Ibn 'Abbas and Anas "My light is My guidance");
Mujahid and Ibn 'Abbas "He directs the affair"; Ubayy, al-Hasan, Abu-l-'Aliya "Adorner";
ad-Dahhak "Illuminer"; al-Qurtubi's broader "by Him their lights shine". On the name
itself the article reports as-Sa'di (light in essence, His veil light; sensory and of
meaning), al-Qurtubi (said of Allah by way of praise; not among perceived lights) and
Ma'arif (Munawwir, by scholars' agreement) side by side, no position.
Disagreement kept on mathalu nurihi: Allah's guidance in the believer's heart (Ibn
'Abbas; Ibn Mas'ud's reading), the believer (Ubayy and his reading "man amana bihi",
Sa'id ibn Jubayr, ad-Dahhak), Muhammad ﷺ (Ka'b al-Ahbar to Ibn 'Abbas, Sa'id ibn Jubayr
in another chain), the Qur'an (al-Hasan, Ibn Zayd; at-Tabari's choice), obedience (Ibn
'Abbas via al-'Awfi); Ibn 'Atiyya's pronoun objection noted.
Parts of the similitude only as glossed: mishkah, wick, glass (az-Zajjaj), the olive
in open sun (at-Tabari and Ibn Kathir's preference) against "midst of trees" (rejected
by Ibn 'Atiyya), Ubayy's sunless tree, al-Hasan's "not on earth, a parable"; nur 'ala nur per Mujahid,
as-Suddi, Ibn 'Abbas, Ubayy's five lights, at-Tabari, as-Sa'di's fitrah reading.
Qira'at: durriyy/durri'/dirri' (al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi; at-Tabari prefers durriyy);
tawaqqada/yuqadu/tuqadu (at-Tabari: all correct, prefers tawaqqada).
Hadith: Tirmidhi 2642 ('Abdullah ibn 'Amr), "created His creation in darkness", quoted
whole, graded hasan by at-Tirmidhi; Ibn Kathir attaches it from Ahmad and al-Bazzar.
No asbab ('Awfi's single-chain report on the Jews' question left out).
Dropped from the old article, unsupported by the files fetched: the "light in my heart"
du'a, 29:69, 24:40, the anti-esoteric paragraph and the self-check section.
Defect fixed: the old article called the readings of the similitude "complementary
rather than competing"; the files record them as a disagreement, at-Tabari picks one.
