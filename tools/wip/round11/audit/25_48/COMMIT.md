Tadabbur 25:48 — the winds as glad tidings before His mercy, and pure
water sent down.

Spine: the ordinary sequence of wind then rain read as designed kindness.
The wind is the herald walking ahead of the rain, and as-Sa'di explains the
timing as mercy inside mercy — servants take the good news before the rain
falls and ready themselves rather than being caught all at once.

Sources:
- at-Tabari: winds are al-mulaqqihah (fertilizing); bushra glossed as life
  and rain-relief sent on His servants.
- Ibn Kathir: the wind-types catalogue (gather, carry, drive, herald, sweep,
  seed); tahur = the instrument of purification (like sahur, waqud), which he
  calls the soundest reading, setting aside faul=fail.
- al-Qurtubi: every tahur is pure but not every pure is tahur; the mubalagha
  form makes it pure-and-purifying (jumhur); "tahur" is reserved for water.
- al-Baghawi + as-Sa'di + Maarif: pure-in-itself and purifying-for-others;
  purifies from hadath and khabath; ummah's consensus.

Disagreement kept (both named, no verdict): tahur as merely "pure" (ahl
ar-ra'y / Abu Hanifa) vs pure-and-purifying (jumhur) vs repeatedly-purifying
(Malik).

Hadith: Buda'ah well, "Water is pure, nothing defiles it" (Abu Dawud 66).
Grading reported, not upgraded: at-Tirmidhi hasan, Ahmad sahih (via Ibn
Kathir), also an-Nasa'i; Ibn al-Arabi and Abu Umar weakened its chain — kept
as a dispute.

Rain-as-mercy tied to 25:49–50 in clauses (revives dead land, distributed to
be remembered); kept to 25:48. No asbab confirmed; wrote placement instead.
No modern-meteorology miracle claims.

Dropped: the Sahih Muslim "rain by grace vs by a star" hadith (Ibn Kathir
places it under 25:50); Ikrimah/Khalid athar; the full qullatayn/used-water
fiqh — out of scope, not a fiqh manual.
