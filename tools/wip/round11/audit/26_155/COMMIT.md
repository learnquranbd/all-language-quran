Card plus deep bilingual article on Ṣāliḥ's answer to Thamūd: the
she-camel as a God-given sign, and the water divided into turns
(shirb) on alternating known days.

Sources (all fetched for 26:155):
- at-Tabari (15): shirb = the share/allotment of water; she was
  brought out of a rock or a hilltop (left open); the boundary runs
  both ways; three vocalizations shirb/sharb/shurb.
- al-Muyassar (16): Allah brought her from the rock; "she-camel of
  Allah"; a share each on a known day.
- al-Qurtubi (90), via Ibn ʿAbbās: red she-camel, ten-months pregnant
  (ʿushrāʾ), out of the mountain; on her day she drank their whole
  supply in the morning and gave milk at day's end.
- as-Sa'di (91): she came from a solid smooth rock, witnessed by all;
  the water-for-milk exchange.
- al-Baghawi (94): shirb = a share and portion of water.
- Ibn Kathir (14 ar; 169 en): the crowd's demand, the promise to
  believe, the rock splitting exactly as asked; most still disbelieved.

Hadith: Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 3379 (Ibn ʿUmar) — at al-Ḥijr the Prophet ﷺ
sent his companions to draw only from the well the she-camel used to
drink from. Sahih by Bukhari's placement; verse-specific (the shirb).

Kept as read: at-Tabari's rock-or-hilltop left undecided.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's "days of eating and drinking" report (a fatḥa
shāhid about Minā, not this verse). No origin detail beyond the fetched
tafsir; no isrāʾīliyyāt.

Reserved for later verses: the harm prohibition (156), the hamstringing
(157) and the punishment (158), though present in (16) and (169).

Condemned-people clause carried in both languages: the verse licenses
nothing against any living person or community.
