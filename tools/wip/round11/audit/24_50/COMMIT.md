Card and deep bilingual article for an-Nur 24:50, the diagnosis of those
who claim faith yet flee the judgment of Allah and His Messenger (24:47-49),
coming readily only when the ruling favours them (24:49), contrasted with the
believers of 24:51.

Spine: the three possibilities the verse lays over the refusal — a disease in
the heart, doubt, or fear of an unjust verdict — and the "bal" that overturns
all three: the fault is in them, not in the ruling.

Sources (all pre-fetched, none re-fetched):
- at-Tabari: irtāb = doubt in the prophethood; the grammatical point that God is
  named first for reverence though the ruling is the Messenger's (singular
  liyaḥkuma in 24:48).
- Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged): the three possibilities, "whichever it is, pure
  disbelief"; Allah and His Messenger free of the injustice imagined.
- as-Sa'di: the sick-heart image; doubt hardening into an accusation; the ruling
  as utmost justice (cites 5:50); faith is not bare words; it is forbidden to
  think ill of the Sharī'a's rulings.
- al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi: the interrogative as rebuke; ḥayf = to deviate/wrong.
- al-Muyassar: the disease of hypocrisy; the "bal" turn.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: the Bishr / Jew / Ka'b Ibn Ashraf occasion (on Tabari's
  authority) and the nuance that the real driver was self-interest.

Disagreements kept, unadjudicated: Ibn Kathir (gharīb, mursal) vs Ibn al-Arabi
(bāṭil, part sound) on the al-Hasan narration; Ma'arif vs Ibn Kathir on the
cause. No sound connected hadith attaches to the verse — stated plainly; the
only narration is reported graded-weak, never upgraded.

"Licenses nothing against any living person or community" carried in both
languages: the verdict is on a described refusal, a mirror for the reader, not
a label for anyone unheard or for a people as a class.

Dropped to fit budget: Qurtubi's fiqh on the duty to answer a summons and the
dhimmī jurisdiction rules (confirmed, not doubted; outside the spine).
