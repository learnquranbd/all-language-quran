Card plus 8-section bilingual article on 30:46: one act of Allah, the
winds, unfolded into a chain of mercies that ends on gratitude.

Traces the verse's four purposes in order: the winds as mubashshirat
(heralds sent before the rain), tasting His mercy (the rain that
revives land and servants), ships sailing by His command, seeking His
bounty through trade, and the closing laʿallakum tashkurun.

Sources (all fetched for 30:46):
- at-Tabari (via Mujahid, Qatada): signs of His oneness; glad tidings =
  rain; bounty = apportioned provision.
- al-Qurtubi: winds are heralds because they precede the rain; "bi-
  amrihi" added because winds are not always favourable and a gale may
  sink the ships, all by His command.
- Ibn Kathir (ar + abridged en): ships driven only by wind; bounty =
  trade and travel region to region; thank for favours outward and
  inward, countless.
- as-Sa'di: the taste teaches recognition and longing for righteous
  deeds; shukr is the goal that increases and keeps favours, while
  ingratitude exposes them to removal.
- al-Muyassar, al-Baghawi: winds stir the clouds and souls rejoice
  before the rain; rain = fertility; bounty = sea trade.

Hadith: Bukhari 846 (Zayd ibn Khalid al-Juhani, sahih) — the believer
attributes rain to "the bounty of Allah and His mercy," the verse's own
faḍl and raḥma; the test is who you credit.

Dropped: the wind-duʿāʾ (unverified this session; one on-theme sound
hadith already fills the slot). Ma'arif grouped file is all 30:41
material, not used. No sabab; placement stated instead. Kept distinct
