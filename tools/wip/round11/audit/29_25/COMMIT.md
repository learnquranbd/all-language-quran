Ibrahim names the idols a bond of affection, not gods: the social glue
of shared false worship that dissolves into mutual cursing on the Day.

Card (tawheed, hereafter): the warmth of belonging that feels like love,
and which of my loyalties rest on Allah versus mere membership.

Article, 8 sections, 1,581 EN words:
- Placement after the fire-rescue (29:24); innama as restriction; Ibn
  Kathir's rebuke of the deed.
- The qira'at kept as a live disagreement, no side taken: accusative
  (maf'ul li-ajlih — idols taken OUT OF affection) per al-Qurtubi, Ibn
  Kathir, at-Tabari; nominative (ma = "that which" — idols ARE merely
  the affection) per al-Baghawi, at-Tabari, as-Sa'di. at-Tabari judges
  the readings close and all sound.
- The worldly function (at-Tabari, al-Muyassar, Ibn Kathir): worship as
  the thread binding the tribe.
- The reversal: yakfuru = mutual disowning, yal'anu = followers/leaders
  curse each other (Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi), tied to 7:38 and 43:67.
- The idols turn too (as-Sa'di, 46:6; al-Qurtubi, 21:98 — idols as fuel).
- The love that survives: Qatada in at-Tabari (every friendship becomes
  enmity except the God-fearing's); Sahih al-Bukhari 660, the two who
  love for Allah's sake among those Allah shades.
- Licensing section in both languages: condemns idolatry, licenses
  nothing against any living worshipper or community — Ibrahim argues
  and then emigrates (29:26), he does not harm.
- Application: a single test on every loyalty — remove Allah and see
  what still stands.

Dropped: Ibn Abi Hatim's Umm Hani "people of tawhid" report (ungraded,
non-canonical, tangential). Ma'arif said nothing verse-specific on 29:25.
Reciter names omitted to avoid the reciter/mufassir Ibn Kathir clash.
