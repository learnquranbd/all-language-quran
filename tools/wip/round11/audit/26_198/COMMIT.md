Card plus an eight-section bilingual article on the hypothetical of
26:198: had the Qur'an come down upon a non-Arab reciter.

Sources gave:
- al-Qurtubi and al-Baghawi read al-aʿjamin as one not Arab of tongue;
  al-Baghawi's aʿjami/ʿajami distinction (unclear speech vs non-Arab
  stock) is used to block any ethnic reading of the verse.
- as-Sa'di: a reciter whose tongue the hearers cannot grasp and who
  cannot put the message to them as it should be put.
- Ibn Kathir frames it as the intensity of Quraysh's disbelief, and
  gathers his own parallels (15:14-15, 6:111, 10:96-97); confirmed via
  the English abridgement.
- al-Muyassar: even recited in sound Arabic they would still deny and
  manufacture an excuse.
- Set against 26:195's clear Arabic tongue as the mirror image.

Disagreement kept: at-Tabari reads the word at its widest ("the beasts
that do not speak") against the others' non-Arab person; left as a
difference that reaches the same end.

Framing: a full section states plainly, in both languages, that this is
excuse-proofing of the deniers, not a ranking of peoples, and licenses
nothing against any living person or community; Quraysh named as the
deniers in view.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 79 (rain on three kinds of ground), used as a
general narration explicitly marked not attached by the mufassirun to
this verse.

Dropped: Ma'arif's 195-196 material and its two secondhand hadiths
(Maqil ibn Yasar, Ibn Mas'ud) as off-point and unverifiable here; no
sabab claimed; 26:199-201 left for their own verses.
