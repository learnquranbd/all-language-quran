Covers the two-verse pair: who the warning actually reaches
(36:11) and the comprehensive recording of deeds and their
traces (36:12).

Sources gave:
- as-Sa'di: innamā restricts where the benefit lands, not the
  duty; the two marks of the receptive heart (sound aim in
  seeking truth + awe of ar-Rahman unseen); forgiveness for
  sins, noble reward for righteous deeds and good intention.
  On 36:12, āthār as the broad legacy a person sets in motion
  in life and after death; imām mubīn = al-Lawh al-Mahfuz.
- Ibn Kathir (abridged): "unseen" = knowing Allah watches when
  no one else does; reward "vast and great"; 67:12 set beside.
  Cites man-sanna (Muslim, from Jarir; full in Ibn Abi Hatim
  with the Prophet reciting the verse) and the three continuing
  deeds (Muslim, Abu Hurayra).
- al-Qurtubi: resurrection reading of nuhyi al-mawtā preferred;
  both āthār ranges (legacy + footsteps to the mosque, the
  Banu Salima sabab); imām mubīn ranges over al-Lawh al-Mahfuz
  and the pages of deeds.
- Ma'arif: deeds "sent ahead" as baggage; recording = preservation.
- al-Baghawi: āthār = the sunna one sets + footsteps.

Disagreements kept: the two readings of āthār (footsteps vs
wider legacy) given as complementary, per Ibn Kathir; the range
of imām mubīn (Preserved Tablet vs record of deeds) left open.

Hadith quoted whole: Banu Salima, Sahih Muslim 665a (Jabir),
sound by its place in the Sahih. Three continuing deeds
(Muslim 1631) referenced for the encouragement.

Dropped: the weak "curtain over the eyes" narrations; secondary
footstep reports; the reviving-the-heart reading kept only as a
minority note. No hadith number asserted for man-sanna.
