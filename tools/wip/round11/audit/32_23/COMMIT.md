Card plus a deep bilingual article on the verse that gives Mūsā the Book,
forbids doubt of "his meeting," and names the Torah a guide for Banī Isrāʾīl.

The article is built around the main tafsir crux — the referent of liqāʾihi —
and names the sides without resolving it:
- the Prophet ﷺ meeting Mūsā on the Night of the Isrāʾ (Ibn ʿAbbās, Qatādah;
  carried by at-Tabarī, Ibn Kathīr, al-Baghawī, al-Muyassar, Ma'arif);
- Mūsā's receiving the Book with acceptance (Mujāhid, az-Zajjāj via al-Qurtubī;
  as-Suddī via al-Baghawī), and the related reading on the Qur'an with 27:6
  (Ma'arif);
- Mūsā meeting his Lord (at-Tabarānī from Ibn ʿAbbās, in Ibn Kathīr);
- meeting Allah at the end / Qiyāmah, including al-Qurtubī's taqdīm-taʾkhīr.

Kept the consolation frame (as-Saʿdī, at-Tabarī, al-Muyassar): the Qur'an is no
novelty among books nor its bringer a stranger among messengers. Included
al-Hasan al-Baṣrī's reading of an omitted antecedent, with an-Naḥḥās's note that
it is gharīb and comes via ʿAmr ibn ʿUbayd — grading not upgraded.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3239 (Ibn ʿAbbās), the Isrāʾ vision that itself recites
32:23; quoted whole, reported as collected in al-Bukhārī's Ṣaḥīḥ. al-Baghawī's
Anas narration mentioned without a grading claim.

hudan li-banī Isrāʾīl: two pronoun views (Qatādah = Mūsā; al-Ḥasan = the Book),
as-Saʿdī on uṣūl/furūʿ, link to 17:2. Stated plainly in both languages that the
verse honors the Torah as real guidance for its people and licenses nothing
against any living community.

Dropped: no sabab asserted (none established); 32:24–25 distortion material not
imported; Anas narration not graded.
