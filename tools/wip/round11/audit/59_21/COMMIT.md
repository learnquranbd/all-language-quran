Replaces the 857-word, 6-section article with a 1,777-word, 8-section one,
built only from the eight tafsir files fetched for 59:21.

Sources: Ibn Kathir frames the verse as magnifying the Qur'an and argues
a fortiori from the mountain's hardness to the human heart; at-Tabari and
Ibn Kathir carry Ibn 'Abbas (via al-'Awfi): the mountain would split from
the Qur'an's weight and from fear of Allah, so people are told to take it
with intense fear and takhashshu'. At-Tabari adds Qatada (Allah excused
the deaf mountain, not the son of Adam) and glosses tilka-l-amthal and
la'allahum yatafakkarun as reflecting in order to turn back and yield.
Glosses of khashi'/mutasaddi' from al-Qurtubi, at-Tabari, al-Muyassar,
al-Baghawi, with al-Qurtubi's alternative (humbled by the obedience
charged on it, splitting lest it disobey). As-Sa'di on the Qur'an's
effect on hearts and the fruits of reflection.

Disagreements kept: parable vs real perception (Ma'arif citing Mazhari;
al-Qurtubi's "parable for the disbelievers"); the addressee (at-Tabari:
the Prophet; al-Qurtubi: people, the Prophet as a favour, the ummah);
who turns away (at-Tabari: the son of Adam; al-Baghawi: the disbeliever).

Hadith: the weeping palm trunk, which Ibn Kathir calls mutawatir and
brings as a comparison, quoted whole from Sahih al-Bukhari 3584 (Jabir).
Sister verses as Ibn Kathir cites them: 13:31, 2:74. No asbab in any source.

Dropped from the old article as unsourced for this verse: 78:7, 23:2,
7:143, 35:28 and the khashyah/khawf distinction, the morphology claim
for mutasaddi'. Card unchanged.
