Mūsā enters Memphis unseen, intervenes for a wronged Israelite, and a single
blow unintentionally kills the Egyptian — a strong man's zeal carried one strike
too far, named at once as Satan's work.

Sources gave: at-Tabari (city = Manf, midday; Satan "stirred my anger until I
struck and he died"); al-Qurtubi (istighātha = seeking rescue; aiding the
wronged a duty in every law; wakz/lakz lexical cluster; al-Hasan and ad-Dahhak
on the act); as-Sa'di (the one blow killed by Mūsā's strength; his remorse;
Satan's adorning and whispering); al-Baghawi (anger rose, "let him go," the
threat; regret, killing not his aim, burial in the sand); al-Muyassar (the act
was BEFORE prophethood); Ma'arif + Ibn Kathir (shiʿatihi = Israelite,
ʿaduwwihi = Copt; Mujahid: a punch with the fist).

Core reading, unanimous in the fetched tafsir and held in the article's own
voice: the death was UNINTENDED, not qatl ʿamd — Qatada "the prophet of God did
not intend to kill him," Ibn Ishaq and al-Qurtubi likewise. Mūsā's "this is from
Satan's work" is framed as a God-fearing man naming the fitna, never a confession
of murder; no verdict taken that impugns the prophet.

Disagreement kept with both names: Ma'arif (after preaching began, shiʿa =
disciples) vs al-Muyassar (whole episode before the call).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4712, the intercession hadith — Mūsā "I killed a soul I
was not commanded to kill," graded by Bukhari's own Sahih, framed as the awe of
the Day, not a ranking of prophets.

Carries the "licenses nothing against any living person or community" sentence in
both languages (§8): the verse permits no vigilante violence; its lesson is the
sanctity of life, honest ownership, and the turn to God.

Dropped: Bukhari 660 (stock, off-verse); the tongue-knot/coal story (belongs to
20:27); the named-individuals report (marked qīl); 28:16-19 sequel left untold
for its own targets.
