Card and deep bilingual article on the trumpet-blast of resurrection
and the criminals gathered zurqan.

Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, Ibn Kathir, al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di,
al-Muyassar and Ma'arif al-Qur'an, all fetched on 20:102.

Kept the readings of zurqan side by side without adjudicating them:
blue/gray eyes from terror (Ibn Kathir), blue eyes from thirst
(at-Tabari, al-Azhari, al-Zajjaj, al-Baghawi), blind (al-Kalbi,
al-Farra', at-Tabari; cf. 17:97 and 20:124-125), whole colour/face
changed (as-Sa'di, al-Muyassar), fixed stare of fear and false hope
disappointed (al-Qurtubi). Reconciled by Ibn 'Abbas via Sa'id ibn
Jubayr: the Day has stages, one blue, one blind.

Also carried the qiraat of the verb (yunfakhu passive, the cities'
and Tabari's choice; nanfukhu for Abu 'Amr to match wa-nahshuru;
Ibn Hurmuz's yanfakhu; the sur/suwar and non-mushaf yuhsharu variants).

Hadith: Tirmidhi 2430, the Bedouin asking about the Sur — "a horn that
will be blown into." Graded hasan by Tirmidhi (Arabic wording; not
upgraded to the English print's "hasan sahih"). Directly on the verse.

Licensing: stated plainly in both languages that zurqan describes an
eschatological terror-state of the criminals on that Day and is no
comment on any living person with blue or gray eyes, and that mujrimin
names a class defined by shirk, licensing no verdict on anyone living.

Dropped: Abu Hurayrah's "horn vast as the heavens" and the poised
horn-bearer reports — related by Ibn Kathir but grading unverified
here, so flagged as unverified and not used to fill the hadith slot.
Did not preempt 20:103-104 (the "ten days / one day" whisper).
