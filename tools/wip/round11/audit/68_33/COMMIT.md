Card and 8-section bilingual article for 68:33, the verse that steps out of
the garden parable (68:17-32): kadhalika al-'adhab, wa la-'adhabu al-akhirati
akbar, law kanu ya'lamun.

Sources: Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar; en grouped 68:29-38), Muyassar (grouped
68:27-33), Qurtubi, Sa'di, Baghawi, Ma'arif (grouped 68:33-34).
- kadhalika: as with the garden, so in this world for whoever opposes the
  command and disbelieves the messengers (Tabari), oversteps the limits
  (Baghawi); Ibn 'Abbas, Qatada, Ibn Zayd via Tabari: the punishment of this
  world, Ibn Zayd adding loss of wealth (also Qurtubi). Ibn Kathir and
  Muyassar: stinginess, withholding the poor's right, ingratitude. Sa'di: the
  thing transgressed with is taken when most needed.
- Quraysh: parable for the Quraysh (Ibn Kathir en), Makkan famine (Ma'arif);
  Qurtubi's drought admonition (qila), Badr parable (Ibn 'Abbas), and the
  objection that the surah is Makkan. All kept, none chosen.
- Withheld right obligatory or voluntary: Qurtubi's own preference reported.
- Akbar: greater on the Day (Tabari), ashaqq (Ibn Kathir), greater and more
  severe (Muyassar); worldly punishment no expiation (Ma'arif).
- law kanu ya'lamun: answer supplied as deterrence and repentance; "these
  mushrikun" (Tabari) vs anyone who knows (Sa'di, Muyassar). Both kept.
Licensing sentence in both languages, plus no test for labelling anyone;
Ma'arif's note that the wicked often prosper used against reading loss as
a verdict. 68:34 named only as the next verse.
Hadith: none. Ibn Kathir's al-Bayhaqi report noted as unchecked and unused.
Asbab: none. Parallels: none (32:21 not cited by any fetched source).
Dropped: the Bayhaqi report's content, Ibn Kathir en's Yemen/Ethiopia
origins, 68:28 and 68:32 glosses (neighbour ground).
