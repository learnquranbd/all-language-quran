Replaces the 726-word, 6-section article with a 1,720-word, 8-section
deep article on every soul tastes death, and the test of evil and good.

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar + en), al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di,
al-Baghawi, al-Muyassar, Ma'arif al-Qur'an on 21:35; at-Tabari, Ibn
Kathir and al-Qurtubi also fetched on 21:34 for the context.
- 21:34: Tabari and Ibn Kathir on the polytheists hoping to outlive
  him (55:26-27); al-Qurtubi's occasion (rayb al-manun taunt, given
  without a chain), al-Farra' on the fa', no gloating over death.
  Ibn Kathir's al-Shafi'i couplet. Licensing sentence in both languages.
- Kullu nafsin: Tabari's pangs and cup, Muyassar, Sa'di; one line on
  3:185 (Qurtubi points back) and 29:57.
- Ma'arif's disagreement on whether angels die, kept with no position;
  al-Khidr: Ibn Kathir reports the argument from 21:34, Sa'di asserts it.
- Sharr/khayr glosses (Tabari, Baghawi, Ibn Zayd, Sa'di, Muyassar) and
  Ibn 'Abbas's list via 'Ali ibn Abi Talha (Tabari, Ibn Kathir).
- Fitnatan: Qurtubi's masdar 'ala ghayr al-lafz; Qatada splits bala'
  and fitnah, Ibn Jurayj's Ibn 'Abbas report calls both bala'. No source
  explains why evil is named first; the article says so.
- Patience and gratitude; Ma'arif's 'Umar saying via Ruh al-Ma'ani.
- Return for recompense (all); Sa'di with 41:46.
Hadith: no tafsir attaches one; Muslim 2999 (Suhaib) quoted whole and
marked as not attached to the verse.
Dropped from the old article: the gold-assay etymology of fitnah,
89:15-16, 7:168, 21:47, the evil-first teaching point (no source
comments on the order). 3:144 asbab not in any fetched text.
