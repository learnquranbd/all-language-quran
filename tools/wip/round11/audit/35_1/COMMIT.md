Card plus deep bilingual article on the opening of Surah Fatir: praise to
the Originator of the heavens and the earth, the angels made messengers with
wings, and His adding to creation as He wills.

Sources (all pre-fetched, none re-fetched):
- Fatir read through the root: al-Qurtubi (al-fatr = splitting open; al-ibtida
  wa-l-ikhtira), the Ibn Abbas well-story "ana fatartuha" from Ibn Kathir and
  al-Qurtubi, Ibn Abbas's gloss badiʿ, Ad-Dahhak (= khaliq), al-Baghawi (no
  prior model), and al-Qurtubi's note that the One who could begin can restore.
- al-hamd: at-Tabari (complete thanks to the only One worthy of worship);
  as-Sa'di (creation as proof of power, dominion, mercy, wisdom, knowledge);
  al-Muyassar (attributes of perfection, blessings seen and hidden).
- Angels as messengers: Ibn Kathir (between Him and His prophets), at-Tabari
  (to whom He wills), al-Qurtubi (Jibril, Mikaʾil, Israfil, Angel of Death;
  Yahya b. Sallam and as-Suddi's two readings), as-Sa'di (qadari vs dini
  errands), Ma'arif (the "link" sense).
- as-Sa'di's point that none of the angels is excepted, proving their complete
  obedience, with 66:6.
- Wings affirmed as revealed, kayf left to Allah, not reduced to metaphor and
  no physical picture drawn (salaf method); counts illustrative not limiting
  (Ma'arif; Abu Hayyan's alternative that the numbers describe the messengers).

Disagreement kept open with no position: the scope of "He increases in
creation what He wills" — the angels'/their wings (most mufassirun per
al-Mahdawi, al-Hasan, as-Suddi); beauty of voice (az-Zuhri, Ibn Jurayj);
comeliness of features (Qatada); handsome face, handwriting, intellect; and
the absolute reading (az-Zamakhshari, as-Sa'di, Ma'arif).

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 3232 (Ibn Masʿud: the Prophet saw Jibril having 600
wings), quoted from Bukhari's wording; al-Qurtubi notes the same in Sahih
Muslim. Confirmed via hadith.js; grading = authentic by inclusion, not
upgraded. an-Najm 53:18 used as placement via al-Baghawi.

Dropped: al-Qurtubi's ungraded Israfil 12,000-wing report (general, unattached);
at-Tabari's grammar of mathna/thulatha/rubaʿ and the iʿrab options for fatir.
