Sources: at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi, al-Muyassar, al-Baghawi, as-Sa'di, Ibn Kathir
(ar), all fetched live for this verse. Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en) and Ibn Kathir
(en, abridged) read in full despite being grouped 37:88-97 by the API;
confirmed their fetched text covers only 37:88-89 (the tauriyah discussion)
and adds nothing specific to 37:97, so neither is cited here.

Kept as corroborating detail, not a disagreement: at-Tabari's report of a
structure shaped like a tannur (kiln), and the two measured-wall reports
al-Qurtubi (via Ibn Abbas, RA) and al-Baghawi (via Muqatil) each preserve
independently — a stone wall thirty cubits high, with al-Baghawi's version
adding twenty cubits wide. Al-Qurtubi's three-step sequence (fill with
firewood, kindle, then throw him in) is used for the command's execution.

Linguistic core of the article: at-Tabari's gloss of al-jahim as the Arabs'
term for fire piled on fire, al-Baghawi's "greatest part of the fire," and
al-Qurtubi's grammatical point that the definite article in al-jahim is
kinaya — pointing back to that structure's own fire, not asserting this
worldly fire is the Hereafter's Hellfire. Cross-referenced against 37:55
(already shipped) where the same surah uses al-jahim for the Hereafter's
fire, to make the distinction concrete rather than asserted from memory.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4563, Ibn Abbas (RA), confirmed live via
quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-4563. "Hasbuna Allahu wa ni'ma
al-wakil" said by Ibrahim (AS) when thrown into the fire, and later by the
Prophet ﷺ when told of the gathering army (3:173) — the hadith's own
cross-reference. Al-Qurtubi's own shorter mention of the same moment, via
'Abdullah ibn 'Amr with a different (singular) wording, is named but not
quoted, to avoid hybridizing two variants.

As-Sa'di's framing of the fire as the people's own jaza (repayment) for the
idol-breaking is reported as their reasoning, not endorsed. A dedicated
paragraph states plainly, in both languages, that the people who built this
structure were a specific, ancient people, long gone, and that the verse
licenses no hostility toward any living person or community.

Scope held deliberately narrow: this verse is the command and its
execution only. The rescue, and the fire's coolness/safety, are confirmed
as belonging to Surat Al-Anbiya 21:69, a different surah, and were not
imported; 37:98 (they intended harm, made most debased) is named only as
the next verse's content, not detailed.

Dropped: at-Tabari's/al-Qurtubi's aside naming a man "al-Haizan" and the
attached hadith about someone swallowed into the earth for arrogant
walking — tangential, flagged by Qurtubi himself with "Allahu a'lam," and
not used anywhere in the card or article.
