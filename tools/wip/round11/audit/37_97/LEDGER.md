# LEDGER 37:97

## Tafsir fetched (from PREP.md, pre-fetched) and used

- at-Tabari (t15_97.txt, 403 chars). Used: structure built resembling a
  tannur ("بنوا له بنيانا يشبه التنور"), firewood carried and lit; the
  linguistic gloss on al-jahim as the Arabs' term for "جمر النار بعضه على
  بعض، والنار على النار" (burning coals piled on each other, fire upon
  fire). Also used: this verse placed immediately after the people's
  argument-defeat (verb qalu matched against 37:95's qala in the Arabic
  given in PREP.md itself, not a tafsir claim).
- al-Qurtubi (t90_97.txt, 627 chars). Used: "تشاوروا في أمره لما غلبهم
  بالحجة" (they consulted about him once he had overcome them with the
  argument); the three-step sequence "تملئونه حطبا فتضرمونه ثم ألقوه فيه"
  (fill with firewood, kindle, then throw him in); Ibn Abbas's (RA) report
  of a stone wall thirty cubits high toward the sky, filled with fire;
  the kinaya reading of the definite article in al-jahim ("والألف واللام
  في الجحيم تدل على الكناية، أي: في جحيمه") — pointing back to that
  structure's own fierce fire, not asserting this worldly fire is the
  Hereafter's Hellfire itself; the brief mention, via 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr,
  that the phrase was said at this moment (not quoted — see hadith note
  below, kept to Bukhari's wording only).
- al-Muyassar (t16_97.txt, 92 chars). Used: "لما قامت عليهم الحجة لجؤوا
  إلى القوة" (when the proof stood against them, they resorted to force) —
  the one-line summary of the argument-to-force turn.
- al-Baghawi (t94_97.txt, 188 chars). Used: "معظم النار" (al-jahim is the
  greatest part of the fire); Muqatil's report of a stone wall thirty
  cubits high and twenty cubits wide, filled with firewood.
- as-Sa'di (t91_97.txt, 137 chars). Used: the structure built "عاليا
  مرتفعا" (raised up high) with fire kindled inside it; the fire framed as
  "جزاء على ما فعل، من تكسير آلهتهم" (repayment for his breaking of their
  idols) — reported as their own reasoning, not endorsed by the article.
- Ibn Kathir, ar (t14_97.txt, 203 chars). Used only for its own restraint:
  it points to "ما تقدم بيانه في سورة الأنبياء" (what was already explained
  in Surat al-Anbiya) without repeating the rescue/fire-coolness content —
  cited as a model for this article's own decision not to import 21:69.
- Ibn Kathir, en abridged (t169_97.txt, grouped 37:88-97). Read in full.
  Its only content specific to 37:97 is the same forward-pointer to Surat
  Al-Anbiya' (21:68-70) for what happened after the throw; not otherwise
  cited since it adds nothing beyond at-Tabari/al-Qurtubi on this verse.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an, en (t168_97.txt, grouped 37:88-97, 20215 chars).
  Read (head + targeted greps for "jahim", "throw", "fire", "idol",
  "worship", "hasbuna", etc.). Confirmed its entire fetched text addresses
  37:88-89 (the tauriyah/ta'rid discussion on "inni saqim") and never
  reaches 37:97's own content. Not cited for this verse — dropped as
  off-topic for this ayah despite being grouped under it.

## Hadith

- Sahih al-Bukhari, Dar-us-Salam 4563, narrated Ibn Abbas (RA). Fetched
  live via `node tools/wip/round11/hadith.js Bukhari 4563 --save`.
  URL: https://quranx.com/hadith/Bukhari/DarusSalam/Hadith-4563.
  Wording used whole, first ten words: "Allah is Sufficient for us and He
  Is the Best..." Arabic: "حسبنا الله ونعم الوكيل قالها إبراهيم عليه
  السلام حين ألقي في النار". No separate grading given beyond inclusion in
  Sahih al-Bukhari; none upgraded.
- Cross-reference within the same hadith to 3:173 (Prophet Muhammad ﷺ's
  identical words when told of the gathering army) — this is the hadith's
  own stated cross-reference, not an added claim.
- Al-Qurtubi's own tafsir separately preserves a shorter mention of the
  same moment via 'Abdullah ibn 'Amr ibn al-'As ("حسبي الله ونعم الوكيل",
  singular "sufficient for me", a different wording from Bukhari's plural
  "sufficient for us"). Not quoted, to avoid a hybrid of two variants;
  named only as existing, with Bukhari's wording kept as the sole quoted
  source per the standing rule.

## Dropped

- At-Tabari's / al-Qurtubi's mention of a man named "al-Haizan" and the
  attached hadith about someone swallowed into the earth for arrogant
  walking. Qurtubi himself flags it with "Allahu a'lam" and it is
  tangential and potentially confusing for this verse — dropped entirely
  per the orchestrator's instruction, not used anywhere in card or article.
- The fire's failure to burn Ibrahim (AS), and its being commanded cool
  and safe — confirmed as belonging to Surat Al-Anbiya 21:69, a different
  surah outside this article's sources. Named only as "a different verse"
  in the article, with no detail imported.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an (en), despite being grouped under 37:97 by the API,
  contributes nothing to this verse specifically (see above) — not cited.
- No claim was softened instead of dropped; what could not be confirmed
  against the fetched text was left out.
