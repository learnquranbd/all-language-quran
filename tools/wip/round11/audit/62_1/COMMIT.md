Replaces the 668-word, 6-section article with a 1,729-word, 8-section deep article.

Sources: at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir (ar and abridged en), al-Muyassar, as-Sa'di,
al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi and Ma'arif al-Qur'an, all fetched for 62:1.
- Tense: Ma'arif on the Musabbihat; only al-Jumu'a and at-Taghabun (64:1) open
  with yusabbihu; past = certitude, present = continuity.
- Scope: at-Tabari's seven heavens and earths, willingly and unwillingly;
  Ibn Kathir's speaking and inert, with 17:44.
- What tasbih is: Ma'arif's symbolic vs real reading (its own view: real);
  al-Muyassar's yunazzih; as-Sa'di's four verbs (glorify, yield, hold as god, worship).
- The four names glossed only in the commentators' words; as-Sa'di's "because"
  tying al-Malik to the verb; the shared n-z-h of yunazzih / al-munazzah for al-Quddus.
- Kept as a range: al-'Aziz as retribution (at-Tabari), never overcome
  (al-Muyassar), mastery over all (as-Sa'di). 62:3's pair per Ibn Kathir.
- Al-Qurtubi's reported reading of Abu al-'Aliya and Nasr ibn 'Asim: all four
  names in the nominative ("He is the King").
Hadith: Sahih Muslim 879 (Ibn Abbas) quoted whole, Muslim 877 (Abu Hurayrah)
summarised: al-Jumu'a and al-Munafiqun in the Friday prayer (attached by
Ibn Kathir). Muslim 854 on the best day, from al-Qurtubi, marked as about
the day and not attached to the verse.
Dropped from the old article: 62:11 caravan and al-Bukhari's Jabir hadith,
24:41, 57:1/59:1/61:1 listed by key, and unsourced glosses of al-Malik and
the al-'Aziz/al-Hakim pairing. No asbab; al-Qurtubi's second Friday hadith
not confirmed and not used.
