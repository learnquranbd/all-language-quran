Maryam withdraws from her family to a place toward the east.

The surah turns from Zakariyyā and Yaḥyā to Maryam. Ibn Kathir and
as-Sa'di read the pairing as one lesson: a pure child to an aged father
and barren mother, then a child with no father at all — both proofs of
Allah's power, paired here and in Āl ʿImrān and al-Anbiyā. as-Sa'di
frames it as a climb from wonder to greater wonder.

Her honour kept high on the sources' own terms: Ibn Kathir on her line
(bint ʿImrān, of Dawud), her mother's dedication of her to the Sacred
House, and her growth per Āl ʿImrān 3:37; as-Sa'di on the merit of being
named in the Book Muslims recite east and west.

Intabadhat: at-Tabari and Ma'arif on the root nabadha (to cast away /
withdraw from the crowd); al-Qurtubi's gloss and the Qur'an's own use of
the root.

Disagreements kept as disagreements: why she withdrew — purification
(as-Suddi, al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi) vs. worship (al-Qurtubi's preferred
reading, per Ma'arif; Nawf al-Bikali) vs. drawing water (Ibn Ishaq);
where "eastward" points (as-Suddi, at-Tabari, Qatadah, as-Sa'di,
al-Baghawi); and Maryam's rank — prophetess vs. supremely favoured woman
(al-Qurtubi, "the first more apparent"), both named, no verdict.

Why the east: at-Tabari and al-Qurtubi on the east being esteemed above
the west; Ibn Abbas (via at-Tabari, Ibn Kathir, al-Qurtubi, Ma'arif) on
the Christians' eastward qibla as the birthplace of ʿĪsā; al-Baghawi
traces it to al-Hasan.

No hadith: no fetched tafsir attaches a sound Prophetic report to this
verse; stated plainly. Material is Companion/Successor athar. Annunciation
(19:17 onward) deliberately not pre-empted.
