God's verdict on the mocked believers: today, for their patience, they
alone are the winners. Built on 23:108-110 — the powerful laughed at a
band of believers until laughter crowded out God's remembrance, and the
Day answers it.

Sources gave a firm consensus:
- bi- of cause (at-Tabari): rewarded *because* they endured the harm of
  the mockery — the ridicule borne is itself the qualifying deed.
- Two patiences (al-Qurtubi, as-Sa'di, al-Muyassar): ṣabr over the harm
  AND over obedience; Sa'di adds "until they reached Me".
- al-fāʾizūn defined (Ibn Kathir: felicity, safety, the Garden, saved
  from the Fire; at-Tabari: bliss and honour that never end).

Kept the qirāʾāt reading as a difference, not a ruling: Hamza and
al-Kisāʾī read innahum (kasra) as fresh praise; others annahum (fatḥa)
as the second object, "I rewarded them the winning of Paradise"
(al-Baghawi). at-Tabari leans kasra; both left standing.

Cross-reference to al-Muṭaffifīn drawn from Qurtubi/Sa'di (83:34) and
Ibn Kathir (83:29 and 83:30): the laughter of the world is inverted on
the Day.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (Abu Hurayra, RA), the seven Allah shades,
used as a GENERAL narration on reward on the Day — marked in both
languages as not attached by the mufassirūn to 23:111, since none of
them attaches a hadith here.

Closing on al-Qurtubi's derived warning against mocking the weak, with
the plain statement that the verse licenses nothing against any living
person or community.

Dropped: the 40-year Malik narration and Ma'arif's "five requests"
(both on 23:108, off the reward theme).
