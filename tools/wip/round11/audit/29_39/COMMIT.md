Three names, three powers, one arrogance, one end: Qārūn (wealth),
Pharaoh (the throne), Hāmān (the minister who ran it). The article's angle
is the triad AS A SET — not the individual stories (Qārūn's treasure and
Pharaoh's infanticide already shipped in 28:76 and 28:4-8) — three doors
into the one sin of istikbār, leveled together.

Sources: Ibn Kathir (ar + en) identifies the three and, on 29:40, the
differentiated ends (Qārūn swallowed by the earth; Pharaoh, Hāmān and troops
drowned). at-Tabari: Moses came to all of them (jamīʿahum) with the bayyināt;
istikbār = refusing to affirm the signs and follow Moses; mā kānū sābiqīn =
could not outrun Allah, "We were fully able over them." as-Saʿdi: arrogance
in two directions — against the truth and over God's servants; at the end
they surrendered and submitted. Ma'arif on 29:38's mustabṣirīn: perceptive
but confined to the worldly, blind to the Day (links 30:7).

Disputes kept open: the elided "We destroyed" vs. coordination with
ʿĀd/Thamūd (29:38) or with "ṣaddahum" (Qurtubi via al-Kisāʾī); and sābiqīn as
escaping the punishment vs. preceding others in disbelief.

Hadith: Sahih Muslim 91 (Ibn Masʿūd) — a mustard seed of pride bars Paradise;
"pride is disdaining the truth and contempt for people," which maps onto the
two faces of istikbār the tafsir names. Marked as a general teaching on kibr,
not tied to the verse. Deliberately distinct from 28:76's Bukhari isbāl hadith.

Licensing sentence included in both languages: condemns three named past
tyrants; licenses nothing against any living person or community.
