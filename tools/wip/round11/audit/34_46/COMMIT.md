Card plus deep bilingual article on 34:46, the reply to the "majnūn"
slander: the one cure is honest, unclamoured reflection — stand for
Allah, in pairs and singly, then think.

Sources gave:
- "one thing" (bi-wāḥida) read four ways and kept as a spread:
  obedience to Allah (Mujahid, at-Tabari/al-Qurtubi); lā ilāha illā
  Allāh (Ibn Abbas, as-Suddi via al-Qurtubi); the Qur'an; or a single
  trait explained by "that you stand" as badal (at-Tabari, al-Baghawi).
- "stand for Allah" = not physical standing but rising to seek the
  truth, cf. 4:127 (al-Qurtubi, al-Baghawi); purely for Allah with no
  whim or tribal partisanship (Ibn Kathir); mind cleansed of prior
  belief (Ma'arif); with resolve and sincerity (as-Sa'di).
- "mathnā wa furādā" = two-by-two and singly; the point is reflection
  away from the crowd (at-Tabari's pair-debate-then-alone; al-Qurtubi's
  collected senses incl. al-Mawardi day/night; Ma'arif's two ways).
- al-Qurtubi's reflection checklist, and as-Sa'di's portrait of the
  Prophet's bearing and speech against the signs of madness.
- ṣāḥibikum argument: forty years of known character (Ma'arif; as-Sa'di).

Disagreement kept (no position): the waqf at "then reflect" — Abu Hatim
and Ibn al-Anbari stop there; Muqatil (via al-Baghawi) turns reflection
to the heavens and earth; the majority tie it to the man himself.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 4770 (Ibn Abbas), the Safa address ending in
the verse's own phrase "a warner to you before a severe punishment."
Reported as among the soundest narrations; grading not upgraded; Bukhari
wording used whole, not hybridised with the Muslim variant.

Dropped: the weak Abu Umama prayer-gloss on "mathnā wa furādā" (Ibn
Kathir names its isnad weak and the reading far-fetched); the Burayda
garment narration (sound but not on this verse).
