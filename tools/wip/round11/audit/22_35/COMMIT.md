The verse names the four marks of the mukhbitīn (the humble) promised
good tidings in 22:34: a heart that trembles at Allah's mention, patience
under affliction, prayer kept upright, and spending from provision.

Sources gave: at-Tabari frames the verse as "the description of the
mukhbitīn" (khushūʿ/khuḍūʿ), and via Ibn Zayd, "their hearts do not
harden." Ibn Kathir (ar + en abridged) reads wajal plainly as fear and
carries Mujahid ("content in faith") and ath-Thawri ("content, accept the
decree, submit"). al-Qurtubi traces the trembling to strength of yaqīn,
lines up the sister verses 8:2, 39:23 and 5:83, and rebukes staged
ecstasy (shrieking/braying) against the Prophet ﷺ and Companions'
understanding and weeping. as-Saʿdi: the fear made them leave the ḥarām;
patience with no tasakhkhut; prayer upright in outward and inward
servitude; the partitive "min" shows how light the command is, closing on
"spend, and Allah will provide for you and increase you." al-Muyassar and
al-Baghawi fill in the ṣabr and infāq categories (zakāh, dependents, fī
sabīlillāh, recommended charity).

Kept as a difference: Maʿarif reads wajal as awe before majesty, beside
Ibn Kathir/Qurtubi's plain "fear" — presented as two sides, not rivals.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 660 (the seven Allah shades), quoted whole; its
seventh figure who "remembers Allah in seclusion and his eyes flood" is
the trembling heart made visible, and it also touches the sin-fear and
secret-charity marks.

Dropped: the "low ground" etymology of ikhbāt (in no fetched tafsir); the
weak (ruwiya) report naming Abu Bakr/ʿUmar/ʿAli as the occasion of 22:34.
