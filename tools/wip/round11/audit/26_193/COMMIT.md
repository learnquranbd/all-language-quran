Card and deep bilingual article on 26:193, the verse's answer to the
forgery charge running through the surah: the Qur'an's provenance is
trust at every link.

Sources (all pre-fetched, none re-fetched):
- Jibril identification stated as the tafsir gives it. Ibn Kathir names
  the salaf (Ibn Abbas, Muhammad b. Ka'b, Qatadah, 'Atiyyah, as-Suddi,
  ad-Dahhak, az-Zuhri, Ibn Jurayj) and calls it "a matter in which
  there is no dispute"; at-Tabari reports the same from the authorities
  of ta'wil; as-Sa'di adds Jibril is the best and strongest of the
  angels.
- Spine from as-Sa'di: al-Amīn = trusted to add nothing and subtract
  nothing — the integrity of the wording itself.
- Two qira'at kept as the sources give them (at-Tabari, al-Qurtubi,
  al-Baghawi): light nazala (Spirit as subject) vs heavy nazzala (Lord
  sends via the Spirit). at-Tabari rules both widespread and close in
  meaning, since the Spirit descends only by Allah's command — carried
  as the article's "two readings, one descent".
- Amānah thread drawn across the surah's refrain (26:107, 125, 143,
  162, 178) into the angel al-Amīn; az-Zuhri's cross-ref to 2:97
  (Ibn Kathir) used only for the named carrier / by-Allah's-permission.
- Mujahid's remark (via Ibn Kathir) that the earth will not consume one
  the Spirit addressed — reported as his own words, not a ruling.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 2 (Aisha RA; al-Harith b. Hisham RA
asks how the waḥy came — ringing bell / angel as a man). Graded sound
by its collector; marked that no fetched mufassir attaches a hadith to
this verse. Confirmed not reused elsewhere in surah 26.

No asbab (verse placement written instead). No licensing sentence
(verse is about revelation, not a condemned people).

Dropped: the Quraysh al-Amīn title for the Prophet ﷺ (not in any
fetched tafsir for this verse); heart/Arabic exposition reserved for
26:194-195.
