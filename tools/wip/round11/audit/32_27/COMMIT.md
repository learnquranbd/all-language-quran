Card and deep bilingual article for 32:27, the barren land revived.

Centres the verse on the daily agricultural sign and the closing call to
sight (afalā yubṣirūn), kept distinct from the shipped resurrection-earth and
travel-and-see gazes (29:20, 30:9).

Sources:
- Ibn Kathir: sawq = water sent from sky or sayh (rivers/mountain flow) to lands
  at their time; God's luṭf/iḥsān; Egypt/Nile meant but not alone; cross-ref 80:24
  and 36:33, 36:35.
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an: water routed overland, not rain on the field itself.
- at-Tabari: juruz lexis — ناقة جروز / سيف جراز, land that consumes its own growth;
  dialect forms (Banū Tamīm = jarz); Qatada = dusty earth.
- al-Qurtubi (via az-Zamakhshari): juruz = growth cut off, not a permanent salt-flat,
  proof is fa-nukhriju bihi zarʿan; Muḥammad ibn Yazīd — alif-lām makes it general.
- al-Baghawi / as-Saʿdi / al-Muyassar: zarʿ split beast (grass) and man (grain/fruit),
  many kinds / varied colours.
- as-Saʿdi: the sight-vs-insight sting — they looked with the glance of habit and
  routine, not the seeing of understanding men.

Disagreement kept as a disagreement: the location of the juruz land (Yemen /
Abyan / thirsty land / Nile / general), with no position taken; generality is the point.

Hadith: Sahih al-Bukhari 79 (Abu Musa), the parable of rain on three soils, used in §5
and marked explicitly as a general narration NOT attached to 32:27.

Dropped: the Nile "letter of ʿUmar" story in Ibn Kathir (Ibn Lahīʿah, unnamed link,
not sound, sensitive). No sabab established; verse placement used instead.
