Three marks of the believer open an-Naml: the surah has just called the
Qur'an guidance and good news (27:1–2), and 27:3 names who receives it —
establishing prayer, giving zakah, and certainty of the Hereafter.

Sources: Ibn Kathir reads 27:1–3 as one movement and fills in the content
of that certainty (resurrection, recompense, Paradise and Hell; link to
41:44). at-Tabari gives iqama as the prescribed prayer "within its limits"
and makes the structural point explicit — because they are certain of the
return to Allah they humble themselves in obedience, hoping reward and
fearing punishment, unlike the denier who neither hopes nor fears. al-Muyassar
and as-Sa'di: zakah paid to its rightful recipients. as-Sa'di frames the verse
as faith that must show proof, splits iqama into outward acts and inward
khushu', defines yaqin as knowledge that reaches the heart and calls to
action, and calls certainty of the Hereafter "the root of all good" — the
engine the article is built on. al-Qurtubi notes the same portrait opened
al-Baqara.

Disagreement kept: at-Tabari's second reported reading of zakah (وقيل —
purifying the body from the filth of sins) kept as a minority view, not
pressed.

Hadith: Hadith of Jibril (Sahih al-Bukhari 50), sahih by inclusion, marked
as a general narration not attached to the verse by any tafsir — it names
salah and obligatory zakah under Islam and belief in the meeting/resurrection
under iman, matching the verse's three marks. Avoided stock Bukhari 660/3443.

Dropped: Ma'arif al-Qur'an 168 (the grouped file is entirely ash-Shu'ara
commentary, no an-Naml content); al-Baghawi 94 (verse text only, no comment).

Not a condemned-people verse; no licensing sentence. 27:4–5 left for their
own entries.
